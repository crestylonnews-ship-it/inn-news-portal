  # Taiwan Has Over 50,000 Non-Profit Organizations, Social Service Groups Account for 30%
  
  > According to statistics from the Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics of the Executive Yuan, Taiwan has 57,000 social organizations, with social service and public welfare/charitable groups accounting for approximately 30%, the largest share, highlighting the key role non-profit organizations play in Taiwan's social service sector.
  
  The number of non-profit organizations (NPOs) in Taiwan continues to grow. According to year-end 2019 data from the Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics of the Executive Yuan, the total number of social organizations in Taiwan has reached 57,000, with social service and public welfare/charitable groups accounting for approximately 30%, the highest among all categories. This is followed by academic and cultural organizations at 18.8%, economic and business organizations at 12.3%, and sports organizations at 11%, with these four categories combined accounting for over 70%. These figures were compiled and published by the One-Forty public welfare platform, providing an important reference for understanding the development of non-profit organizations in Taiwan.
  
  From a geographic perspective, Taiwan's social welfare organizations are mainly concentrated in the northern and southern regions, with relatively fewer in the central and eastern areas. In terms of service categories, comprehensive services and disability welfare rank in the top two, followed closely by elderly welfare and children and youth welfare. These data reflect the level of investment by Taiwan's non-profit organizations in the social welfare services sector and demonstrate societal attention to the care needs of disadvantaged groups.
  
  Regarding the definition and classification of non-profit organizations, there are slight variations in different perspectives. The DreamRich business platform notes that non-governmental organizations (NGOs) emphasize a role independent of government, typically focusing on international or regional issues; the internationally renowned organization Doctors Without Borders is an example of this type. In contrast, the scope of non-profit organizations (NPOs) is broader, encompassing organizations of various types ranging from social services and cultural promotion to the popularization of educational resources. The Rosy Arts industry information platform offers a similar definition, emphasizing that non-profit organizations operate not for profit but for public interest or social goals, with their presence visible across fields such as arts, charity, education, politics, public policy, religion, academia, and environmental protection.
  
  Notably, the public often confuses non-profit organizations, social enterprises, and charitable institutions. Platforms such as One-Forty and Uptogo point out that although these organizations may appear similar on the surface in their pursuit of "doing good," they have fundamental differences in their operational logic, funding sources, and legal status. Understanding these distinctions is of great significance for the public when choosing donation recipients, participating in volunteer services, and for the government in formulating related policies.
  
  In terms of talent development, the Institute for Public Welfare and Social Service Education Research (IPSS) at the College of Social Sciences, Tunghai University, has observed that non-profit organization workers need professional competence, empathy, design thinking, and the courage to challenge contemporary social structures. The in-service courses offered by the center attract students from Nantou, Taoyuan, Miaoli and other areas, who travel great distances for organizational development, demonstrating the resilience and passion of talent in the non-profit sector.
  
  Experts believe that non-profit organizations play an important role in Taiwan's society by filling gaps in government provision. As social needs become increasingly diverse, the professionalism and innovation capacity of non-profit organizations will become key factors affecting their development. How to enhance organizational transparency, strengthen resource utilization efficiency, and establish more comprehensive supervision mechanisms will be challenges that Taiwan's non-profit organizations need to address for sustained development.

# 臺灣非營利組織逾5萬個 社會服務團體佔三成最多

> 根據行政院主計總處統計，臺灣社會團體達5.7萬個，其中社會服務及公益慈善團體佔比約3成最多，顯示非營利組織在臺灣社會服務領域扮演關鍵角色。

臺灣非營利組織（NPO）數量持續成長，根據行政院主計總處108年底統計資料顯示，臺灣社會團體總數已達5.7萬個，其中社會服務及公益慈善團體佔比約3成，為各類團體之首。其次依序為學術文化團體佔18.8%、經濟業務團體佔12.3%、體育團體佔11%，四者總和佔比超過7成。此資料由One-Forty公益平臺整理發布，提供臺灣非營利組織發展的重要參考依據。
從地理分佈觀察，臺灣社會公益團體的據點主要集中在北部與南部地區，中部與東部則相對較少。以服務類別來看，綜合性服務與身心障礙福利位居前兩名，老人福利、兒童青少年福利緊隨其後。這些資料反映出臺灣非營利組織在社會福利服務領域的投入程度，也顯示出社會對弱勢族羣照顧需求的關注。
關於非營利組織的定義與分類，各界說法略有差異。DreamRich企業經營平臺指出，非政府組織（NGO）強調獨立於政府之外的角色，通常專注於國際或區域性議題，例如國際知名組織「無國界醫生」即屬此類。相較之下，非營利組織（NPO）的範疇更為廣泛，涵蓋社會服務、文化推廣到教育資源普及等不同型別的組織。Rosy Arts產業資訊平臺也提供類似定義，強調非營利組織不以營利為目的，而是為了公共利益或社會目標而運作，涉及的領域從藝術、慈善、教育、政治、公共政策、宗教、學術到環保等議題，皆可見非營利組織的身影。
值得注意的是，大眾對於非營利組織、社會企業與慈善機構的認知往往容易混淆。One-Forty與Uptogo等平臺均指出，雖然這些組織在表面上似乎都是為了「做好事」，但在實際的運作邏輯、資金來源以及法律地位上，它們有著本質上的區別。Understanding這些差異對於民眾選擇捐獻物件、參與志願服務，以及政府制定相關政策，皆具有重要意義。
在人才培育方面，東海大學社會科學院公益慈善與社會服務教育研究中心（IPSS）觀察到，非營利組織工作者需要具備專業能力、同理心、設計思維，以及挑戰當代社會結構的勇氣。該中心提供的在職課程吸引來自南投、桃園、苗慄等地的學員，他們為了組織發展不辭千裡參與學習，展現出非營利領域人才的堅毅態度與熱情。
專家認為，非營利組織在臺灣社會中扮演彌補政府供給落差的重要角色。隨著社會需求日益多元，非營利組織的專業化與創新能力將成為影響其發展的關鍵因素。未來如何提升組織透明度、強化資源運用效率，以及建立更完善的監督機制，將是臺灣非營利組織持續發展需要面對的課題。
