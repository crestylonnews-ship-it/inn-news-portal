  > The World Health Organization (WHO) announced on the 29th that it has rated the global public health risk of the monkeypox outbreak as "moderate," while noting that the outbreak has spread to 16 African countries.   The World Health Organization (WHO) issued its latest statement on Sunday (the 29th) regarding the monkeypox outbreak that has been unfolding since May, formally rating the risk level of this outbreak to global public health as "moderate." In its statement, the WHO called on medical personnel and the public in all countries to closely monitor for signs of rash and to strengthen surveillance and reporting mechanisms. Notably, this is the second time the WHO has issued a warning regarding monkeypox since the 2022 outbreak, indicating that the situation continues to pose a sustained threat.
  
  According to reports from FTV News, the monkeypox outbreak has now spread to 16 African countries, and the WHO has declared it a "Public Health Emergency of International Concern" (PHEIC). This means the outbreak has transcended the scope of a single country and carries the risk of international spread, requiring a coordinated global response. The African region, with relatively weak medical resources and surveillance systems, may see faster disease diffusion, making it a key area for current prevention and control efforts.
  
  Meanwhile, Housefun News cited a warning from WHO Public Health Emergency Programme Executive Director Mike Ryan, noting that outbreaks of endemic diseases such as monkeypox and Lassa fever are becoming more persistent and frequent. Ryan specifically highlighted that Europe may face the risk of a major monkeypox surge this summer, urging European countries to prepare their medical capacity and epidemic prevention strategies in advance. This statement complements the "moderate risk" rating reported by Global Biotech Monthly, indicating that the WHO's assessment of the outbreak considers both global overall risk and regional potential threats.

Experts believe that due to a long-standing lack of sufficient vaccines and treatments in Africa, the disease can spread covertly within communities, thereby increasing the likelihood of cross-border diffusion. While the WHO has rated the global risk as moderate, it emphasized that all countries must remain on high alert, particularly the 16 African nations, which should strengthen vector control, community education, and medical resource scheduling.

# 猴痘疫情升溫 WHO評全球風險中等 非洲16國受影響

> 世界衛生組織（WHO）29日發布宣告，將猴痘疫情全球公共衛生風險評為「中等」，同時指出疫情已蔓延至非洲16國。WHO公共衛生緊急計畫執行主任萊恩警告，猴痘與拉薩熱等地方性流行病爆發正變得更頻繁，歐洲夏季恐迎來大爆發。

世界衛生組織（WHO）於週日（29日）針對5月以來爆發的猴痘（monkeypox）疫情發布最新宣告，正式將此次疫情對全球公共衛生的風險等級評定為「中等」。WHO在宣告中呼籲各國醫療人員與公眾密切注意皮疹跡象，並加強監測與通報機制。值得注意的是，這是繼2022年猴痘疫情後，世衛組織再度針對該病發出警示，顯示疫情形勢仍具持續性威脅。
根據民視新聞網報導，猴痘疫情目前已蔓延至非洲16個國家，WHO宣佈此疫情構成「國際關注的突發公共衛生事件」（PHEIC）。此舉意味著疫情已超越單一國家範圍，具有國際傳播風險，需全球協調應對。非洲地區因醫療資源與監測系統相對薄弱，疫情擴散速度可能較快，成為當前防控重點區域。
另一方面，好房網News引述WHO公共衛生緊急計畫執行主任萊恩（Michael Ryan）的警告指出，猴痘及拉薩熱（Lassa fever）等地方性流行病的爆發，正變得更具持續性及頻繁發生。萊恩特別提到，歐洲地區在夏季可能面臨猴痘疫情大爆發的風險，呼籲歐洲各國提前整備醫療量能與防疫策略。此說法與環球生技月刊所報導的「風險中等」評級形成互補，顯示WHO對疫情的評估兼顧全球整體風險與區域性潛在威脅。
綜合多家媒體報導，此次猴痘疫情的特點在於非洲多國同時受影響，且疫情傳播模式與2022年歐洲爆發時有所不同。專家認為，非洲地區因長期缺乏足夠疫苗與治療藥物，疫情容易在社群中隱蔽傳播，進而增加跨國擴散的機率。WHO雖將全球風險評為中等，但強調各國仍須保持高度警戒，特別是非洲16國應加強病媒控制、社群教育與醫療資源排程。
後續觀察將聚焦於WHO是否會進一步升級風險評級，以及歐洲各國是否已針對夏季潛在爆發做好準備。此外，猴痘與拉薩熱等地方性流行病的頻繁化趨勢，也引發公共衛生專家對全球防疫體系長期韌性的關注。
