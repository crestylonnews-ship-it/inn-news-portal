  # Africa Accelerates Space Research Layout as Transnational Institutions and International Lunar Exploration Plans Advance in Parallel
  
  > The African Union is actively establishing a space agency and expanding international participation, while Africa's first lunar exploration mission is expected to launch alongside a transnational effort, showcasing cross-border scientific integration and aerospace ambitions.
  
  In recent years, African countries have significantly increased their participation in space research and aerospace affairs, actively building a voice on the international aerospace stage through regional transnational integration and cooperation with international organizations. Research published on the academic platform link.springer.com indicates that space activities across Africa have seen substantial growth in recent times. This development trend is first reflected in countries' active participation in international forums related to outer space affairs, particularly the various standing institutions and conferences under the United Nations (UN) concerning outer space activities, demonstrating that African regional nations are gradually placing space governance and space technology on the agenda as important cross-border policy issues.
  
  Regarding transnational organizational mechanisms and institutional development, CUP Media reported that the African Union (African Union) has held the "2023 NewSpace Africa Conference" and is moving forward with plans to establish the "African Space Agency" in Cairo, Egypt. This multilateral cooperation framework aims to integrate the resources and scientific research capabilities of member states through transnational joint programs, enabling Africa to secure a place on the global stage of aerospace and space technology competition.
  
  However, the report also noted the objective realities facing Africa's overall infrastructure, pointing out that among the seven continents worldwide, Africa is currently the only one that has not yet established a domestic rocket launch site. This limitation in hardware conditions further highlights the urgency of transnational cooperation and infrastructure upgrades.
  
  Beyond internal regional organizational preparations and infrastructure discussions, Africa has also begun to venture into the field of deep space exploration. According to a report published on NetEase News (www.163.com), Africa's first lunar exploration project, "Africa2Moon," will participate in a transnational cooperative mission scheduled for launch around 2029. This lunar mission is expected to conduct scientific exploration near the Leibnitz-Beta Plateau in the vicinity of the Moon's south pole, while also laying the groundwork for future international lunar research station construction.
  
  This transnational aerospace cooperation plan not only carries the scientific visions of international partners but also serves as an important practical pathway for Africa to advance substantive deep space exploration and lunar scientific research.
  
  Synthesizing publicly available information and media reports, Africa's aerospace development demonstrates a multi-layered approach: on one hand, at the political and governance level, Africa is strengthening its participation through multilateral international platforms such as the United Nations and planning to establish a unified African Space Agency in Cairo, promoting policy coordination through regional summits; on the other hand, at the cutting-edge scientific research level, Africa is gradually realizing its research vision by joining deep space exploration missions and lunar exploration programs. Although significant breakthroughs are still needed in large-scale surface aerospace infrastructure such as rocket launch sites, African countries continue to accelerate their long-term development in orbital applications and deep space exploration through transnational collaborative mechanisms and international scientific research partnerships.

# 非洲加速推動太空科研佈局 跨國機構與國際探月計畫同步進展

> 非洲聯盟積極設立太空總署並拓展國際參與，非洲首個探月計畫預計隨跨國任務啟動，展現跨國科研整合與航太發展企圖。

非洲各國近年在外太空科研與航太事務的參與度顯著提升，透過區域跨國整合以及與國際組織的合作，積極在國際航太舞臺建立發言權。學術文獻平臺《link.springer.com》的研究指出，非洲全境的太空活動近期呈現相當大幅度的增長，這種發展趨勢首先體現在各國積極參與外太空事務相關的國際論壇，特別是聯合國（UN）轄下與外太空活動有關的各大常設機構與會議，顯示非洲區域國家正逐步將太空治理與太空科技列為重要的跨國政策議題。
在跨國組織機制與機構建設方面，《*CUP媒體》報導指出，非洲聯盟（African Union）已舉辦「2023 新太空非洲大會」（NewSpace Africa Conference），並著手推動在埃及首都開羅設立「非洲太空總署」（African Space Agency）。這項多邊合作架構旨在透過跨國聯合計畫整合各成員國的資源與科研能力，使非洲在全球航太與太空科技競爭的國際舞臺上佔有一席之地。
然而，報導亦提及非洲整體基礎設施面臨的客觀現實，指出綜觀全球七大洲，目前非洲是唯一尚未建立本土火箭發射場的大洲，這項硬體條件的限制也凸顯出跨國合作與基礎建設升級的急迫性。
除了區域內部的組織籌備與基礎設施探討，非洲亦開始涉足深空探測領域。根據《網易新聞（www.163.com）》刊載之報導，非洲首個探月專案「Africa2Moon」將參與預定於 2029 年前後發射的跨國合作任務。該項探月任務預計在月球南極附近的萊布尼茲－貝塔高原（Leibnitz-Beta Plateau）開展科學探索，同時為未來的國際月球科研站建設工作奠定基礎。
這項跨國航太合作計畫不僅承載了國際夥伴的科學構想，也成為非洲推動實質深空探索與月球科研的重要實踐路徑。
綜合各方公開資訊與媒體報導，非洲航太領域的發展呈現出多層次的推動模式：一方面在政治與治理層面，透過聯合國等國際多邊平臺強化參與，並於埃及開羅規劃設立統一的非洲太空總署，藉由區域峰會促進政策協調；另一方面在尖端科研層面，藉由加入深空探測任務與月球探索計畫，逐步實現科研願景。儘管目前在火箭發射場等大型地表航太基礎設施方面仍有待突破，非洲各國正持續透過跨國協同機制與國際科研夥伴關係，加速推進其在軌道應用及深空探索領域的長遠發展。
