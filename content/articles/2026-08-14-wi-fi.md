  # Escalating Cross-Border Cyber Attacks: US Airline Wi-Fi Hacked with Fake Hotspot; Chinese Hackers Suspected of AI-Driven Autonomous Attacks on Taiwan
  
  > A Delta Air Lines flight's Wi-Fi was compromised by a hacker impersonating a hotspot, prompting the captain to send an emergency report. At the same time, Chinese hackers were accused of using AI to autonomously attack Taiwan's government and energy infrastructure, as cross-border cybersecurity threats continue to intensify.
  
  US airline Delta Air Lines Flight 591 recently experienced a malicious interference incident involving its onboard Wi-Fi network. The flight was traveling from Las Vegas to Atlanta when a passenger, who had just concluded their attendance at the globally renowned hacking conference DEF CON 34, was suspected of using network penetration testing tools to disrupt the aircraft's original Wi-Fi signal during the flight. The passenger set up a counterfeit hotspot named "Delta WiFi Fast" (an Evil Twin attack), attempting to lure passengers into connecting. The captain sent an emergency report through the ACARS system, and upon the plane's safe landing, the involved passenger was arrested.
  
  This incident highlights the new threats facing aviation cybersecurity and has sparked concern over protective measures for onboard communication systems.
  
  Meanwhile, Taiwan's cybersecurity landscape has also seen new developments. Israeli AI cybersecurity startup Dream Security revealed this week to the Financial Times that suspected Chinese hackers launched an AI-driven autonomous attack operation targeting Taiwanese government agencies and energy companies. The report indicates that the attackers used artificial intelligence technology to autonomously execute penetration testing and attack steps, signaling that cyber warfare tactics are evolving toward automation and intelligence. If confirmed, this would be the first known case of an AI-driven, state-level cyber attack against Taiwan, raising concerns about the protection of critical infrastructure.
  
  Although the two incidents occurred in different regions, they jointly reflect the嚴峻 (severe) trends in global cybersecurity. The Delta Airlines incident shows that even during flight, passenger devices can serve as entry points for cyber attacks; meanwhile, the case of Taiwan being targeted by AI-driven autonomous attacks reveals that state-level hacker organizations are adopting more advanced technical means. Cybersecurity experts warn that as AI tools become more widespread, the barrier to launching cyber attacks is lowering, and similar incidents may occur more frequently in the future. Relevant authorities are strengthening monitoring and protective measures, but experts emphasize that cross-border cooperation and technological upgrades remain the key to addressing these challenges.

# 跨國網路攻擊升溫：美航機上Wi-Fi遭偽造 中國駭客疑似AI自主攻臺

> 達美航空班機Wi-Fi遭駭客偽造熱點，機長緊急通報；同期中國駭客被指使用AI自主攻擊臺灣政府與能源設施，跨國網路安全威脅持續升溫。

美國達美航空（Delta Air Lines）第591號班機日前發生機上Wi-Fi網路遭惡意幹擾事件。該班機由拉斯維加斯飛往亞特蘭大，一名剛結束全球知名駭客大會DEF CON 34行程的乘客，涉嫌在飛行途中利用網路滲透測試工具幹擾飛機原有Wi-Fi訊號，建立名為「Delta WiFi Fast」的假冒熱點（Evil Twin），試圖誘騙乘客連線。機長透過ACARS系統緊急通報，飛機安全降落後涉案乘客被拘捕。
此事件凸顯航空網路安全面臨的新型威脅，也引發對機上通訊系統防護措施的關注。
與此同時，臺灣資安局勢亦出現新動向。以色列AI資安新創Dream Security本週向金融時報透露，疑似中國駭客發動AI自主攻擊行動，目標鎖定臺灣政府機關與能源公司。該報導指出，攻擊者利用人工智慧技術自主執行滲透測試與攻擊步驟，顯示網路戰術正朝向自動化、智慧化演進。此舉若屬實，將是臺灣面臨的首例AI驅動型國家級網路攻擊案例，引發對關鍵基礎設施防護的擔憂。
兩起事件雖發生在不同地區，卻共同反映全球網路安全形勢的嚴峻趨勢。達美航空事件顯示，即使在空中飛行期間，乘客裝置仍可能成為網路攻擊的入口；而臺灣遭AI自主攻擊的案例，則揭示國家級駭客組織正採用更先進的技術手段。資安專家警告，隨著AI工具普及，網路攻擊門檻降低，未來類似事件可能頻繁發生。相關單位正加強監控與防護措施，但專家強調，跨國合作與技術升級仍是應對之道。
