  # Entrepreneurial Mindset and Policy Focus: A Multi-Faceted Observation of AI Development in Asia
  
  > Y Combinator CEO emphasizes first-principles thinking, Hong Kong Legislative Council highlights AI applications as a priority for industrial diversification, while the impact of AI on working hours sparks discussion.
  
  Garry Tan, CEO of the globally renowned startup accelerator Y Combinator, recently shared insights on entrepreneurial trends in the age of artificial intelligence, advising founders to focus on their unique knowledge and interests rather than blindly chasing hot trends. He reflected on the downturn following the 2003 internet bubble burst, when job opportunities in the San Francisco Bay Area were scarce and the tech industry was filled with pessimism, yet it also gave rise to a new wave of internet 2.0 startups. Tan admitted that he once strayed from his familiar web development field by pursuing the mobile technology boom, using his experience as a reminder for Taiwan's startup community to approach the opportunities and challenges of the AI era with first-principles thinking.
  
  Documents from the Hong Kong Legislative Council's Panel on Commerce, Innovation and Technology indicate that the region's industrial diversification priorities will focus on enterprises in e-commerce, creative industries, green technology, pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, AI applications, and data centers, thereby creating more high-quality employment opportunities. This policy direction resonates with Tan's emphasis on AI entrepreneurial thinking, highlighting the importance that various parts of Asia place on the AI industry.
  
  Meanwhile, media reports indicate that executives at tech companies, which have invested hundreds of billions of dollars in AI tool development over the years, have long insisted that the technology would reduce people's working hours. A Google engineering director stated four years ago that AI could make a four-day workweek a reality by 2025, but this claim has yet to be widely validated, reflecting differing perspectives on AI's impact on the labor market.
  
  A synthesis of various sources reveals that the AI entrepreneurial ecosystem is shifting from a technology-driven phase to one that equally emphasizes mindset and policy. Tan's experience sharing provides psychological-level reference for founders, while Hong Kong's policy planning demonstrates the government's proactive layout for AI applications. The promise of reduced working hours remains to be validated by future developments. Cross-border events and industry dynamics across Asia show that AI development is not only about technological innovation but is also closely linked to employment structures, education models, and public policy—trends that warrant continued attention.

# 創業思維與政策聚焦 亞洲AI發展多面向觀察

> Y Combinator執行長強調第一性原理思維，香港立法會將AI應用列為產業多元化重點，同時AI對工作時間影響引發討論。

全球知名新創加速器Y Combinator執行長陳嘉興（Garry Tan）近日針對人工智慧時代的創業趨勢提出洞察，指出創始人應專注於自身獨特知識與興趣，而非盲目追逐熱潮。他回顧2003年網際網路泡沫破裂後的低谷期，當時舊金山灣區工作機會稀缺，科技業瀰漫悲觀情緒，卻也催生了網際網路2.0創新浪潮；陳嘉興坦承自己曾因追逐行動技術熱潮而偏離熟悉的網頁程式領域，以此提醒臺灣新創圈應以第一性原理思維面對AI時代的機遇與挑戰。
香港立法會工商及創新科技事務委員會檔案顯示，該地區產業多元化重點將聚焦於電子商務、創意產業、綠色科技、製藥、生物技術、人工智慧應用及資料中心等企業，藉此創造更多優質就業機會；此政策方向與陳嘉興所強調的AI創業思維形成呼應，凸顯亞洲各地對人工智慧產業的重視。
另一方面，媒體報導指出，多年來投入數千億美元研發AI工具的科技公司高層曾堅稱該技術將減少人們工作時間，Google一名工程總監更於四年前表示AI有望在2025年前實現每週工作四天願景，但此說法尚未獲普遍驗證，反映AI對勞動市場的影響仍存在不同觀點。
綜合各來源觀察，AI創業生態正從技術驅動轉向思維與政策並重的階段；陳嘉興的經驗分享提供創始人心理學層面的參考，香港的政策規劃則顯示政府層面對AI應用的積極佈局，而工作時間縮減的承諾仍待後續發展驗證。亞洲跨國事件與產業動態顯示，人工智慧領域的發展不僅涉及技術創新，更與就業結構、教育模式及公共政策緊密相連，未來趨勢值得持續關注。
