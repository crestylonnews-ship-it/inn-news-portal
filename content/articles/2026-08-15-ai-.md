  # Africa Declares AI a Strategic Priority, Yet Struggles to Break Free from U.S. Tech Giant Dependence
  
  > The African Union has designated artificial intelligence as a strategic priority, with multiple nations advancing AI sovereignty policies; however, analysts note that Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, and South Africa remain heavily reliant on U.S. tech giants' infrastructure.
  
  The African Union Commission, in collaboration with the Ethiopian government and security institutes, hosted the "High-Level Policy Dialogue on African AI Development and Regulation" in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, drawing participation from over 40 African countries, including heads of state, government leaders, ministerial representatives, and diplomats. The conference formally declared AI a strategic priority for Africa, emphasizing investment, inclusivity, and innovation as core to development.
  
  The International Monetary Fund (IMF) recently released a report stating that Africa could achieve faster economic growth through AI if it accelerates its actions. Citing healthcare and public finance as examples, the report explained that while AI cannot replace Africa's severely insufficient nursing staff and doctors, it can assist with triage, diagnosis, and follow-up care; meanwhile, countries from Kenya to South Africa have already used AI data analytics to strengthen tax compliance and raise funds for development.
  
  However, multiple analyses simultaneously point out that Africa's AI development faces an infrastructure sovereignty dilemma. According to a survey by the nonprofit media outlet Rest of World, although Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, and South Africa are actively promoting AI sovereignty and data control policies—viewing over-reliance on U.S. tech giants as a security risk—their actual infrastructure construction still depends on cloud and chip resources provided by companies such as Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, and Meta.
  
  A Boston Consulting Group (BCG) analysis further指出 that economic fragmentation, brain drain, and dependence on imported technology have long constrained Africa's development. The report proposes three priority actions, calling on African nations to independently develop and cultivate their own digital and AI infrastructure and capabilities.
  
  Al Jazeera, meanwhile, has focused on the resource pressures brought by the rapid expansion of AI infrastructure, noting that the construction of data centers and AI systems across Africa has imposed new burdens on energy and water resources, sparking discussions about development costs and sustainability.
  
  Synthesizing perspectives from all sides, African countries have clearly prioritized AI at the policy level and are actively promoting data sovereignty and local capacity building; yet at the practical infrastructure level, they remain heavily dependent on the technology and platforms provided by U.S. tech giants. How to balance open cooperation with autonomous control will be the key to whether Africa's AI development can truly take root.

# 非洲宣佈AI為戰略優先 卻難脫美科技巨頭依賴

> 非洲聯盟宣佈將人工智慧列為戰略優先，多國推動AI主權政策；但分析指出，奈及利亞、肯亞、埃及與南非仍高度依賴美國科技巨頭基礎設施。

非洲聯盟委員會與衣索比亞政府、安全研究所合作，於衣索比亞亞的斯亞貝巴舉辦「非洲人工智慧發展與監管高層政策對話」，吸引超過40個非洲國家參與，包括國家元首、政府首長、部長級代表與外交官。會議正式宣佈將人工智慧列為非洲戰略優先事項，強調投資、包容性與創新為發展核心。
國際貨幣基金組織（IMF）近期發表報告指出，非洲若能加速行動，可透過AI實現更快經濟增長。報告以醫療與公共財政為例，說明AI雖無法取代非洲嚴重不足的護理人員與醫師，但能協助分流、診斷與後續照護；同時，肯亞至南非等國已運用AI資料分析強化稅務合規，為發展籌措資金。
然而，多項分析同時指出非洲AI發展面臨基礎設施主權困境。根據非營利媒體《Rest of World》調查，奈及利亞、肯亞、埃及與南非雖積極推動AI主權與資料控制政策，將過度依賴美國科技巨頭視為安全風險，但實際建設仍仰賴Google、Microsoft、Nvidia與Meta等企業提供的雲端與晶片資源。
《商業週刊》（BCG）分析進一步指出，經濟碎片化、人才流失與對進口技術的依賴，長期制約非洲發展。報告提出三項優先行動，呼籲非洲國家自主發展、培育並掌握數位與AI基礎設施與能力。
《半島電視臺》（Al Jazeera）則關注AI基礎設施快速擴張帶來的資源壓力，指出資料中心與AI系統在非洲各地擴建，已對能源與水資源造成新負擔，引發關於發展成本與永續性的討論。
綜合各方觀點，非洲各國在政策層面已明確將AI視為戰略優先，並積極推動資料主權與本土能力建構；但在實際基礎設施層面，仍高度依賴美國科技巨頭提供的技術與平臺。如何平衡開放合作與自主可控，將是非洲AI發展能否真正落地關鍵。
