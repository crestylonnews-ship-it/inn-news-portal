  # Middle East Conflict Escalates, Doctors Without Borders Expands Aid, UN Warns of Humanitarian Supply Chain Impact
  
  > The Middle East situation has escalated rapidly, with Doctors Without Borders adjusting its programs to meet humanitarian needs; the UN Refugee Agency warns the crisis is creating ripple effects far beyond the region, impacting global humanitarian supply chains.
  
  The conflict in the Middle East has escalated sharply in recent days, drawing heightened international attention. According to a statement issued by Doctors Without Borders (MSF) Taiwan, the organization expresses deep concern over the rapid escalation of conflict across the entire Middle East region. Following military strikes by the United States and Israeli forces on Iran, Iran has carried out retaliatory actions in multiple countries, causing the situation to deteriorate rapidly. MSF stated that it is closely monitoring developments and adjusting its programs to prepare for rapidly changing humanitarian needs. The escalation of violence has left millions across the region living in fear, with multiple cities severely affected.
  
  Doctors Without Borders (MSF) Hong Kong also issued a similar statement, noting that under the ceasefire agreement in place for the past fifteen months, the people of Lebanon have never truly enjoyed security, and the conflict has now escalated once again. The latest round of attacks, along with evacuation orders issued for all southern suburbs of Beirut and nearly the entire southern region of the country, are forcing more people to flee their homes. The organization emphasized that virtually nowhere is safe at present, underscoring the severity of the situation.
  
  A report issued by the World Health Organization (WHO) indicates that since February 2026, the escalation of conflict in Iran and the broader Middle East region has affected the health of people across the region and beyond. Civilians have been forced to leave their homes, violence has resulted in casualties among civilians and medical personnel, medical facilities have been damaged, and health services have been disrupted. Patients and those with chronic conditions are facing shortages of medical resources.
  
  The UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) issued a warning last Friday, stating that the Middle East crisis has generated far-reaching chain reactions well beyond the region, with increasingly severe negative impacts on global humanitarian supply chains and aid delivery. Speaking at a press briefing in Geneva, spokesperson Carlotta Wolff expressed concern over the security situation along major Gulf shipping routes and surrounding areas, particularly the Strait of Hormuz. If instability in the Middle East continues, rising costs, transport delays, and constrained shipping capacity could further limit humanitarian operations.
  
  Meanwhile, an international opinion piece published in Ta Kung Pao notes that the Middle East security order is undergoing profound changes. On August 7, 2026, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan signed the Mecca Common Defense Agreement in Mecca. Under the agreement, any armed attack against any of the three parties will be regarded as a challenge to the collective security of all three and will trigger a collective defense mechanism. This agreement expands upon the Common Defense Agreement reached between Saudi Arabia and Pakistan in 2025, incorporating Turkey—a NATO member—reflecting a trend among Middle Eastern nations shifting from reliance on the United States toward seeking strategic autonomy.
  
  Synthesizing information from various sources, both Doctors Without Borders and the WHO have emphasized the direct impact of the conflict on civilian health and safety, while the UN Refugee Agency has focused on the indirect effects of the crisis on the global humanitarian aid system. Different media outlets have highlighted different aspects of the situation: MSF has focused on medical aid needs, UNHCR on the risk of supply chain disruptions, and Ta Kung Pao on the evolution of the Middle East security architecture from a geopolitical perspective. The situation remains to be seen, and international humanitarian organizations continue to call on all parties to safeguard civilian safety and the operation of medical facilities.

# 中東衝突升級 無國界醫生擴充援助 聯合國警告人道供應鏈受衝擊

> 中東局勢急劇升溫，無國界醫生調整專案應對人道需求；聯合國難民署警告危機產生遠超地區範圍的連鎖效應，全球人道供應鏈受衝擊。

中東地區衝突近期急劇升溫，引發國際社會高度關注。根據無國界醫生臺灣分會發布的宣告，組織對整個中東地區衝突急劇升溫深感憂慮。美國及以色列軍隊對伊朗發動襲擊後，伊朗又在多個國家採取報復行動，形勢急轉直下。無國界醫生表示，組織正密切關注局勢發展，並調整專案以準備應對急速變化的人道需求。暴力升級已令整個地區數百萬民眾生活在恐懼之中，多個城市受到嚴重影響。
無國界醫生香港分會也發布類似宣告，指出在過去十五個月的停火協議之下，黎巴嫩民眾從未真正得到安全保障，如今衝突再度升級。最新一輪襲擊，以及對貝魯特所有南部郊區及該國幾乎整個南部地區發出的撤離令，正迫使更多人逃離家園。該組織強調，目前幾乎沒有任何一處是安全的，突顯局勢的嚴峻性。
世衛組織（WHO）發布的報告指出，自二零二六年二月以來，伊朗及中東地區的衝突升級已對該地區及更廣泛範圍的人們健康產生影響。民眾被迫離開家園，暴力導致平民及醫療人員傷亡，醫療設施受損，健康服務受到幹擾。傷患及慢性病患者面臨醫療資源短缺的困境。
聯合國難民署（UNHCR）上週五發出警告，指中東危機已產生遠超地區範圍的深遠連鎖效應，對全球人道主義供應鏈及援助交付的負面影響日益嚴重。發言人卡洛塔·沃爾夫在日內瓦舉行的新聞釋出會上表示，海灣主要航線及周邊地區，特別是霍爾木茲海峽的安全形勢令人擔憂。如中東局勢持續動盪，成本上升、運輸延誤及運力受限，可能會進一步限制人道主義行動。
另一方面，大公報刊登的國際觀點文章指出，中東安全秩序正在發生深刻變化。二零二六年八月七日，土耳其、沙烏地阿拉伯與巴基斯坦在麥加簽署《麥加共同防禦協議》。根據協議安排，針對三方中任何一方的武裝攻擊都將被視為對三方共同安全的挑戰，並觸發集體防禦機制。這一協議是在二零二五年沙特與巴基斯坦達成的《共同防禦協議》基礎上擴充套件而來，將土耳其這一具有北約成員國身份的國家納入其中，反映中東國家從依賴美國到尋求戰略自主的趨勢。
綜合各來源資訊，無國界醫生及世衛組織均強調衝突對平民健康與安全的直接衝擊，聯合國難民署則關注危機對全球人道援助體系的間接影響。不同媒體對局勢的描述側重點各異：無國界醫生聚焦醫療援助需求，聯合國難民署關注供應鏈中斷風險，而大公報則從地緣政治角度分析中東安全架構的演變。局勢發展仍待觀察，國際人道組織持續呼籲各方維護平民安全與醫療設施運作。
