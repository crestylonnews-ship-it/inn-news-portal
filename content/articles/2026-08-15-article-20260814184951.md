  # Tariff War Reshapes Global Trade Landscape; US-China Rivalry Impacts Americas Economy
  
  > A French scholar notes that US tariff hikes are prompting countries to reroute trade, though Mexico and Canada face challenges due to their deep economic integration with the US; Morris Chang warns the US-China trade war harms the global economy; the USTR's annual report continues tracking trade barriers worldwide.
  
  César Hidalgo, professor at the Toulouse School of Economics in France, has analyzed that when the US imposes tariffs globally, other economies are not retaliating with reciprocal tariffs, and most countries naturally tend to adjust their trade routes to bypass the United States. This observation reveals a profound shift currently underway in the global trade landscape—the US is attempting to reshape trade relations through tariff tools, but this may backfire, prompting other economies to build trade networks that circumvent the US. Hidalgo further explained that Mexico and Canada are exceptions; these two countries are highly integrated with the US economy and find it difficult to redirect their trade routes like other nations can.
  
  This means that under the framework of the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA), the economic relations among the three countries exhibit different degrees of resilience and vulnerability in the face of tariff war pressures.
  
  Morris Chang, founder of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC), has offered his perspective on the US-China trade war, clearly stating that it is detrimental to the global economy. Chang's view echoes the concerns of the international economics community regarding the rise of trade protectionism. As the world's two largest economies, the US and China's trade friction not only affects bilateral economic and trade exchanges but also reverberates globally through supply chain chain reactions. For the Americas, the trade disputes between the US and China directly impact trans-Pacific trade routes, while Mexico and Canada, as the US's most important trading partners, have their economic trajectories closely tied to the US and find it harder to flexibly adjust their trade directions compared to other economies in Europe or Asia.
  
  The Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) releases an annual comprehensive National Trade Estimate report, documenting the various barriers US companies face in global markets, including high tariffs, forced local production requirements, and related technology and regulatory restrictions. This report reflects the US government's ongoing attention to the global trade environment and demonstrates that US companies continue to encounter multiple non-tariff barriers in overseas markets.
  
  From the perspective of the Americas, US companies also face challenges such as local tariff structures, import quotas, and regulatory environments when expanding into Latin American and Caribbean markets. These factors form a complex interactive relationship with the US policy of imposing tariffs globally.
  
  Drawing on multiple sources, the current global trade system is at a critical juncture of structural reorganization. The US is attempting to reduce trade deficits and protect domestic industries through tariff tools, but economists point out that this approach may accelerate other economies' efforts to establish alternative trade networks. Professor Hidalgo's analysis is particularly noteworthy, as he highlights the indirect effects of tariff wars—when the US raises import barriers, other countries may instead deepen their economic and trade cooperation, forming new trade architectures that exclude the US.
  
  For the Americas, this trend brings both challenges and opportunities: Latin American countries may seize the moment to strengthen regional integration, while Mexico and Canada must find strategies to cope with trade uncertainty while maintaining close economic ties with the US.
  
  Morris Chang's warnings about the US-China trade war further underscore the global economy's dependence on trade stability. Supply chains in high-tech industries such as semiconductors are highly globalized, and any trade friction between major economies will produce widespread ripple effects. The USTR's annual report provides another observational indicator; by systematically tracking trade barriers that countries impose on US companies, one can continuously monitor the evolving trends of the global trade environment. In the future, economic and trade development in the Americas will be deeply influenced by the interplay of multiple factors, including the direction of US trade policy, the evolution of US-China relations, and progress on regional trade agreements.

# 關稅戰重塑全球貿易版圖 美中角力牽動美洲經濟

> 法國學者指出美國加徵關稅促使各國繞道貿易，墨西哥與加拿大因經濟高度整合難跟進；張忠謀警告美中貿易戰不利全球經濟；美國貿易代表辦公室年度報告持續追蹤各國貿易障礙。

法國土魯斯經濟學院教授伊達爾戈（César Hidalgo）分析指出，當美國對全球課徵關稅，其他經濟體並未相互加稅，多數國家會很自然地傾向調整貿易路線，繞開美國。這項觀察揭示了當前全球貿易格局正在發生的深刻轉變——美國試圖透過關稅工具重塑貿易關係，卻可能適得其反，促使其他經濟體建立繞過美國的貿易網路。伊達爾戈進一步說明，墨西哥與加拿大是例外情況，這兩國與美國經濟高度整合，難以像其他國家一樣重新調整貿易路線。
這意味著北美自由貿易協定（USMCA）框架下的三國經濟關係，在關稅戰衝擊下呈現出不同的韌性與脆弱性。
臺灣積體電路製造公司創辦人張忠謀針對美中貿易戰發表看法，明確表示美中貿易戰不利全球經濟。張忠謀的觀點呼應了國際經濟學界對貿易保護主義升溫的擔憂。美中作為全球前兩大經濟體，其貿易摩擦不僅影響雙邊經貿往來，更透過供應鏈連鎖反應波及全球。對於美洲地區而言，美國與中國的貿易糾葛直接衝擊跨太平洋貿易路線，而墨西哥與加拿大作為美國最重要的貿易夥伴，其經濟走向與美國高度繫結，難以像歐洲或亞洲其他經濟體那樣靈活調整貿易方向。
美國貿易代表辦公室（USTR）每年都會釋出一份詳盡的《國家貿易估算報告》（National Trade Estimate），記錄美國企業在全球市場面臨的各種障礙，包括高關稅、強制本地生產，以及相關技術與監管限制。這份報告反映了美國官方對全球貿易環境的持續關注，也顯示美國企業在海外市場仍面臨多重非關稅壁壘。
從美洲地區的角度檢視，美國企業在拉丁美洲與加勒比海市場拓展時，同樣面臨當地關稅結構、進口配額與監管環境等挑戰，這些因素與美國對全球課徵關稅的政策形成複雜的互動關係。
綜合多方來源觀察，當前全球貿易體系正面臨結構性重組的關口。美國透過關稅工具試圖減少貿易逆差並保護本土產業，但經濟學家指出，這種做法可能促使其他經濟體加速建立替代性貿易網路。伊達爾戈教授的分析特別值得關注，他點出關稅戰的間接效應——當美國提高進口門檻，其他國家之間反而可能深化經貿合作，形成排除美國的新貿易架構。
對於美洲地區而言，這種趨勢既帶來挑戰也蘊含機遇：拉丁美洲國家可能趁勢加強區域整合，而墨西哥與加拿大則需在維持與美國緊密經濟聯絡的同時，尋找應對貿易不確定性的策略。
張忠謀對美中貿易戰的警告，進一步凸顯了全球經濟對貿易穩定性的依賴。半導體等高科技產業的供應鏈高度全球化，任何主要經濟體之間的貿易摩擦都會產生廣泛的連鎖效應。美國貿易代表辦公室的年度報告則提供了另一項觀察指標，透過系統性追蹤各國對美國企業的貿易障礙，可以持續掌握全球貿易環境的演變趨勢。未來美洲地區的經貿發展，將深受美國貿易政策走向、美中關係演變，以及區域貿易協定進展等多重因素交織影響。
