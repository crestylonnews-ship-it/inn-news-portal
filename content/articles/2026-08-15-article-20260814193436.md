  # Africa Launches New Health Security Agenda to Tackle Multiple Infectious Disease Threats
  
  > The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) has released a new public health agenda, emphasizing lessons learned from past outbreaks and strengthening health systems to address multiple infectious disease threats including COVID-19, Ebola, Mpox, and cholera.
  
  The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) recently released a report titled "The New Public Health Order: Africa's Health Security Agenda," which notes that the African continent demonstrated decisive leadership during the COVID-19 pandemic and underscores the importance of strengthening health systems through lessons learned from past outbreaks. The report explicitly states that African countries have taken swift action to limit the virus's impact and are using this opportunity to build more robust public health emergency response mechanisms.
  
  According to data from the Africa CDC and the World Health Organization (WHO) through the end of 2023, pandemic hotspots in Africa are concentrated in parts of East, West, and Southern Africa. In recent years, the COVID-19 pandemic, Ebola virus, Mpox, and seasonal cholera have repeatedly emerged across multiple regions of Africa, creating a complex landscape of simultaneous outbreaks. The Africa CDC emphasizes that this new agenda aims to integrate past response experiences and establish a more resilient health security framework.
  
  Dr. Janabi, head of the WHO Regional Office for Africa, stated that his vision focuses on achieving better health outcomes for all Africans through innovation, equity, and collaboration. Official information from WHO headquarters in Geneva also indicates that integrating resources, driving innovation, and harnessing community leadership are key pathways to ending HIV, viral hepatitis, and sexually transmitted infections. This aligns with the regional cooperation direction advocated by the Africa CDC.
  
  Analysts point out that Africa's challenges in recent years from overlapping multiple outbreaks have exposed gaps in health systems regarding resource allocation and response capacity. The introduction of the new public health agenda symbolizes African countries' strategic shift from reactive response to proactive prevention. However, experts also caution that ensuring equitable resource distribution to the most vulnerable communities remains a key challenge in implementing the agenda. Future observations will focus on whether national policy implementation and regional cooperation mechanisms can function effectively.

# 非洲啟動衛生安全新議程 應對多重傳染病威脅

> 非洲疾病控制與預防中心發布新公共衛生議程，強調從疫情中汲取經驗，強化衛生系統以應對COVID-19、埃博拉、猴痘與霍亂等多重傳染病威脅。

非洲疾病控制與預防中心（Africa CDC）近日發布〈新公共衛生秩序：非洲衛生安全議程〉報告，指出非洲大陸在應對COVID-19疫情期間展現果斷領導力，並強調透過過往疫情經驗強化衛生系統的重要性。該報告明確指出，非洲各國已迅速採取行動限制病毒衝擊，並藉此契機建立更完善的公共衛生應變機制。
根據非洲疾病控制與預防中心與世界衛生組織（WHO）截至2023年底的資料顯示，非洲疫情熱點主要集中在東非、西非與南部非洲的部分國家。近年來，COVID-19大流行、埃博拉病毒、猴痘（Mpox）以及季節性霍亂等傳染病在非洲多地反覆出現，形成多點爆發的複雜局面。非洲疾病控制與預防中心強調，此一新議程旨在整合過往應對經驗，建立更堅實的衛生安全架構。
世界衛生組織非洲區域辦事處負責人Dr. Janabi表示，其願景聚焦於透過創新、公平與合作，為所有非洲民眾實現更佳的健康成果。WHO總部位於日內瓦的官方資訊亦指出，整合資源、推動創新與發揮社群領導力，是終結艾滋病毒、病毒性肝炎與性傳播感染的關鍵途徑。這與非洲疾病控制與預防中心所倡導的區域合作方向不謀而合。
分析人士指出，非洲在過去數年面對多重疫情交織的挑戰，暴露出衛生系統在資源分配與應變能力上的落差。新公共衛生議程的提出，象徵非洲各國試圖從被動應對轉向主動預防的戰略調整。然而，專家也提醒，如何確保資源公平分配至最脆弱的社群，仍是落實議程的關鍵挑戰。後續觀察將聚焦於各國政策執行情形與區域合作機制是否能有效運作。
