  # EU Advances Space Act to Strengthen European Space Autonomy and Industrial Competitiveness
  
  > The European Commission proposed the Space Act in June 2025, aiming to unify Europe's fragmented space regulatory standards; meanwhile, the European Space Agency continues to advance its scientific programs, and Taiwan is actively participating in the European Space Technology Exhibition to deepen cooperation.
  
  On June 25, 2025, the European Commission proposed the EU Space Act, a move regarded as an important step for the EU to strengthen its strategic autonomy in space. According to a report by the Taiwan Information Office for Science and Technology Industry (iKnow), the act aims to unify the fragmented space industry regulatory standards across European countries to address the increasingly congested and competitive space environment and enhance Europe's competitiveness in the global space market. The report notes that Europe's space regulations are currently fragmented, restricting cross-border business development and increasing compliance costs for enterprises, while the new act hopes to improve this situation through unified regulations.
  
  The Council of the European Union's website further explains that the EU's space policy not only focuses on industrial development but also emphasizes the contribution of space activities to daily life. According to the Council, the EU is investing in space activities to help Europeans address today's global challenges, while space policy also plays a key role in supporting the dual transition toward a green and digital economy and promoting European strategic autonomy. The Council also pointed out that space investment helps create jobs, drive growth and investment, and advance the frontiers of scientific research.
  
  Regarding scientific research, Wikipedia data shows that the European Space Agency's (ESA) science program is a long-term space science and exploration mission plan managed by the agency's Science Programme Committee, which funds missions developed, launched, and operated by European space agencies and other organizations through the formulation of decadal space programs. This science program covers a wide range of space exploration fields and serves as an important foundation for European space activities.
  
  Deutsche Welle's (DW) report analyzes the shift in European space policy from a geopolitical perspective. The report points out that over the past few decades, Europe has been highly dependent on the United States for space security, but the Trump administration's "America First" policy and the rapid advancement of space commercialization have prompted Europe to seek greater space autonomy. DW believes that Europe's desire to have a place in the space race reflects a deeper European demand for strategic autonomy.
  
  Taiwan's space cooperation with Europe continues to deepen. According to a report by the National Science and Technology Council of Taiwan, the European Space Agency and Taiwan have already achieved cooperation in multiple applications such as satellite imaging. As a cross-governmental organization of the EU, the ESA's missions and activities cover multiple aspects including space exploration, scientific research, technological development and application, and international cooperation.
  
  In addition, the Industrial Technology Research Institute (ITRI) of Taiwan is actively participating in international space activities and has planned a Taiwan space industry delegation for the 2026 European Space Technology Exhibition, expected to conduct matchmaking and exchanges with relevant entities in the UK, Northern Europe, and Germany during the exhibition, and to visit key European space companies such as ArianeGroup, Isar Aerospace, and The Exploration Company.
  
  Synthesizing information from various sources, the EU's promotion of the Space Act and its efforts to strengthen space autonomy reflect Europe's strategic intent to seek greater voice in the global space competition. However, the specific content, timeline for passage, and actual impact of the Space Act remain to be observed and confirmed. Taiwan's continued deepening of space technology cooperation with Europe through participation in events such as the European Space Technology Exhibition also demonstrates that the space sector has become an important dimension of Taiwan's international scientific and technological cooperation.

# 歐盟推動太空法案 強化歐洲太空自主與產業競爭力

> 歐盟執委會於2025年6月提出《太空法案》，旨在統一歐洲分散的太空監管標準；同時歐洲太空總署持續推動科學計劃，臺灣亦積極參與歐洲太空技術展深化合作。

歐盟執委會於2025年6月25日提出《歐盟太空法案》（EU Space Act），此舉被視為歐盟強化太空戰略自主的重要一步。根據臺灣科技產業資訊室（iKnow）報導，該法案旨在統一歐洲各國分散的太空產業監管標準，以因應日益擁擠且競爭激烈的太空環境，並提升歐洲在全球太空市場的競爭力。報導指出，目前歐洲太空法規各自為政，限制了跨國業務發展，增加企業合規成本，而新法案期望透過統一規範來改善此一狀況。
歐盟理事會網站進一步說明，歐盟的太空政策不僅關注產業發展，更強調太空活動對日常生活的貢獻。根據歐盟理事會的說法，歐盟正投資太空活動以協助歐洲人應對當今的全球挑戰，同時太空政策也扮演關鍵角色，支援綠色與數位經濟的雙重轉型，並促進歐洲戰略自主。歐盟理事會同時指出，太空投資有助於創造就業、帶動成長與投資，並推動科學研究邊界的拓展。
在科學研究方面，維基百科資料顯示，歐洲太空總署（ESA）的科學計劃是一項長期的空間科學與太空探索任務規劃，由該機構科學理事會管理，透過制定各十年期航天計劃，資助歐洲航天機構和其他組織所開發、發射和運營的任務。這項科學計劃涵蓋廣泛的太空探索領域，是歐洲太空活動的重要基礎。
德國之聲（DW）的報導則從地緣政治角度分析歐洲太空政策的轉變。報導指出，過去幾十年來，歐洲在太空安全方面高度依賴美國，但川普政府的「美國優先」政策以及航天商業化的快速推進，促使歐洲尋求提升自身的太空自主能力。DW認為，歐洲希望在太空競賽中擁有一席之地，這反映了歐洲對戰略自主的更深層訴求。
臺灣與歐洲的太空合作亦持續深化。根據臺灣國家科學及技術委員會的報導，歐洲太空總署與臺灣雙方在衛星拍攝等多方應用上已有合作成就。歐洲太空總署作為歐盟的跨政府組織，其任務與活動涵蓋太空探索、科學研究、科技發展與應用，以及國際合作等多個面向。
此外，臺灣工業技術研究院亦積極參與國際太空活動，規劃2026年歐洲太空技術展的臺灣太空產業交流團，預計於展會期間與英國、北歐及德國等地區相關單位進行媒合交流，並拜訪ArianeGroup、Isar Aerospace、The Exploration Company等歐洲關鍵太空企業。
綜合各來源資訊，歐盟推動《太空法案》與強化太空自主能力，反映歐洲在全球太空競爭中尋求更大話語權的戰略意圖。然而，《太空法案》的具體內容、透過時程與實際影響，仍有待後續觀察與確認。臺灣透過參與歐洲太空技術展等活動，持續深化與歐洲的太空科技合作，亦顯示太空領域已成為臺灣國際科技合作的重要面向。
