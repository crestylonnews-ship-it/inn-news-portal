  # Middle East Energy Transition and Crisis Coexist as Global Supply Chains Face New Challenges
  
  > Escalating Middle East geopolitical conflicts expose the fragility of global fossil fuel dependence, while the region simultaneously advances energy transition and energy storage deployment, drawing attention to national policies and market trends.
  
  A recent report by the United Nations highlights that the ongoing geopolitical crisis in the Middle East is exposing a core vulnerability of the global economy—its heavy reliance on fossil fuel supply chains that pass through conflict zones. The report emphasizes that as the world's primary oil and natural gas producing region, the Middle East's stability directly affects global energy security; any supply disruption would trigger cascading impacts on the international economy. This analysis situates Middle East energy issues within the framework of global climate and energy governance, underscoring the complex interplay between energy transition and geopolitical risk.
  
  According to a report by Grand View Research, the Middle East has long dominated the global oil market but is undergoing a profound transformation. Driven by economic diversification and environmental sustainability goals, governments, national oil companies (NOCs), and private enterprises in the region are entering a new era—one where fossil fuels and renewable energy coexist, and energy security and decarbonization targets are pursued simultaneously. The report notes that countries such as Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates have actively invested in solar and wind power and are promoting the green hydrogen industry, attempting to build a more resilient energy structure while maintaining their traditional energy advantages.
  
  In terms of energy storage technology, the Global Information Intelligence (GII) report "2025 Middle East and Africa Energy Storage Outlook" analyzes how the energy storage market in the Middle East and Africa (MEA) region is being shaped by multiple driving factors, barriers, and policies. The report conducts scenario analyses for key markets including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Israel, and South Africa, and covers energy storage trends in other MEA countries. Energy storage technology is regarded as a critical infrastructure supporting the Middle East's energy transition, helping to balance the intermittent power supply of renewable energy while enhancing grid stability.
  
  The International Energy Agency's (IEA) flagship annual report, the "World Energy Outlook 2025" (WEO), provides authoritative energy analysis and forecasting from a global perspective. Updated annually, the report incorporates the latest energy data, technologies, market trends, and government policies to examine the impacts of different energy scenarios on energy security, energy access, and emissions across the entire energy system. The WEO 2025 analytical framework covers multiple geographic regions including the Middle East and South Asia, and East Asia and Oceania, offering a macro-level perspective for understanding energy shifts in the Middle East.
  
  Tensions in the Middle East have directly affected energy policies in neighboring countries. According to China Times News, the Ministry of Economic Affairs stated that following the escalation of geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East, the government has immediately activated an energy emergency response team and prepared three major plans to stabilize energy supply, with measures covering energy scheduling and emergency preparedness for March and April. This move reflects the tangible pressure that Middle East conflicts are placing on economies with high dependence on imported energy, and further highlights the close link between regional energy security and international developments.
  
  A synthesis of various sources reveals that the Middle East energy landscape is at a critical juncture marked by both transition and risk. On one hand, ongoing geopolitical conflicts continue to expose the world's vulnerability to fossil fuel supply chain dependence; on the other, regional nations are actively promoting energy diversification and energy storage development in an effort to reduce risks and seize new opportunities in green energy. The trajectory of Middle East energy policy in the future will simultaneously affect progress toward global climate goals and the regional energy security landscape.

# 中東能源轉型與危機並存 全球供應鏈面臨新挑戰

> 中東地緣衝突加劇，暴露全球化石燃料依賴脆弱性；同時該區正推動能源轉型與儲能佈局，各國政策與市場動向引發關注。

聯合國近日發布報告指出，中東持續的地緣危機正凸顯全球經濟的核心弱點——對經過衝突地區的化石燃料供應鏈的高度依賴。報告強調，中東作為全球石油與天然氣的主要產區，其穩定性直接牽動世界能源安全，一旦供應中斷，將對國際經濟造成連鎖衝擊。此分析將中東能源議題置於全球氣候與能源治理的框架下，凸顯能源轉型與地緣政治風險交織的複雜局面。
根據 Grand View Research 發布的報告，中東長期主導全球油市，但正經歷深刻轉型。在經濟多元化和環境永續目標的驅動下，該區政府、國家石油公司（NOCs）及民間企業正共同邁入一個新時代——化石燃料與可再生能源並存，能源安全與減碳目標同步追求。報告指出，沙烏地阿拉伯、阿拉伯聯合大公國等國已積極投資太陽能與風電，並推動綠色氫能產業，試圖在維持傳統能源優勢的同時，建立更具韌性的能源結構。
在儲能技術方面，日商環球訊息有限公司（GII）發布的《2025 年中東與非洲儲能展望》報告分析，中東與非洲（MEA）地區的儲能市場正受到關鍵驅動因素、障礙與政策的多重影響。報告針對沙烏地阿拉伯、阿聯酋、以色列與南非等重點市場進行情境分析，並涵蓋 MEA 地區其他國家的儲能趨勢。儲能技術被視為支撐中東能源轉型的重要基礎設施，有助於平衡可再生能源的間歇性供電，同時提升電網穩定性。
國際能源署（IEA）年度旗艦報告《2025 年世界能源展望》（WEO）則從全球角度提供權威的能源分析與預測。該報告每年更新，納入最新能源資料、技術與市場趨勢，以及各國政府政策，探討不同能源前景對能源安全、能源可及性與排放的影響，涵蓋整個能源系統。WEO 2025 的分析框架涵蓋中東與南亞、東亞與大洋洲等多個地理區域，為理解中東能源變遷提供宏觀視角。
中東局勢的緊張已直接影響周邊國家的能源政策。中時新聞網報導，經濟部表示，中東地區地緣衝突加劇後，政府已即刻啟動能源應變小組，並備妥三大方案以穩固能源供應，相關措施涵蓋 3、4 月份的能源排程與應急準備。此舉反映中東衝突對進口能源依賴度較高的經濟體已產生實質壓力，也凸顯區域能源安全與國際局勢的緊密連結。
綜合各來源觀察，中東能源版圖正處於轉型與風險並存的關鍵階段。一方面，地緣衝突持續暴露全球對化石燃料供應鏈的依賴弱點；另一方面，該區國家積極推動能源多元化與儲能建設，試圖降低風險並掌握綠色能源新契機。未來中東能源政策的走向，將同時影響全球氣候目標的達成進度與區域能源安全格局。
