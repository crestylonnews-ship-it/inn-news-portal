  # Asian Public Health Challenges: International Scholars Call for Governance Reform
  
  > The WHO emphasizes integration and community leadership to end infectious diseases, but PLOS scholars question whether reform will maintain the status quo; Asian journals focus on regional cooperation, with historical analysis pointing out that sovereignty concepts constrain cross-border disease prevention.
  
  The World Health Organization (WHO) recently released a report stating that integrating healthcare services, promoting innovative strategies, and strengthening community leadership are key pathways to ending HIV, viral hepatitis, and sexually transmitted infections. The organization emphasized that these infectious diseases continue to pose a significant public health burden globally, and that cross-sectoral collaboration and grassroots participation are essential to improving prevention and control outcomes.
  
  However, the same issue has sparked different interpretations in academia. An opinion piece published in PLOS Global Public Health by Indira Dewi Kantiana and colleagues pointed out that the WHO-led global health reform process may maintain the status quo due to influence from those being reformed, and that accountability mechanisms and independent oversight remain weak, leaving the effectiveness of reforms open to question.
  
  Public health issues in Asia continue to attract international attention. The table of contents of Volume 38, Issue 5 of the Asia Pacific Journal of Public Health shows that the journal continues to publish regional research covering disease surveillance, health policy, and community health, reflecting the commitment of countries in East Asia, Southeast Asia, and South Asia to public health issues. Meanwhile, the journal Global Public Health focuses on health access, global health policy, and inequality, providing a platform for cross-national comparative research.
  
  An article titled "Challenges Facing Global Public Health" published on the website of China's Center for Disease Control and Prevention pointed out that global public health is currently undergoing unprecedented and complex changes. Addressing these challenges requires formulating effective policies, strengthening international cooperation, promoting health education, and enhancing public health literacy to build a more resilient global health system.
  
  A historical perspective also provides important reference. An article from the Chinese University of Hong Kong, "From Post-War East Asia," analyzes how modern international health and disease prevention have partial origins in fourteenth-century European quarantine history, which laid the foundation for public health and influenced international health cooperation from the nineteenth century onward. However, since the establishment of the Westphalian sovereignty concept in the mid-seventeenth century, the cross-border disease prevention ideal of "diseases know no borders" has still been constrained by national sovereignty. The establishment of international health institutions by various countries in the early twentieth century showed that cooperative mechanisms were gradually developing, yet tensions between sovereignty and health governance have persisted.
  
  Drawing on all sources, both the WHO and academic journals agree that public health requires strengthened international cooperation and policy innovation, but hold different views on the direction and effectiveness of reforms. Asian countries face a double burden of infectious and non-communicable diseases, and the strengthening of regional health networks remains to be implemented. Whether future global health governance can break through sovereignty frameworks and establish more effective accountability mechanisms will affect public health progress across multiple countries.

# 亞洲公共衛生挑戰 國際學者呼籲改革治理架構

> 世衛組織強調整合與社群領導力以終結傳染病，但 PLOS 學者質疑改革維持現狀；亞洲期刊聚焦區域合作，歷史分析指出主權觀念限制跨國防疫。

世衛組織（WHO）近期發布報告指出，整合醫療服務、推動創新策略與強化社群領導力，是終結艾滋病毒、病毒性肝炎與性傳播感染的關鍵途徑。該組織強調，此類傳染病在全球仍構成重大公共衛生負擔，尤其需透過跨部門合作與基層參與，提升防治成效。
然而，同一議題在學術界引發不同解讀；PLOS Global Public Health 期刊刊載觀點文章，由 Indira Dewi Kantiana 等人指出，WHO 主導的全球衛生改革程式，可能因受改革物件影響而維持現狀，且問責機制與獨立監督仍顯薄弱，改革成效有待觀察。
亞洲地區公共衛生議題持續受到國際關注。《Asia Pacific Journal of Public Health》第 38 卷第 5 期目錄顯示，該期刊持續刊載區域性研究，涵蓋疾病監測、衛生政策與社群健康等主題，反映東亞、東南亞與南亞各國對公共衛生議題的投入。同時，《Global Public Health》期刊亦聚焦健康取得、全球衛生政策與不平等問題，提供跨國比較研究平臺。
中國疾控中心網站發表文章〈全球公共衛生面臨的挑戰〉，指出當前全球公共衛生正經歷前所未有的複雜變遷，應對挑戰需制定有效政策、加強國際合作、促進健康教育並提升公眾健康素養，以建構更堅韌的全球衛生體系。
歷史視角亦提供重要參考。香港中文大學文章〈從戰後東亞經〉分析，現代國際衛生與防疫作為部分源自十四世紀歐洲檢疫歷史，奠定公共衛生基礎並影響十九世紀以降的國際衛生合作；但自十七世紀中葉威斯特伐利亞主權觀念確立後，「疾病無國界」的跨國防疫理想仍受國家主許可權制。二十世紀初各國設立國際衛生機構，顯示合作機制逐步發展，然主權與衛生治理間的張力持續存在。
綜合各來源，世衛組織與學術期刊均認同公共衛生需加強國際合作與政策創新，但對改革方向與成效存在不同看法。亞洲各國面臨傳染病與非傳染病雙重負擔，區域衛生網路的強化仍有待落實。未來全球衛生治理能否突破主權框架、建立更有效問責機制，將影響多國公共衛生進展。
