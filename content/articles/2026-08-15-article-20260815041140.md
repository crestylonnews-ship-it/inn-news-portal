  # Africa's Climate Change Threat Intensifies, Renewable Energy Transition Pace Varies
  
  > Africa is considered the world's most vulnerable continent to climate change, with approximately 600 million of its nearly 1.5 billion population lacking access to electricity. North Africa has committed to global climate governance, while West Africa's renewable energy capacity growth remains sluggish, leaving overall transition potential largely untapped.
  
  Africa's climate change is becoming an increasingly severe global threat, with the continent classified by multiple sources as "the most vulnerable on Earth" due to its extreme sensitivity to climate impacts. Africa's population approaches 1.5 billion, of whom approximately 600 million still lack access to electricity, yet the continent simultaneously possesses abundant renewable energy and mineral resources, providing uniquely favorable natural conditions for achieving universal electricity access. The United Nations Environment Programme notes that cooling demand has become one of the primary drivers of electricity growth, and since the "Global Cooling Pledge" was launched at COP28 in 2023, countries have been actively encouraging strategies such as passive cooling, improved energy efficiency, and innovation in cooling technologies through sustainable cooling actions.
  
  As member states of the African Union, North African countries have explicitly emphasized their commitment to participating in global climate governance within the framework of "Agenda 2063: The Africa We Want," and have stressed ensuring that all people have access to modern, efficient, reliable, affordable, renewable, and environmentally friendly energy. The International Renewable Energy Agency's (IRENA) report "Renewable Energy Development and Deployment in North Africa: Policies and Finance" shows that the North Africa region is committed to promoting renewable energy development, but policy implementation and financing mechanisms still face challenges. The region's renewable energy development must balance energy accessibility with low-carbon transition goals to address increasingly frequent extreme climate events.
  
  Energy transition progress in West Africa shows significant disparities. Since 2019, renewable energy installed capacity has increased in countries such as Senegal, Mali, Togo, and Burkina Faso, primarily driven by solar energy, but the overall growth rate remains slow and heavily reliant on international development fund financing. There are clear gaps in energy structures and clean energy development across Africa's various regions, and the pace of transition in East Africa, Central Africa, and Southern Africa also varies. Experts point out that Africa's energy transition potential remains to be further unlocked, requiring diverse exploration of clean energy utilization methods to lay a solid foundation for national energy accessibility and low-carbon transition.

# 非洲氣候變遷威脅加劇 可再生能源轉型步伐參差

> 非洲被視為全球氣候變遷最脆弱大陸，人口近15億中約6億缺乏電力。北非承諾參與全球氣候治理，西非可再生能源裝機容量增長緩慢，整體轉型潛力待釋放。

非洲氣候變化正成為日益嚴重的全球性威脅，該大陸被多個訊息來源歸類為「地球上最脆弱的大陸」，因其對氣候變化的影響極度敏感。非洲大陸人口近15億，其中約6億人仍無法獲得電力供應，然而這片大陸同時擁有豐富的可再生能源與礦產資源，為實現電力全覆蓋提供了得天獨厚的自然條件。聯合國環境規劃署指出，冷卻需求已成為驅動電力成長的主要因素之一，2023年COP28推動「全球冷卻承諾」以來，各國正積極透過永續冷卻行動，鼓勵採行被動式冷卻、提高能源效率及冷卻裝置創新技術等策略。
北非國家作為非洲聯盟成員國，在《Agenda2063：The Africa We Want》框架中明確強調了參與全球氣候治理的承諾，並指出要確保所有人獲得現代、高效、可靠、經濟實惠、可再生且環境友好的能源。國際可再生能源機構（IRENA）發布的《北非可再生能源開發部署：政策與融資》報告顯示，北非地區正致力於推動可再生能源開發，但政策落實與融資機制仍面臨挑戰。該地區的可再生能源發展需兼顧能源可及性與低碳轉型目標，以應對日益頻繁的極端氣候事件。
西非地區的能源轉型進展呈現顯著差異。自2019年以來，塞內加爾、馬裡、多哥和布吉納法索等國家的可再生能源裝機容量有所增加，尤其以太陽能為主，但整體增速較慢，且主要依賴國際開發基金的資助。非洲各個區域的能源結構與清潔能源發展存在明顯落差，東非、中非與南部非洲的轉型步伐亦不盡相同。專家指出，非洲能源轉型潛力有待進一步釋放，需透過多元探索清潔能源利用方式，為本國能源可及性與低碳轉型奠定堅實基礎。
