  # Iran War Shakes Asia Energy Security as Countries Struggle Between Climate Goals and Energy Transition
  
  > Geopolitical conflicts and extreme climate are intertwining, creating policy divisions among Asian countries on their energy transition paths. IMF and World Bank reports indicate that energy transition carries economic benefits, but a Commercial Times report shows some countries are reverting to coal reliance due to energy security concerns, affecting climate target achievement.
  
  The International Monetary Fund (IMF) released a research report stating that energy transition in the Asia-Pacific region will have profound impacts on each country's economy, emphasizing high uncertainty in future fossil fuel demand. The report's baseline scenario shows coal demand is expected to shrink by 15% by 2035, but if global policy ambition and technology adoption are higher, the decline could reach 45%. The study highlights that Asia, as the world's largest market for oil, natural gas, and coal, will profoundly influence global decarbonization efforts through its energy transition path. Meanwhile, the World Bank also released a report emphasizing that East Asia possesses substantial untapped renewable resources, and accelerating clean energy transition can not only enhance competitiveness and create大量 employment opportunities, but also strengthen energy security.
  
  The report points out that East Asia's industrial rise over the past several decades relied primarily on coal to achieve rapid growth, but this also made the region a major source of global greenhouse gas emissions. Now, the economic situation and pressing priorities have shifted.
  
  However, geopolitical changes are posing challenges to Asia's energy transition. The Commercial Times reported that the Iran war has prompted Asian countries to seek diversified energy solutions, but this may affect the achievement of climate goals. FutureCoal, an fossil fuel industry organization formerly known as the World Coal Association, stated that this second energy shock within less than a decade is consolidating coal's importance to Asia's energy security. Data from the Global Energy Monitor shows that while global coal-fired power generation decreased by 0.6% last year, new coal-fired power generation capacity increased by 3.5%, reflecting that some Asian countries, driven by energy security considerations, still tend to expand coal-fired power facilities.
  
  Energy policy divergence has also become a key obstacle to regional transition. EY's 2025 World Energy Outlook analysis pointed out that 2024 was the hottest year on record and the first year in which global average temperature exceeded 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels. Despite mounting warming pressures, countries are showing increasingly pronounced divergences in energy transition strategies and pathways, leading to increased corporate de-risking demands.
  
  The International Energy Agency's "2025 Global Energy Review" also noted that the interplay of extreme climate, moderating high inflation, and geopolitical uncertainty has created multiple challenges and transition pressures for energy markets; although energy-related carbon emissions have reached new highs, progress in renewable energy and clean technology development remains evident.
  
  A comprehensive review of various sources reveals that Asia's energy transition faces a complex situation where economic opportunities and practical challenges coexist. Both the IMF and World Bank reports affirm the economic benefits of clean energy transition, but analyses from the Commercial Times and EY indicate that geopolitical risks and energy security considerations may slow the coal phase-out process. Whether Asian countries can strike a balance between climate goals and energy security in the future will depend on the progress of policy coordination, technology transfer, and international cooperation.

# 伊朗戰爭衝擊亞洲能源安全 各國在氣候目標與能源轉型間拉扯

> 地緣政治衝突與極端氣候交織，亞洲各國在能源轉型路上面臨政策分歧。IMF與世行報告指出能源轉型具經濟效益，但工商時報報導顯示部分國家因能源安全考量重新依賴煤炭，影響氣候目標達成。

國際貨幣基金組織（IMF）發布研究報告指出，亞太地區能源轉型將對各國經濟產生深遠影響，並強調未來化石燃料需求存在高度不確定性。報告基線情境顯示，至2035年煤炭需求預期將萎縮15%，但若全球政策抱負與技術採用程度更高，降幅可能達45%。該研究凸顯亞洲作為全球最大石油、天然氣與煤炭市場，其能源轉型路徑將深刻影響全球減碳程式。同時，世界銀行亦發布報告強調，東亞擁有大量待開發的可再生資源，加速清潔能源轉型不僅能提升競爭力、創造大量就業機會，更能增強能源安全。
報告指出，東亞過去數十年的工業崛起主要依賴煤炭，實現快速增長，但也使該地區成為全球溫室氣體排放的主要來源，如今經濟形勢與當務之急已發生轉變。
然而，地緣政治變局正對亞洲能源轉型帶來挑戰。工商時報報導指出，伊朗戰爭促使亞洲國家尋求多元能源方案，卻可能影響氣候目標的實現。前身為世界煤炭協會的化石燃料產業組織FutureCoal表示，這場不到十年內發生的第二次能源衝擊，正在鞏固煤炭對亞洲能源安全的重要性。全球能源監測組織資料顯示，去年全球燃煤發電量下降0.6%，但新增燃煤發電裝置容量卻增加3.5%，反映部分亞洲國家在能源安全考量下，仍傾向擴建燃煤發電設施。
能源政策分歧亦成為區域轉型關鍵障礙。安永發布的2025年世界能源展望分析指出，2024年是有紀錄以來最熱的一年，也是全球平均氣溫首次高於工業化前平均值達攝氏1.5°C的一年。儘管升溫壓力持續加劇，各國在能源轉型策略與路徑上卻出現日益明顯的分歧，企業去風險化需求隨之增加。
國際能源總署發布的《2025全球能源回顧》亦指出，極端氣候、高通膨趨緩與地地緣政治不確定性交織，使能源市場面臨多重挑戰與轉型壓力；雖然能源相關碳排放再創新高，但再生能源與潔淨技術的發展仍具進展。
綜合各來源觀察，亞洲能源轉型正面臨經濟機遇與現實挑戰並存的複雜局面。IMF與世行報告均肯定清潔能源轉型的經濟效益，但工商時報與安永分析顯示，地緣政治風險與能源安全考量可能延緩減煤程式。未來亞洲各國能否在氣候目標與能源安全間取得平衡，將取決於政策協調、技術轉移與國際合作進展。
