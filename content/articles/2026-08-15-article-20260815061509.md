  # Climate Vulnerability in the Americas Highlighted; UN Warns of Earth Tipping Point Risks
  
  > The World Meteorological Organization notes that the Americas region is highly susceptible to extreme weather, while a UN briefing warns that breaching Earth system tipping points will trigger irreversible changes, calling for strengthened multi-hazard early warning systems.
  
  The fourth region of the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) covers North America, Central America, and the Caribbean, administered by the WMO Regional Office for the Americas (RAM), serving 27 member states and providing professional support services through the North America, Central America and Caribbean Representative Office (NCAC). The organization states that due to the complex geographical environment of the region, it is highly vulnerable to extreme weather events such as tropical cyclones, droughts, floods, storm surges, and tornadoes, with climate change further intensifying the frequency and severity of these disasters.
  
  The Science Advisory Committee to the UN Secretary-General issued a briefing on June 23, 2026, raising alarms about Earth system tipping points. The briefing indicates that once these tipping points are breached, a cascade of irreversible changes may be triggered, profoundly impacting human society and Earth's ecosystems. This warning aligns with the WMO's assessment of climate risks in the Americas, underscoring the urgency of global climate action.
  
  According to WMO analysis, the climate challenges facing the Americas region exhibit strong regional characteristics. Tropical cyclones frequently strike Caribbean nations and the southeastern coastal United States, while drought conditions severely affect agricultural production and drinking water security in the "Dry Corridor" of Central America. Floods and storm surges threaten low-lying coastal cities, and the tornado belt across the central United States causes significant property damage and casualties each year. Climate change is expected to drive continued increases in the intensity and frequency of the aforementioned extreme weather events, further exacerbating regional vulnerability.
  
  In response to this situation, WMO emphasizes that establishing multi-hazard early warning systems and robust response mechanisms is crucial. Multi-hazard early warning systems can simultaneously monitor and issue alerts for multiple types of meteorological and climate disasters, enhancing countries' capacity to respond to extreme weather events. The UN briefing also calls on governments to accelerate emission reduction targets to avoid triggering irreversible changes to Earth systems. Experts note that countries in the Americas need to strengthen cross-border cooperation, sharing meteorological data and emergency resources to address increasingly severe climate challenges.

# 美洲氣候脆弱性凸顯 聯合國警告地球臨界點風險

> 世界氣象組織指出美洲地區極易受極端天氣影響，聯合國簡報警告突破地球系統臨界點將引發不可逆轉變化，呼籲強化多災種預警系統。

世界氣象組織（WMO）第四區域涵蓋北美洲、中美洲與加勒比海地區，由WMO美洲區域辦公室（RAM）管轄，服務範圍包含27個會員國，並透過北美、中美洲與加勒比代表處（NCAC）提供專業支援服務。該組織指出，由於該區域地理環境複雜，極易受到熱帶氣旋、乾旱、洪水、海岸湧浪與龍捲風等極端天氣事件影響，而氣候變遷更進一步加劇這些災害的發生頻率與強度。
聯合國秘書長科學諮詢委員會於2026年6月23日發布簡報，針對地球系統臨界點（tipping points）提出警示。簡報內容指出，一旦突破地球系統臨界點，可能引發一連串不可逆轉的變化，對人類社會與地球生態系統造成深遠影響。此警示與WMO對美洲地區氣候風險的評估相互呼應，凸顯全球氣候行動的迫切性。
根據WMO的分析，美洲地區面臨的氣候挑戰具有高度區域特性。熱帶氣旋頻繁侵襲加勒比海國家與美國東南部沿海，乾旱問題則嚴重影響中美洲「乾旱走廊」地區的農業生產與居民用水安全。洪水與海岸湧浪對低窪沿海城市構成威脅，而龍捲風帶橫跨美國中部地區，每年造成重大財產損失與人員傷亡。氣候變遷被認為會使上述極端天氣事件的強度與頻率持續上升，進一步加劇區域脆弱性。
針對此一形勢，WMO強調建立多災種預警系統與強有力的應變機制至關重要。多災種預警系統能夠同時監測與預警多種型別的氣象與氣候災害，提升各國應對極端天氣事件的能力。聯合國簡報亦呼籲各國政府加速推進減排目標，避免觸發不可逆轉的地球系統變化。專家指出，美洲國家需加強跨國合作，共享氣象資料與應急資源，以應對日益嚴峻的氣候挑戰。
