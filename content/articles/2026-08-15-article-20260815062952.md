  # Iran Closes Strait of Hormuz, Demands US Accept Conditions Before Reopening
  
  > Iran's newly appointed Supreme National Security Council secretary Rezaei announced on the 13th that the US must halt war and blockade operations, lift economic sanctions, release frozen assets, and support a Middle East ceasefire, or the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed. The UN has warned that rising oil prices are already impacting humanitarian conditions across Asia and Oceania.
  
  Mohsen Rezaei, newly appointed secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, publicly stated on August 13, 2026, that the United States must change its current policies and accept multiple conditions proposed by Iran before Iran will reopen the Strait of Hormuz. According to UK-based shipping websites, Rezaei clearly outlined four demands: the US must halt war and blockade operations, lift economic restrictions on Iran, release frozen Iranian assets, and support a ceasefire agreement covering the entire Middle East region, including Lebanon and Gaza. Additionally, Iran has communicated other related demands to Washington through mediators participating in indirect negotiations.
  
  Rezaei emphasized that the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen as long as the US does not change its behavior or accept Iran's conditions. The announcement is seen as indicating that the timing for reopening one of the world's most critical energy waterways remains highly uncertain.
  
  The Strait of Hormuz is a vital chokepoint for global energy transport, carrying approximately 20% of the world's oil supply, most of which is destined for Asia. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) released a report stating that the escalation of tensions in the Middle East by late February 2026 has produced cross-border spillover effects, intensifying humanitarian crises across Asia and Oceania. The report specifically identifies rising oil prices as the primary impact, directly caused by reduced traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. This region, heavily dependent on Middle Eastern energy imports, is experiencing significant economic and livelihood pressures from oil price volatility, and the UN continues to monitor the situation.
  
  The UN Department of Political and Peacebuilding Affairs (DPPA), which manages global special political missions and supports conflict prevention, peacemaking, political dialogue, and peacebuilding, noted that tensions in the Middle East could affect stability across a broader region. The UN is coordinating international responses to potential risks through existing mechanisms. Iran's announcement and the UN's warnings demonstrate that closing energy waterways has evolved from a geopolitical issue into a transnational humanitarian and economic challenge. The US has not yet issued a public response to Iran's conditions, and the international community is closely watching for developments in negotiations and energy market movements.

# 伊朗關閉荷姆茲海峽 要求美接受條件才重啟

> 伊朗最高國家安全委員會新任秘書雷札伊13日宣告，美國須停止戰爭與封鎖、解除經濟限制、釋放凍結資產並支援中東停火，否則荷姆茲海峽將持續關閉。聯合國警告油價上漲已衝擊亞洲與大洋洲人道局勢。

伊朗最高國家安全委員會新任秘書雷札伊（Mohsen Rezaei）於2026年8月13日公開表示，美國必須改變現行政策並接受伊朗提出的多項條件，伊朗才會重新開放荷姆茲海峽。根據英國航運相關網站引述，雷札伊明確列出四項要求：美國須停止戰爭與封鎖行動、解除對伊朗的經濟限制、釋放遭凍結的伊朗資產，並支援涵蓋整個中東地區的停火協議，其中包括黎巴嫩與加薩地區。此外，伊朗也已透過參與間接談判的調解人，向華府傳達其他相關要求。
雷札伊強調，只要美國不改變其行為，也不接受伊朗提出的條件，荷姆茲海峽就不會重新開放。外界認為，這項宣告意味全球最重要能源航道之一的重新開放時機仍充滿不確定性。
荷姆茲海峽是全球能源運輸的關鍵咽喉，承載約20%的世界石油供應，其中大部分運往亞洲。聯合國人道事務協調廳（OCHA）發布報告指出，2026年2月底中東局勢升級已產生跨國溢位效應，加劇亞洲與大洋洲的人道危機。報告明確提到，主要衝擊來自油價上漲，而油價上漲的直接原因正是荷姆茲海峽交通量減少。此區域依賴中東能源進口，油價波動已對民生與經濟造成實質壓力，聯合國正持續監測後續發展。
聯合國政治和建設平事務部（DPPA）負責管理全球特別政治任務，支援衝突預防、促和、政治對話與建設平工作。該部門指出，中東地區的緊張局勢可能影響更廣泛區域的穩定，聯合國正透過現有機制協調國際社會應對潛在風險。伊朗的宣告與聯合國的警告顯示，能源航道封閉不僅是地緣政治問題，更已轉化為跨國人道與經濟挑戰。目前美國官方尚未對伊朗條件作出公開回應，國際社會正關注後續談判進展與能源市場動向。
