  # EU Releases Energy Union Status Report: Green Transition Faces Multiple Challenges
  
  > The European Commission released the Energy Union Status Report in September 2024, showing significant carbon reduction achievements while facing multiple challenges including geopolitics, AI-driven electricity demand, and industrial competitiveness, as energy transition policy enters a critical adjustment period.
  
  On September 11, 2024, the European Commission released the "State of the Energy Union Report 2024," providing a comprehensive review of the EU's recent energy policy progress and future challenges. According to analysis by the Science and Technology Information Institute (STLI), the EU's greenhouse gas emissions decreased by 32.5% between 1990 and 2022, and the development of renewable energy has been recognized. However, the report also points out that the EU still faces significant difficulties in industrial cultivation, and the path to green transition has not been entirely smooth.
  
  CNA reports that Europe is currently facing multiple variables including geopolitical shifts, rising electricity consumption driven by artificial intelligence (AI), and climate targets, with countries rethinking their energy layouts. This "energy transformation," which is crucial to European industrial development and economic security, is profoundly influencing the direction of EU energy policy. TechNews further analyzes that the European Commission has recently released a series of energy policy adjustments, with the core goal of completely stopping Russian natural gas imports by the end of 2027 and accelerating energy independence through the "REPowerEU" plan.
  
  In response to supply chain instability caused by geopolitical factors, the EU is shifting from its past strategy of relying on natural gas as a "transition fuel" to a more aggressive electrification path, with specific measures including promoting a cross-border grid blueprint to address green electricity grid integration issues.
  
  HK01 examines Europe's energy transition dilemma from a different perspective, pointing out that while Europe has indeed been at the forefront of the world in market construction, it faces significant difficulties in industrial cultivation. The report cites the views of Peng Wensheng, Chief Economist at CICC, noting that over the past 20 years, there have been clear differences in the policy levers and pathways for green transition among major economies, implying that the European model may not be applicable to other regions and reflecting differing views within Europe on the pace and approach of transition.
  
  EY (Ernst & Young), a member enterprise of the OECD, emphasizes in its analysis report that the clean energy transition is key to achieving net-zero emissions, and that European energy policy has far-reaching implications for national security and market competitiveness. In the September 2024 Energy Union Status Report, the EU not only outlined progress made so far but also clearly identified future challenges that still need to be addressed, including energy price volatility, infrastructure investment needs, and ensuring that industries do not relocate overseas due to excessively high transition costs.
  
  A synthesis of media reports shows that the EU's energy transition is at a critical stage of policy adjustment. On one hand, carbon reduction and renewable energy development have achieved concrete results; on the other hand, geopolitical risks, surging electricity demand from the rise of the AI industry, and concerns about industrial competitiveness are all prompting the EU to re-examine its energy policy direction. The European Commission has clearly set the goal of stopping Russian natural gas imports by 2027 and is promoting a more aggressive electrification path, but how to balance environmental goals with economic realities remains a focal point of continued attention from governments and industries across member states.

# 歐盟發布能源聯盟現況報告 綠色轉型面臨多重挑戰

> 歐盟執委會於2024年9月發布能源聯盟現況報告，顯示減碳成果顯著，但同時面臨地緣政治、AI耗電與產業競爭力等多重挑戰，能源轉型政策正進入關鍵調整期。

歐盟執委會於2024年9月11日發布「2024年能源聯盟現狀報告」（State of the Energy Union Report 2024），全面檢視歐盟近年能源政策進展與未來挑戰。根據工業技術研究院（STLI）分析該報告內容，歐盟溫室氣體排放量自1990年至2022年間已減少32.5%，再生能源發展成果獲得肯定，但同時指出歐盟在產業培育方面仍面臨巨大困難，綠色轉型之路尚未完全順遂。
中央社報導指出，當前歐洲正面臨地緣政治情勢改變、人工智慧（AI）帶動耗電量攀升，以及氣候目標等多重變數，各國正重新謀略能源佈局。這項攸關歐洲工業發展與經濟安全的「能源變局」，正深刻影響歐盟能源政策的走向。TechNews進一步分析，歐盟執委會近期發布一系列能源政策調整，核心目標設定於2027年底前全面停止進口俄羅斯天然氣，並透過「REPowerEU」計畫加速能源自主。
面對地緣政治引發的供應鏈不穩定，歐盟正從過去依賴天然氣作為「過渡燃料」的策略，轉向更激進的電氣化路徑，具體措施包括推動跨境電網藍圖以解決綠電併網問題。
香港01報導則從不同角度檢視歐洲能源轉型困境，指出歐洲在市場建設方面確實走在全世界前列，但在產業培育方面卻面臨巨大困難。該報導引用中金公司首席經濟學家彭文生的觀點指出，過去20年間，主要經濟體綠色轉型的政策抓手和政策路徑存在明顯差異，暗示歐洲模式未必適用於其他地區，也反映歐洲內部對轉型速度與方式存在不同看法。
經濟合作與發展組織（OECD）成員企業安永（EY）在分析報告中強調，清潔能源轉型是實現淨零排放的關鍵，而歐洲能源政策對國家安全及市場競爭力具有深遠影響。歐盟在2024年9月的能源聯盟現況報告中，除了概述至今進展，也明確點出未來仍需面對的挑戰，包括能源價格波動、基礎設施投資需求，以及確保產業不致因轉型成本過高而流失海外。
綜合各媒體報導可見，歐盟能源轉型正處於政策調整的關鍵階段。一方面，減碳與再生能源發展已有具體成果；另一方面，地緣政治風險、AI產業崛起帶來的電力需求激增，以及產業競爭力擔憂，均促使歐盟重新檢視能源政策方向。歐盟執委會已明確設定2027年停止進口俄羅斯天然氣的目標，並推動更激進的電氣化路徑，但如何平衡環保目標與經濟現實，仍是各國政府與產業界持續關注的焦點。
