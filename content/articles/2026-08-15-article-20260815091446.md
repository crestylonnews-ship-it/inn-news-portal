  # Africa's Energy Transition Presents Opportunities as International Community Focuses on Climate Finance and Cooperation
  
  > Africa faces the dual challenges of climate change and energy shortages. The IMF notes that the five Sahel countries need to increase spending by 8% of GDP to address climate impacts. The G7, G20, and China-Africa cooperation are successively promoting renewable energy development, while emerging fields such as hydrogen are expanding rapidly.
  
  Global climate change is profoundly affecting the energy development path of the African continent. According to an analysis by the Regional, Country, and Global Governance Database, energy transition is the only way for Africa to achieve sustainable development. At the same time, climate change has exposed structural weaknesses in Africa's energy system, including water scarcity and overreliance on fossil fuels, making it imperative to accelerate the development of clean and renewable energy. The study also outlines the characteristics and trends of global and African climate change and thoroughly examines the specific impacts of climate change on Africa's energy transition.
  
  The International Monetary Fund (IMF) recently released a report focusing on Africa's climate finance gap. The report points out that Burkina Faso, Chad, Mali, Mauritania, and Niger — five countries in the West African Sahel region — are among the poorest in the world. To address the impacts of climate change, these five countries need to increase spending by an average of approximately 8% of their gross domestic product (GDP). In addition to the resources needed for post-pandemic recovery, the African continent requires extra climate finance support on top of its existing development financing needs. The IMF emphasizes that figuring out how to pay for Africa's climate change response has become a key issue urgently requiring resolution by the international community.
  
  At the international cooperation level, the Group of Seven (G7) and the Group of Twenty (G20) have both been looked to with high expectations. According to a recommendations report published by the International Renewable Energy Agency (IRENA), the G7 can support African countries in developing impactful renewable energy projects by facilitating existing project development facilities. It should also increase public funding flows for Africa's energy transition through multiple channels and use strategic and policy tools to advance the energy transition process. Meanwhile, an article published by Project Syndicate notes that the recent 29th UN Climate Change Conference (COP29) held in Baku and the G20 summit held in Rio de Janeiro have become important turning points, particularly significant for developing countries.
  
  The African Union's participation in the Rio discussions as the newest full member of the G20 has drawn considerable attention, with South Africa currently holding the group's rotating presidency. The article argues that the G20 should play a greater role in Africa's energy agenda.
  
  China-Africa cooperation has also become an important force driving Africa's energy transition. The Paper reported that Fang Li, Chief Representative of the World Resources Institute (WRI) Beijing Office, specifically mentioned the "Africa Light Belt" project supported by WRI. As an important component of the practical cooperation project list of the Third Belt and Road Forum for International Cooperation, the project is also a key measure to implement the China-Africa Cooperation Forum's "China-Africa Cooperation Declaration on Climate Change," aiming to help African countries bridge their energy gaps.
  
  Emerging energy sectors are also demonstrating development momentum. Tencent News reported that at least 12 African countries are currently carrying out hydrogen energy projects, while another 19 countries have established dedicated regulatory frameworks or national strategies for this emerging industry, indicating a clear trend of African nations developing a green hydrogen economy. Statistical data shows that the green energy wave is driving Africa to explore a path of sustainable development.
  
  Synthesizing various perspectives, Africa's energy transition faces both the severe challenges brought by climate change and the historical opportunity of accelerated international cooperation. Ensuring that climate finance is effectively delivered, coordinating multilateral cooperation mechanisms, and balancing energy accessibility with environmental sustainability will be the core issues that Africa's future energy development must address.

# 非洲能源轉型迎機遇 國際社會聚焦氣候融資與合作

> 非洲面臨氣候變遷與能源短缺雙重挑戰，IMF指出薩赫勒五國需增支佔GDP 8%應對氣候影響；G7、G20及中非合作相繼推動可再生能源發展，氫能等新興領域亦快速擴張。

全球氣候變遷正深刻影響非洲大陸的能源發展路徑。根據區域國別與全球治理資料庫分析指出，能源轉型是非洲實現可持續發展的必由之路，而氣候變化同時暴露了非洲能源體系的結構性弱點，包括水資源匱乏與對化石能源過度依賴等問題，亟須加快清潔能源與可再生能源的發展步伐。該研究同時闡述全球及非洲氣候變化的特徵與趨勢，並深入探討氣候變化對非洲能源轉型產生的具體影響。
國際貨幣基金組織（IMF）近期發布報告關注非洲氣候融資缺口問題。報告指出，位於西非薩赫勒地區的布吉納法索、查德、馬裡、毛裡塔尼亞和尼日等五個國家，是全球最貧困的國家群體，為應對氣候變化影響，這五國平均需增加的支出約佔其國內生產總值（GDP）的8%。除新冠疫情危機恢復所需的各項資源外，非洲大陸在現有發展融資需求基礎上，仍需額外氣候融資支援。IMF強調，如何為非洲的氣候變化「買單」，已成為國際社會亟待解決的關鍵議題。
在國際合作層面，七國集團（G7）與二十國集團（G20）相繼被寄予厚望。根據國際可再生能源署（IRENA）發布的建議報告，G7可透過促進現有專案開發設施，支援非洲國家開發具影響力的可再生能源專案；同時應透過多種渠道增加非洲能源轉型的公共資金流，並運用戰略與政策工具推動能源轉型程式。另一方面，Project Syndicate刊載文章指出，近期於巴庫舉行的第二十九屆聯合國氣候變化大會及於裡約熱內盧舉行的G20峰會成為重要分水嶺，尤其對發展中國家而言意義深遠。
非洲聯盟作為G20最新正式成員參與裡約各項討論備受矚目，而南非現正擔任該集團輪值主席國。文章認為，G20應在非洲能源議程中發揮更大作用。
中非合作亦成為推動非洲能源轉型的重要力量。澎湃新聞報導，世界資源研究所（WRI）北京代表處首席代表方莉特別提及WRI參與支援的「非洲光帶」專案。該專案作為第三屆「一帶一路」國際合作高峰論壇務實合作專案清單的重要內容，也是落實中非合作論壇《中非應對氣候變化合作宣言》的關鍵舉措，旨在協助非洲國家彌合能源缺口。
新興能源領域同樣展現發展動能。騰訊新聞報導指出，目前至少有12個非洲國家正在開展氫能專案，另有19個國家針對這一新興行業制定了專門的監管框架或國家戰略，非洲國家發展綠氫經濟的趨勢明顯。統計資料顯示，綠色能源浪潮正推動非洲探索可持續發展之路。
綜合各方觀點，非洲能源轉型既面臨氣候變遷帶來的嚴峻挑戰，也迎來國際合作加速推進的歷史機遇。如何確保氣候融資有效到位、如何協調多邊合作機制、如何平衡能源可及性與環境可持續性，將是未來非洲能源發展必須回應的核心課題。
