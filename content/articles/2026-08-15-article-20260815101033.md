  # EU Passes Carbon Border Tax; Energy-Intensive Industries Seek Electricity Price Protection for Competitiveness
  
  > The European Parliament has approved the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) rules, while energy-intensive industries are simultaneously urging the EU to prioritize competitive electricity prices and investment support in the upcoming Electrification Action Plan, warning that persistently high energy costs could undermine industrial competitiveness and decarbonization efforts.
  
  The European Parliament recently approved the new EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) rules with 487 votes in favor, 81 against, and 75 abstentions. The mechanism is regarded as a major milestone in EU climate policy, aimed at encouraging non-EU countries to raise their climate ambitions while ensuring that the EU's and the world's emission reduction efforts are not undermined by production shifting to countries with looser climate policies. This decision by the European Parliament marks the EU's attempt to find a more precise balance between addressing climate change and maintaining industrial competitiveness.
  
  At the same time, European energy-intensive industries are actively calling on the European Commission to prioritize competitive electricity prices and investment support in the upcoming EU Electrification Action Plan (EAP). These industries warn that if energy costs remain persistently high, it will directly threaten the international competitiveness of European industry and could hinder overall decarbonization efforts.
  
  The concerns of energy-intensive industries are not unfounded. The current energy price pressures in Europe could indeed impose a double burden on companies during their transition: on one hand, they must invest heavily in upgrading facilities and transforming technologies, and on the other, they must contend with high electricity costs eroding profit margins.
  
  The impacts of climate change are gradually becoming apparent, reducing the livability of numerous cities and reshaping global settlement patterns. Euronews notes that this trend is particularly urgent for Europe, as the EU is both an advocate of climate policy and at the forefront of industrial transformation. The European Parliament's approval of the CBAM rules demonstrates the EU's desire to internalize carbon costs through policy tools and avoid "carbon leakage"—the phenomenon where companies shift production to regions with looser carbon emission regulations.
  
  However, industry players are concerned that if EU internal electricity prices are not effectively reduced, companies could face a dilemma of "trouble at home and pressure from abroad": externally, they must contend with the trade adjustment pressures brought by CBAM, while internally, they must bear the erosion of competitiveness caused by high energy costs.
  
  Reactions from various quarters reveal that supporters of CBAM argue the mechanism can prompt major global economies to accelerate their emission reduction efforts while protecting EU companies that have already invested heavily in green transformation. Opponents, however, point out that CBAM could be seen as a form of green trade protectionism, imposing an unfair burden on developing countries. Meanwhile, the demands of energy-intensive industries have sparked discussion: on one hand, companies need affordable clean energy to achieve decarbonization goals; on the other hand, excessive electricity price subsidies could weaken market incentives to improve energy efficiency. How the European Commission will balance these complex interests when formulating the Electrification Action Plan remains closely watched.
  
  Subsequent observations indicate that as the EU advances its climate policies, it must simultaneously balance industrial competitiveness and its global leadership role. The implementation details of CBAM, the specific content of the Electrification Action Plan, and the results of negotiations between the EU and its major trading partners will all affect the practical effectiveness of these policies. Dialogue between industry and policymakers is expected to continue, ensuring that the EU's green transition does not stall due to energy cost issues.

# 歐盟透過碳邊境稅 高耗能業盼電價護競爭力

> 歐洲議會透過碳邊界調整機制（CBAM）規則，高耗能產業同時呼籲歐盟在即將推出的電氣化行動計畫中，優先保障具競爭力的電力價格與投資支援，擔憂能源成本持續居高不下將衝擊產業競爭力與去碳化程式。

歐洲議會近日以487票贊成、81票反對、75票棄權的表決結果，正式透過新的歐盟碳邊界調整機制（Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism，CBAM）規則。這項機制被視為歐盟氣候政策的重要里程碑，旨在激勵非歐盟國家提高氣候雄心，同時確保歐盟與全球的減排努力不會因為生產活動轉移至氣候政策較鬆散的國家而遭到破壞。歐洲議會的這項決定，標誌著歐盟在應對氣候變遷與維護產業競爭力之間，試圖尋找更精確的平衡點。
與此同時，歐洲高耗能產業正積極向歐盟執委會發聲，要求在即將推出的「歐盟電氣化行動計畫」（EU Electrification Action Plan，EAP）中，優先考量具競爭力的電力價格與投資支援。這些產業警告，若能源成本持續居高不下，將直接威脅到歐洲產業的國際競爭力，並可能阻礙整體去碳化程式。
高耗能產業的擔憂並非空穴來風，當前歐洲面臨的能源價格壓力，確實可能讓企業在轉型過程中承受雙重負擔：一方面要投入大量資金進行裝置升級與技術轉型，另一方面又要面對高昂的電力成本侵蝕利潤空間。
氣候變遷的影響已逐漸顯現，不僅降低了多座城市的宜居性，也正在重塑全球居住格局。Euronews指出，這一趨勢對歐洲而言尤為迫切，因為歐盟既是氣候政策的倡導者，也是產業轉型的前沿陣線。歐洲議會透過CBAM規則，顯示出歐盟希望透過政策工具將碳成本內化，避免「碳洩漏」現象——即企業將生產轉移至碳排放規範較寬鬆的地區。
然而，產業界擔心的是，若歐盟內部電價未能有效壓低，企業可能面臨「內憂外患」的困境：對外要面對CBAM帶來的貿易調整壓力，對內則要承受高能源成本對競爭力的侵蝕。
觀察各界反應，支援CBAM的觀點認為，這項機制能促使全球主要經濟體加快減排步伐，同時保護歐盟內部已投入大量資源進行綠色轉型的企業。但反對聲音則指出，CBAM可能被視為一種綠色貿易保護主義，對發展中國家造成不公平負擔。此外，高耗能產業的訴求也引發討論：一方面，企業需要可負擔的清潔能源來實現去碳化目標；另一方面，若過度補貼電價，可能削弱市場推動能源效率提升的動力。歐盟執委會在制定電氣化行動計畫時，將如何權衡這些複雜的利害關係，備受關注。
後續觀察顯示，歐盟在推進氣候政策的過程中，必須同時兼顧產業競爭力與全球領導地位。CBAM的實施細節、電氣化行動計畫的具體內容，以及歐盟與主要貿易夥伴之間的協商結果，都將影響這項政策的實際成效。產業界與政策制定者之間的對話，預計將持續進行，以確保歐盟的綠色轉型不會因能源成本問題而停滯。
