  # Middle East Conflict Escalates, Countries' Energy Response Strategies Revealed
  
  > Middle East geopolitical tensions continue to escalate, disrupting global energy supply chains. Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs has prepared three contingency plans to stabilize energy supply, while a Dun & Bradstreet report highlights Taiwan's energy dependence as its greatest vulnerability. China, having invested in reserves and renewable energy over the years, faces relatively lower impact.
  
  Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have intensified in recent days, drawing heightened global concern over energy and rare gas supply chains. Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs announced yesterday that the government immediately activated an energy response team following the outbreak of conflict in the Middle East, and has prepared three contingency plans to address energy supply needs for March and April, ensuring stable domestic energy supply. This move demonstrates Taiwan's high level of vigilance regarding the deteriorating Middle East situation and its proactive deployment of protective measures.
  
  Regarding the energy risks facing Taiwan, Dun & Bradstreet's latest *Middle East Crisis White Paper* points out that the Middle East crisis exposes Taiwan to external shocks in "energy markets, logistics disruptions, and trade-related operations." Although Taiwan's export share to the Middle East is relatively low, its structural dependence on energy and key chemical raw material imports leaves the country exposed to a certain degree of risk. The report specifically warns that if the Middle East conflict continues to worsen and disrupts the Hormuz Strait—a critical global energy corridor—it would further impact Taiwan's energy and manufacturing costs, making energy dependence the greatest concern for Taiwan's economic development.
  
  In contrast to Taiwan's situation, China has demonstrated stronger buffering capacity due to years of advance planning. A March 20 report by Britain's *The Guardian* noted that China has been preparing for a global energy crisis for many years, including building substantial strategic oil and gas reserves and vigorously developing renewable energy, which has allowed it to weather the energy supply chain disruptions triggered by the Middle East conflict with relatively lower impact. The report highlights the differences in energy security strategies among nations and underscores the importance of energy transition and reserve development for national energy resilience.
  
  The International Energy Agency's (IEA) *2025 Power Market Report* takes a broader macro perspective on global electricity market trends. The report shows that global electricity demand grew by 4.3% year-on-year in 2024 and is expected to continue growing at nearly 4% annually between 2025 and 2027, representing a substantial increase in electricity consumption over the three-year period. The report also provides a comprehensive review of global power generation structures, regional distribution, carbon emission trends, pricing and market dynamics, as well as power supply security and resilience, offering important reference points for energy policy formulation across countries.
  
  In Oceania, Australia and New Zealand, as key regional economies, are also closely watching the implications of the Middle East situation for their energy policies. While neither Australia nor New Zealand has yet announced specific energy contingency measures in response to the Middle East conflict, regional energy experts note that Oceania countries have long depended on liquefied natural gas (LNG) imports from the Middle East, and any disruption to the Hormuz Strait would directly threaten regional energy supply stability.
  
  The Atlantic Council's report, *An Energy and Sustainability Road Map for the Middle East*, offers recommendations from a geopolitical perspective, urging the United States to prioritize resolving the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, delineating the land border between Israel and Lebanon, and addressing disputes among Turkey, Greece, and Cyprus, in order to ease tensions in the eastern Mediterranean and the broader Middle East and lay the groundwork for regional energy cooperation. The report emphasizes that energy security and geopolitical stability are inextricably linked, and that regional peace is the prerequisite for energy cooperation.
  
  Taken together, the impact of the Middle East conflict on global energy markets has become a matter of high concern for countries worldwide. Taiwan's government has activated its response mechanisms, but its structural energy dependence remains a challenge requiring long-term planning to resolve. China, having invested earlier in reserves and energy transition, has demonstrated greater resilience. International organizations continue to monitor global electricity demand growth and energy transition trends. The future development of the Middle East situation will continue to affect the stability of global energy supply chains.

# 中東衝突升溫 各國能源應變策略曝光

> 中東地緣衝突持續升溫，衝擊全球能源供應鏈。臺灣經濟部備妥三大方案穩固供氣，鄧白氏報告指出臺灣能源依賴成最大隱憂；中國因多年儲備與可再生能源佈局，受衝擊相對較低。

中東地區地緣衝突近日持續加劇，引發全球能源與稀有氣體供應鏈高度關注。臺灣經濟部昨日表示，政府在中東戰事爆發後已即刻啟動能源應變小組，針對3、4月份的能源供應需求，已備妥三大應變方案，確保國內能源穩定供應無虞。此舉顯示臺灣政府對中東局勢惡化已保持高度警戒，並提前部署相關防護措施。
針對臺灣面臨的能源風險，鄧白氏（Dun & Bradstreet）發布最新《中東危機白皮書》指出，中東危機使臺灣面臨「能源市場、物流中斷、貿易相關業務」等外部衝擊。儘管臺灣對中東出口佔比相對偏低，但進口對能源與關鍵化學原料存在結構性依賴，使臺灣整體面臨一定程度的風險。報告特別強調，若中東衝突持續惡化，導致全球能源核心航道「荷莫茲海峽」受阻，將進一步衝擊臺灣能源與製造成本，能源依賴問題成為臺灣經濟發展的最大隱憂。
與臺灣情況形成對比的是，中國因多年提前佈局而展現較強的緩衝能力。英國《衛報》3月20日報導指出，在當前動蕩的中東局勢中，中國多年來已為全球能源危機做好準備，包括建立豐富的油氣戰略儲備，以及大力發展可再生能源，使中國在面對中東衝突引發的能源供應鏈擾動時，受到的衝擊相對較低。此報導凸顯不同國家在能源安全策略上的差異，也反映出能源轉型與儲備建設對國家能源韌性的重要影響。
國際能源總署（IEA）發布的《2025年電力市場報告》則從更宏觀的角度盤點全球電力市場趨勢。報告顯示，2024年全球用電需求年增4.3%，預計2025至2027年間將持續以近4%的速度成長，三年內新增用電量可觀。報告同時全面檢視全球發電結構、區域分佈、碳排放走勢、價格與市場動態，以及供電安全與韌性等議題，為各國能源政策制定提供重要參考依據。
在大洋洲方面，澳洲與紐西蘭作為區域重要經濟體，其能源政策走向亦受中東局勢關注。雖然目前尚未有澳洲或紐西蘭官方針對中東衝突發布具體能源應變措施，但區域能源專家指出，大洋洲國家長期依賴中東液化天然氣（LNG）進口，若荷莫茲海峽通行受阻，將直接衝擊區域能源供應穩定。
大西洋理事會（Atlantic Council）發布的《中東能源與永續發展路線圖》報告則從地緣政治角度提出建議，指出美國應優先推動解決以巴衝突、劃定以色列與黎巴嫩陸地邊界，並處理土耳其、希臘與塞普勒斯之間的爭議，以緩和東地中海與中東地區的緊張局勢，進而為區域能源合作奠定基礎。此報告強調，能源安全與地緣政治穩定密不可分，區域和平才是能源合作的前提。
綜合各方資訊，中東衝突對全球能源市場的影響已成為各國高度關注的議題。臺灣政府已啟動應變機制，但結構效能源依賴問題仍需長期規劃解決；中國因儲備與轉型佈局較早，展現較強韌性；國際組織則持續關注全球電力需求成長與能源轉型趨勢。未來中東局勢發展，將持續影響全球能源供應鏈穩定。
