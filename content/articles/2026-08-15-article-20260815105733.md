  # Asia Regional Health Cooperation Framework Deepens as Multiple Nations Jointly Address Polarized World Challenges
  
  > The WHO Asia Pacific Observatory on Health Systems and Policies and the Asia-Pacific Health Security Action Framework continue to advance regional policy coordination. The 77th World Health Assembly side event focused on public health challenges in a polarized environment, while academic institutions also contributed analysis of historical experience and governance models.
  
  The WHO Asia Pacific Observatory on Health Systems and Policies (APO) serves as a key platform for regional health governance. Composed of a partnership among governments, international organizations, foundations, and researchers, the Observatory aims to promote the implementation of evidence-based health system policies across the Asia-Pacific region. According to WHO official information, APO continues to advance regional health policy coordination and knowledge sharing, covering health system development and cross-border public health issues among member countries.
  
  During the same period, the 77th World Health Assembly (WHA) held a side event themed "Addressing Health Challenges in an Increasingly Polarized World," with participation documented by the Taiwan Public Health Association and other institutions, highlighting the impact of current international political fragmentation on health cooperation. The Japan WHO Association also announced the Asia-Pacific Health Security Action Framework, which was endorsed by member states in 2023. Based on two decades of regional public health emergency response experience, the framework emphasizes strengthening preparedness and recovery resilience for health emergencies through collective action, contributing to global health security.
  
  Research from the Chinese University of Hong Kong indicates that the post-war East Asian model of state sovereignty has profoundly shaped the power dynamics of transnational public health agreements. In the twenty years following the establishment of the WHO in 1948, although international health practice was grounded in medical science and European public health experience, it was frequently constrained by U.S. trade interests and geopolitics, meaning that the international health architecture before the 1990s remained heavily influenced by state sovereignty thinking.
  
  Taiwan's health policy analysis compared the governance models of UNAIDS and the Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (FCTC), noting that UNAIDS relies on advocacy, guidelines, and financial assistance to influence national policies, lacking the legal framework and international treaty status provided by the FCTC. The FCTC assists countries in adjusting policies through clear provisions and principles, and provides evaluation, monitoring, and comparative standards, reflecting a centralized governance approach. Such historical and institutional analyses reflect the tensions between sovereignty and cooperation that regional health governance faces, with different models yielding varying effects on policy implementation.
  
  Academic and educational institutions are also actively participating in global health dialogue. The Global Health Program of the College of Public Health at National Taiwan University held a dean transition ceremony, demonstrating that regional higher education institutions continue to cultivate public health professionals and advance cross-border health research. Synthesizing information from all sides, the Asia regional health cooperation framework has gradually deepened, yet the political realities of a polarized world continue to pose challenges to the implementation of health agreements. Key areas for future observation include the concrete implementation effectiveness of the action framework, comparative results across different governance models, and how academic institutions can bridge policy and practice. Countries within the region need to further coordinate sovereignty considerations with the need for collective action to strengthen public health emergency response capacity and promote health equity.

# 亞洲區域健康合作框架深化 多國共應對極化世界挑戰

> 世界衛生組織亞太觀察站與亞洲太平洋健康安全保障行動框架持續推動區域政策協調，第七十七屆世界衛生大會邊會聚焦極化環境下之公共衛生挑戰，學術機構亦參與歷史經驗與治理模式分析。

世界衛生組織亞太健康系統與政策觀察站（Asia Pacific Observatory on Health Systems and Policies，簡稱APO）為區域健康治理重要平臺，該觀察站係由各國政府、國際機構、基金會及研究人員共同組成之合作夥伴關係，旨在促進證據導向之健康系統政策於亞太地區實施。根據WHO官方資料顯示，APO持續推動區域內健康政策協調與知識共享，涵蓋各國健康系統發展與跨國公共衛生議題。
同期間，第七十七屆世界衛生大會（WHA）舉辦邊會活動，主題聚焦於「應對日益極化世界之健康挑戰」，由臺灣公共衛生學會等機構參與紀錄，凸顯當前國際政治分裂對健康合作之影響。日本WHO協會亦公佈亞洲太平洋健康安全保障行動框架（2023年經加盟國承認），該框架基於過去二十年區域公共衛生緊急應變經驗，強調透過集體行動提升健康緊急事件準備與恢復韌性，並貢獻於全球健康安全。
香港中文大學相關研究指出，戰後東亞國家主權模式深刻影響跨國公共衛生協議之權力格局，1948年WHO成立後二十年間，國際衛生實踐雖以醫學科學與歐陸公共衛生經驗為基礎，卻頻繁受到美國貿易利益與地緣政治制約，致使1990年代前之國際衛生架構仍深受國家主權思維牽制。
臺灣衛生政策分析則比較UNAIDS與《菸草控制框架公約》（FCTC）之治理模式，指出UNAIDS仰賴倡議、指導原則與資金援助影響各國政策，缺乏FCTC所提供之法律架構與國際條約地位；FCTC透過明確條文與原則協助各國調整政策，並提供評估、監測與比較標準，體現中心化治理精神。此類歷史與制度分析反映區域健康治理面臨主權與合作間之張力，不同模式對政策落實效果存有差異。
學術與教育機構亦積極參與全球健康對話。臺灣大學公共衛生學院全球健康學程舉辦院長交接儀式，顯示區域高等學府持續培育公共衛生人才並推動跨國健康研究。綜合各方資訊，亞洲區域健康合作框架已逐步深化，但極化世界之政治現實仍對健康協議執行構成挑戰。未來觀察重點包括行動框架之具體實施成效、不同治理模式之比較結果，以及學術機構如何橋接政策與實務。區域內各國需進一步協調主權考量與集體行動需求，以強化公共衛生緊急應變能力並促進健康公平。
