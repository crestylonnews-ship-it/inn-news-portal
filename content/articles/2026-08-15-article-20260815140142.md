  # Geopolitics Drives EU to Accelerate Space Autonomy Layout
  
  > To reduce dependence on US space capabilities, the EU is speeding up defense and space autonomy initiatives, competing for polar launch bases in Sweden and Norway, while releasing a space act to reshape the industry ecosystem.
  
  Geopolitical shifts are profoundly affecting Europe's space strategic direction. According to Reuters, to reduce dependence on the US space industry, the EU is turning its attention to two small space launch bases at the northernmost points of Sweden and Norway, competing to become the first satellite orbital launch site on the European continent. US President Trump's "America First" policy, combined with the ongoing Ukraine war, has prompted the EU to accelerate its defense and space autonomy program, which has become the core driving force behind this round of佈局.
  
  PTS News reported that Europe faces dual pressures on economy and national security, making accelerated space佈局 a strategic priority for the EU. Polar launch sites within Sweden and Norway, given their geographical advantages, are seen as key nodes for Europe to achieve space autonomy. If Europe can successfully operate such launch bases, it will significantly reduce dependence on US or Russian launch platforms, enhancing the EU's autonomy and strategic flexibility in satellite orbital deployment.
  
  There have also been significant policy developments. Citing the EU's latest policy documents, the Shanghai Institute of Scientific and Technical Information reported that the EU's release of the "EU Space Act" and the "EU Space Economy Vision" marks a normalization shift in European space policy. The European space economy is now viewed for the first time as a comprehensive ecosystem, covering not only industrial foundations but also extending to orbital and extra-orbital space, including emerging areas such as space mining and space resource utilization. The report estimates that the global space economy will reach 1.6 trillion euros by 2035, and Europe is attempting to secure a favorable position in this rapidly expanding market.
  
  Dual-track development along commercial and military lines is another characteristic of Europe's space strategy. Zhihu column analysis shows that Europe's military space expenditure from 2026 to 2030 will total over 80 billion euros, more than 10 times its commercial space revenue, indicating that the EU regards space as a critical domain for national defense security. In the commercial launch market, SpaceX's Starlink already dominates the global low Earth orbit (LEO) market, with over 6,500 satellites deployed.
  
  Europe's commercial response is Eutelsat OneWeb, currently the only operational non-SpaceX global LEO broadband network, with over 600 satellites in orbit. In January 2026, it placed another order for 340 LEO satellites, manufactured by Airbus, bringing the total to approximately 440 Gen2 satellites across two generations.
  
  The European Space Agency (ESA) and the EU Space Programme Agency (EUSPA) continue to advance related programs. ESA's official website shows the agency regularly releases the latest developments in space exploration, human spaceflight, launchers, telecommunications, navigation, monitoring, and space science. EUSPA is responsible for implementing the EU's space programs, providing reliable, secure, and assured space-related services to maximize socioeconomic benefits for European society and enterprises.
  
  Synthesizing multiple sources, Europe's space autonomy佈局 is moving from policy planning to substantive action, with launch base competition, legislative framework establishment, and commercial and military investment advancing simultaneously, indicating the EU's attempt to carve out a third space power amid US-China space competition. The construction progress of future polar launch bases and the specific implementation of the EU Space Act will be important indicators for observing the effectiveness of Europe's space autonomy.

# 地緣政治驅動 歐盟加速太空自主佈局

> 歐盟為減少對美太空依賴，加速推動國防與太空自主，競逐瑞典與挪威極地發射基地，同時發布太空法案重塑產業生態。

地緣政治變局正深刻影響歐洲太空戰略走向。據路透社報導，為減少對美國太空產業的依賴，歐盟正將目光投向瑞典與挪威最北端的兩座小型太空發射基地，競逐成為歐洲大陸首個衛星軌道發射據點。美國總統川普推行的「美國優先」政策，加上烏克蘭戰爭持續，促使歐盟加速推動國防與太空領域的自主化程式，成為此輪佈局的核心驅動力。
公視新聞網指出，歐洲面臨經濟與國安雙重壓力，加速太空佈局已成為歐盟戰略重點。瑞典與挪威境內的極地發射場，因其地理位置優勢，被視為歐洲實現太空自主的關鍵節點。若歐洲能成功營運此類發射基地，將大幅降低依賴美國或俄羅斯發射平臺的現狀，提升歐盟在衛星軌道部署上的自主權與戰略彈性。
政策層面亦有重大進展。上海科技資訊研究所引述歐盟最新政策檔案指出，歐盟發布《歐盟太空法案》與《歐盟空間經濟願景》，標誌著歐洲太空政策正規化轉變。歐洲太空經濟首次被視為一個綜合生態系統，不僅涵蓋工業基礎，還延伸至軌道與軌道外空間，包括太空採礦與太空資源利用等新興領域。報告預估，到2035年全球太空經濟將達1.6兆歐元規模，歐洲正試圖在這一快速擴張的市場中佔據有利位置。
商業與軍事雙軌並行是歐洲太空戰略的另一特徵。知乎專欄分析顯示，歐洲2026至2030年軍事空間支出總計超過800億歐元，是商業空間收入的10倍以上，顯示歐盟將太空視為國防安全的關鍵領域。在商業發射市場，SpaceX的Starlink已主導全球低地球軌道（LEO）市場，部署超過6,500顆衛星。
歐洲的商業回應是Eutelsat OneWeb，目前為唯一已營運的非SpaceX全球LEO寬頻網路，擁有600多顆在軌衛星，並於2026年1月再訂340顆LEO衛星，由空中巴士製造，預計總計兩代共440顆Gen2衛星。
歐洲太空總署（ESA）與歐盟太空計畫署（EUSPA）亦持續推動相關計畫。ESA官網顯示，該機構持續發布太空探索、人類太空飛行、發射器、電信、導航、監測與太空科學等領域的最新進展。EUSPA則負責執行歐盟太空計畫，提供可靠、安全且具保障的太空相關服務，最大化對歐洲社會與企業的社會經濟效益。
綜合多方來源，歐洲太空自主佈局正從政策規劃邁向實質行動階段，發射基地競逐、立法框架建立、商業與軍事投資同步推進，顯示歐盟試圖在美中太空競爭的夾縫中，開闢第三股太空勢力。未來極地發射基地的建設進度與歐盟太空法案的具體落實，將是觀察歐洲太空自主成效的重要指標。
