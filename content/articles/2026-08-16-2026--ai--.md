  # 2026 Global AI Summit Debuts in Taipei, Focusing on Sovereign AI and Industrial Transformation
  
  > The 2026 Global AI Summit will be held on September 1 at the Grand Hotel in Taipei, focusing on sovereign AI and smart robotics development. An IMF report indicates that Asia's employment market will be affected by AI, with Japan and South Korea's policies showing significant results and research competition intensifying.
  
  Co-organized by the World Information Technology and Services Alliance (WITSA) and the Taiwan Information Software Services Association (TISSA), the "2026 Global AI Summit (WITSAGlobalAISummit2026)" will take place on September 1–2 at the Grand Hotel in Taipei. Under the theme "AI Alive, Beyond the Future," the summit will bring together global AI leaders, government representatives, industry and academic experts to explore AI industry development trends and international cooperation opportunities.
  
  This year's summit is guided by the National Development Council, the Digital Development Ministry, and the Ministry of Economic Affairs, and is co-organized by the Taiwan Digital Sustainability Association, the Taiwan Electrical and Electronics Manufacturers Association, the Taiwan Chain Franchise Promotion Association, the Taiwan Smart Automation and Robotics Association, the Taiwan Industry… and other organizations, demonstrating Taiwan's active role in AI policy and industrial integration. Yahoo Entertainment reported that the conference will focus on topics such as sovereign AI, smart robotics, and AI software-hardware integration, reflecting Asia's attention to autonomous AI development and technological applications.
  
  The International Monetary Fund (IMF) report analyzes the extent to which Asian economies are affected by AI, noting that the Asia-Pacific region may undergo structural transformations in its labor market due to artificial intelligence. Advanced economies are more significantly impacted, with approximately half of jobs exposed to AI disruption, while emerging and developing economies face exposure in only about a quarter of their employment.
  
  A special article by the Industrial Information Research Institute (DTRI) of the Cyberport further examines the current state of AI adoption across global industries, emphasizing that Japan and South Korea have made active efforts in recent years to promote digital transformation and AI industrial policies, achieving tangible results. The institute notes that industry AI surveys in Japan and South Korea have largely been incorporated into official regular surveys, using online questionnaires; as long as enterprises actually utilize AI or related technologies, they are counted in the ratio of AI-using companies, a method that helps accurately track the pace of industrial transformation.
  
  Research from the Japan Science and Technology Agency (JST) provides quantitative data showing intensifying AI R&D competition among major Asia-Pacific countries: the number of published papers in 2023 increased approximately twofold compared to 2021, and patent registrations grew by about 30% in 2023 compared to 2022, highlighting that countries view AI as a key technology for economic development and security assurance and are strengthening policy support.
  
  Synthesizing the various sources, the summit focuses on sovereign AI and industrial applications, while IMF and DTRI reports point out from the perspectives of employment markets and policy effectiveness that AI development in Asia shows disparities—advanced economies face deeper impacts, and Japan and South Korea's policy initiatives have yielded results. JST data further confirms that R&D investment continues to expand, reflecting intense regional competition. Future observations will focus on whether the Taiwan summit can generate concrete cooperation opportunities, and how Asian countries respond in AI governance and workforce transformation—issues that will shape regional industrial structures and the landscape of international cooperation.

# 2026 全球 AI 高峰會臺北登場 聚焦主權 AI 與產業轉型

> 2026 年全球 AI 高峰會 9 月 1 日於臺北圓山大飯店舉行，聚焦主權 AI 與智慧機器人發展。IMF 報告指出亞洲就業市場將受 AI 影響，日韓政策成效顯著，研究競爭加劇。

由世界創新科技與服務聯盟（WITSA）與中華民國資訊軟體服務商業同業公會（TISSA）共同主辦的「2026 年全球 AI 高峰會（WITSAGlobalAISummit2026）」，將於 9 月 1 日至 2 日在臺北圓山大飯店舉行，以「AI Alive, Beyond the Future」為主題，邀集全球 AI 領袖、政府代表、產業及學術專家，共同探討 AI 產業發展趨勢與國際合作契機。
本屆高峰會由國家發展委員會、數位發展部、經濟部指導，並由臺灣數位永續協會、臺灣區電機電子工業同業公會、臺灣連鎖加盟促進協會、臺灣智慧自動化與機器人協會、臺灣工…等單位協辦，顯示臺灣在 AI 政策與產業整合上的積極角色。Yahoo Entertainment 報導指出，會議將聚焦主權 AI、智慧機器人與 AI 軟硬整合等議題，反映亞洲地區在 AI 自主發展與技術應用上的關注。
國際貨幣基金組織（IMF）報告分析亞洲經濟體受 AI 影響的程度，指出亞太地區經濟可能因人工智慧經歷勞動市場結構性轉變，其中先進經濟體受影響程度較高，約有一半的就業崗位暴露於 AI 衝擊下，而新興市場與發展中經濟體僅約四分之一。
資策會數位轉型研究院專題文章進一步觀察全球產業 AI 化現況，強調日本與韓國近年積極推動數位轉型與 AI 產業政策，已取得具體成效；該院指出，日韓的產業 AI 調查多已融入官方定期調查，採用線上問卷方式，只要業者實際運用 AI 或相關技術，即計入使用 AI 企業比率，此方法有助於精確掌握產業轉型進度。
日本科學技術振興機構（JST）研究則提供量化資料，顯示亞太主要國家的 AI 研發競爭加劇：2023 年論文發表數較 2021 年約增兩倍，特許註冊數 2023 年較 2022 年增長約 30%，凸顯各國將 AI 視為經濟發展與安全保障的關鍵技術，並強化政策扶持。
綜合各來源，高峰會聚焦主權 AI 與產業應用，IMF 與資策會報告則從就業市場與政策成效角度，指出亞洲 AI 發展呈現先進經濟體受衝擊較深、日韓政策推動有成效的差異性。JST 資料進一步證實研發投入持續擴大，反映區域競爭激烈。後續觀察將關注臺灣高峰會能否促成具體合作契機，以及亞洲各國在 AI 治理與勞動力轉型上的政策回應，這些議題將影響區域產業結構與國際合作格局。
