  # EU Passes AI Regulatory Framework, UK Returns to Research Program, Tech Industry Reveals High Working Hours Challenge

  The EU has taken a critical step forward in AI regulation. According to reports, the EU AI Act, which aims to establish global standards for AI regulation, was approved by a majority vote in the European Parliament in mid-June. The legislation was submitted separately by the European Commission and the Council of the EU, and the three institutions are expected to enter negotiations to reach a final consensus. This move is seen as an important policy that could set a benchmark for the EU in global AI governance, potentially influencing future compliance requirements and the innovation environment for cross-border technology companies.
  
  The landscape of UK research cooperation is shifting. According to the Instagram account Orientis HK, the UK formally returned to the EU's Horizon Europe research program in 2024. The program carries a budget of €93.5 billion and covers multiple areas including climate technology, defense technology, and AI applications. The return means the UK can continue to participate in EU cross-border research projects and strengthen its engagement in the European tech ecosystem, though specific cooperation details and funding allocations remain to be confirmed.
  
  On the industry side, workforce challenges are emerging. Citing a BBC interview, Economic Daily reported that employees at tech companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic may work over 90 hours per week during AI development sprints, with this "drafted" model into AI teams potentially lasting for weeks. The phenomenon has sparked concerns about employee health and work-life balance amid the AI industry's rapid growth. Different perspectives suggest this reflects the pressure of the technology race and highlights the gap between regulatory frameworks and industry realities.
  
  In summary, the EU's policy advancement and the UK's return to research programs demonstrate Europe's dual-track strategy on technological sovereignty and cooperation, while the industry's excessive working hours serve as a reminder that regulation must balance innovation with workers' rights. Future developments will depend on the outcome of the three-way negotiations, the practical implementation of the UK's return to the program, and societal discussion on AI development work culture.

# 歐盟透過AI監管框架 英國重返科研計劃 科技業曝高工時挑戰

> 歐盟《人工智慧法》獲議會透過，英國2024年重返Horizon Europe計劃，同時BBC報導AI開發週期工時逾90小時，引發工作生活平衡關注。

歐盟在人工智慧監管領域邁出關鍵一步。根據鳴人堂報導，歐盟《人工智慧法》旨在制定人工智慧監管的全球標準，已於6月中獲得歐洲議會多數投票透過。該法案由歐洲執委會與歐盟理事會分別提出版本，三方預計將進行協商以達成最終共識。此舉被視為歐盟在全球AI治理中樹立標竿的重要政策，有望影響未來跨境科技企業的合規要求與創新環境。
英國科研合作格局出現變化。Instagram帳號Orientis HK指出，英國於2024年正式重返歐盟Horizon Europe科研計劃，該計劃預算達935億歐元，涵蓋氣候科技、國防科技與人工智慧應用等多個領域。重返意味著英國可繼續參與歐盟跨國科研專案，強化在歐洲科技生態系中的參與度，但具體合作細節與資金分配仍待進一步確認。
產業實務面則面臨人力挑戰。經濟日報引述BBC採訪的科技業員工透露，OpenAI與Anthropic等公司在AI開發衝刺期，員工每週工時可能超過90小時，且這種「被徵召」至AI團隊的模式可能持續數週。此現象引發對AI產業高速發展下員工健康與工作生活平衡的關注，不同觀點認為這反映技術競賽的壓力，也凸顯監管框架與產業實務間的落差。
綜合觀察，歐盟政策推進與英國重返科研計劃顯示歐洲在科技主權與合作上的雙軌策略，而產業高工時問題則提醒監管需兼顧創新與勞工權益。後續發展將取決於三方協商結果、英國重返計劃的實際執行，以及社會對AI開發工作文化的討論。
