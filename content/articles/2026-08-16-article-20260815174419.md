  # Africa Accounts for Nearly Half of Global Humanitarian Needs as Funding Gap Widens
  
  > UN reports show that as of April 2025, approximately 300 million people worldwide required humanitarian assistance, with Africa accounting for nearly 45%, or about 134 million people; the funding gap has left millions struggling in crisis for decades.
  
  According to the Africa Humanitarian Overview – Mid-Year Update (as of June 2025) published by the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), as of April 2025, approximately 300 million people worldwide required humanitarian assistance, with Africa bearing nearly 45% of the global demand, or about 134 million people. The report notes that Africa remains disproportionately represented in global aid appeals, yet the persistent funding gap has left millions struggling in crisis for decades.
  
  The Global Humanitarian Overview 2026, compiled and published by Humanitarian Action, is an annual assessment report designed to analyze the drivers of humanitarian needs, outline the scale of resources required to support target beneficiaries, and record the humanitarian system's achievements in collective action.
  
  DevelopmentAid's summary of the overview further explains that multi-donor Country-Based Pooled Funds (CBPF) mechanisms enable timely allocation and disbursement of donor resources to address the most urgent humanitarian needs and assist the most vulnerable populations; this mechanism, characterized by local prioritization, flexibility, and coordination, helps save lives and strengthen local response capacity.
  
  OCHA's official page also lists current priority areas, including accountability to affected populations, pre-action, civil-military coordination, climate change, private sector engagement, and evaluation and review; at the regional level, the report specifically highlights Sudan, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, the Central African Republic, Chad, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Mali, Niger, and Nigeria as key focus countries. Across sources, the scale of Africa's humanitarian needs and the funding gap are commonly confirmed facts, while the specific effectiveness of CBPF mechanisms and the actual progress of country-level priorities remain to be observed and further substantiated.

# 非洲人道需求佔全球近半 資金缺口持續擴大

> 聯合國報告顯示，截至2025年4月全球約3億人需人道援助，非洲佔近45%、約1.34億人；資金缺口已讓數百萬人在危機中掙扎數十年。

根據聯合國人道主義事務協調廳（OCHA）發布的《非洲人道主義概覽——年中更新》（截至2025年6月），截至2025年4月，全球約有3億人需要人道主義援助，其中非洲佔據近45%的全球需求負荷，約1.34億人。這份報告指出，非洲在全球援助呼籲中持續處於過度代表狀態，然而資金缺口的長期存在，已讓數百萬人在危機中掙扎數十年。
《2026年全球人道主義概覽》由人道主義行動平臺（Humanitarian Action）整理發布，該檔案為年度評估報告，旨在分析人道需求的驅動因素，並概述支援目標受助者所需的資源規模，同時記錄人道主義體系在集體行動上的成就。
DevelopmentAid 對該概覽的摘要進一步說明，多捐助國基於國家的 pooled funds（CBPF）機制，能夠確保捐助資源及時分配與撥付，以應對最迫切的人道需求並協助最弱勢群體；此機制以在地優先為特徵，具備靈活性與協調性，有助於挽救生命並強化當地應變能力。
OCHA 官方頁面同時列出當前優先關注的議題領域，包括對受影響人群的問責、預先行動、民事軍事協調、氣候變遷、與私部門的參與，以及評估與審查等；在地緣層面，報告特別點名蘇丹、布吉納法索、喀麥隆、中非共和國、查德、剛果民主共和國、馬利、尼日與奈及利亞等國為重點關注物件。綜合各來源，非洲人道需求規模與資金缺口為多家機構共同確認的事實，而 CBPF 機制的具體成效與各國優先順序的實際執行進度，則仍有待後續觀察與進一步資料佐證。
