  # EU Reforms Humanitarian Aid to Address Global Crises as European Security Strategy Faces Challenges
  
  > Conflicts in Gaza, Ukraine, and Sudan have driven humanitarian needs to record highs, prompting the EU to adjust its aid priorities; meanwhile, Europe's security strategy faces a dual test from the Russia-Ukraine war and shifting geopolitics.
  
  Ongoing conflicts in Gaza, Ukraine, Sudan, and other regions are placing unprecedented pressure on the international humanitarian system. According to EU-related reports, humanitarian needs in 2026 have reached historic levels, with an estimated 23.9 billion people requiring assistance. The combination of catastrophic humanitarian conditions and significant funding cuts has forced the EU to readjust its aid priorities to address today's global challenges. The European Commission has announced it will reform its working methods, aiming to maximize the effectiveness and responsiveness of EU humanitarian aid.
  
  The ongoing impact of the Russia-Ukraine war is also profoundly shaping the EU's policy direction in 2026. The European Council on Foreign Relations notes that, referencing a December 2025 paper by Hung Te-chin, the EU faces new trends and challenges in the security and defense domain. The European Commission's official website also indicates that current priorities include strengthening European defense and war-readiness capabilities, reinforcing common border management, addressing migration issues, and enhancing internal security. These measures reflect a shift in the EU's security strategy from traditional defense toward a more comprehensive security framework.
  
  On the link between human rights and economic security, Human Rights Watch reports that French President Emmanuel Macron is urging Europe to become a "true geopolitical and economic power" capable of safeguarding its long-term economic security and competitiveness, in response to what he calls an approaching Chinese "commercial tsunami." This perspective tightly links economic security with human rights protection, arguing that Europe's economic security begins with human rights. This aligns with the EU's long-standing advocacy of a "values-based" foreign policy, but has also sparked debate over how to balance economic interests with human rights positions.
  
  UK political developments are also indirectly affecting Europe's security architecture. BBC reports that ten years after Brexit, UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer announced his resignation on June 22, making him the seventh UK prime minister since Brexit. Political instability could affect the future pattern of UK-EU cooperation in security and defense, particularly regarding coordination mechanisms for cross-border security and migration management.
  
  The evolution of European security strategy can be traced back to the 2003 European Security Strategy, authorized by Javier Solana, the High Representative for the Common Foreign and Security Policy, with the aim of achieving "a secure Europe in a better world," covering threats such as terrorism, the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction, and regional conflicts. However, the current situation far exceeds the framework envisioned in 2003; the scale of the EU's humanitarian crisis, the complexity of geopolitics, and the challenges to economic security are all unprecedented.
  
  Experts observe that EU humanitarian aid reform and security strategy adjustments will become key indicators of European policy in 2026. How to reasonably allocate aid resources under limited funding, how to coordinate divergences among member states on security policy, and how to balance economic security with human rights values will be the core challenges facing the EU in the future. Subsequent developments warrant continued attention.

# 歐盟改革人道援助應對全球危機 歐洲安全戰略迎挑戰

> 加薩、烏克蘭與蘇丹衝突使人道需求創歷史新高，歐盟宣佈調整援助優先順序；同時歐洲安全戰略面臨俄烏戰爭與地緣政治變局雙重考驗。

加薩、烏克蘭與蘇丹等多地衝突持續延燒，國際人道體系正面臨前所未有的壓力。根據歐盟相關報導指出，2026年人道需求已達到歷史最高水平，預估將有239億人需要援助。災難性的人道局勢與資金大幅削減同時發生，迫使歐盟必須重新調整援助的優先順序，以應對當今全球挑戰。歐盟委員會已宣佈將調整其工作方式，目標是最大化歐盟人道援助的有效性和回應能力。
俄烏戰爭的持續影響亦深刻塑造歐盟2026年的政策走向。歐洲研究協會指出，參考洪德欽於2025年12月發表的論文分析，歐盟在安全與防務領域正面臨新趨勢與新挑戰。歐盟委員會官方頁面亦顯示，當前優先工作包含加強歐洲防務與備戰能力、強化共同邊境管理、處理移民議題，以及提升內部安全。這些措施反映出歐盟在安全戰略上從傳統防禦轉向更全面的安全綜合框架。
在人權與經濟安全的連結上，人權觀察組織報導指出，法國總統馬克龍力促歐洲成為有能力維護自身長期經濟安全與競爭力的「真正地緣戰略和經濟強權」，以應對他所稱的中國「商業海嘯」來襲。此觀點將經濟安全與人權保障緊密結合，認為歐洲的經濟安全始於人權。這與歐盟長期倡導的「價值觀導向」外交政策一脈相承，但也引發關於如何平衡經濟利益與人權立場的討論。
英國政治局勢亦對歐洲安全架構產生間接影響。BBC報導指出，脫歐十年後，英國新任首相施紀賢於6月22日宣佈辭職，使英國在脫歐後已出現七位首相。政治動盪可能影響英國與歐盟在安全與防務領域的後續合作模式，特別是涉及跨境安全與移民管理的協調機制。
歐洲安全戰略的演進可追溯至2003年透過的《歐洲安全戰略》檔案，該檔案由共同外交與安全政策高階代表哈維爾·索拉納授權制定，旨在「更美好的世界中實現安全的歐洲」，內容涵蓋恐怖主義、大規模殺傷性武器擴散、區域衝突等威脅。然而，當前局勢已遠超2003年時的預設框架，歐盟面臨的人道危機規模、地緣政治複雜度與經濟安全挑戰均為歷年所未見。
專家觀察認為，歐盟人道援助改革與安全戰略調整將成為2026年歐洲政策的重要指標。如何在資金有限的情況下合理分配援助資源、如何協調成員國在安全政策上的分歧、以及如何平衡經濟安全與人權價值，將是歐盟未來面臨的核心難題。後續發展值得持續關注。
