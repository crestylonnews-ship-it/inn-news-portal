  # Africa's Humanitarian Crisis Deepens as Terrorism and Climate Impacts Intertwine
  
  > A UN report shows Africa accounts for nearly 40% of global humanitarian aid needs, with terrorist attacks and climate disasters intertwining to exacerbate displacement and education disruption crises.
  
  The "2025 Africa Humanitarian Overview" released by the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) states that Africa's climate crisis is simultaneously a humanitarian emergency and a development challenge, as well as an opportunity to strengthen resilience and support sustainable growth. The report emphasizes that health emergencies are increasingly driven by compounding factors such as conflict, displacement, climate shocks, and weakened health systems, placing greater pressure on already fragile services. OCHA's mid-year update report released in June of the same year further quantified the scale of the crisis: as of April 2025, approximately 300 million people worldwide required humanitarian assistance, with Africa accounting for nearly 45% of global cases, highlighting the continent's heavy burden in overall humanitarian needs.
  
  The deterioration of security threats is equally concerning. The UN News website reported that UN Deputy Secretary-General Aminata Touré recently stated publicly that Africa has become the region with the highest number of terrorism-related deaths globally. She pointed out that branches of "Al-Qaeda" and the "Islamic State" are expanding across the African continent, with attacks in the West African coastal region alone rising 250% over the past two years.
  
  Armed conflict has also severely impacted education systems, leading to the closure of over 14,000 schools and affecting millions of children's education. When China Energy News reposted the relevant report, it added that security pressures in parts of Africa have recently intensified, with complex contradictions such as armed conflict, terrorism, political instability, and development deficits intertwining, causing the number of refugees and displaced persons to continue climbing and the humanitarian situation to remain dire. Abdul Abary, UN Secretary-General's Special Representative for Central Africa, also warned that some countries in the region are facing a serious shortage of funding for humanitarian agencies.
  
  The funding gap is seen as a key factor hindering relief efforts. According to China Energy News, Chad's 2025 emergency plan has only received partial funding to date, reflecting that overall humanitarian aid funds have failed to match urgent needs. While OCHA's overview report did not directly list country-specific funding data, it clearly linked the climate crisis with humanitarian emergencies, implying that funding and resource allocation must address both environmental and security challenges simultaneously. Different media outlets had slightly different narrative focuses: UN official channels emphasized specific data on the expansion of terrorism and education disruption, while Chinese official media placed greater emphasis on the structural issues of security pressures and insufficient funding.
  
  Both types of sources confirmed that Africa's humanitarian crisis is on a worsening trajectory, and the interactive impact of conflict and climate change has intensified the vulnerability of populations.
  
  Subsequent observations show that international attention to Africa's humanitarian and security issues continues to intensify. OCHA's mid-year update report and the Deputy Secretary-General's public remarks both point to the need for more coordinated cross-border response strategies. The rise in terrorist activities in coastal regions, along with phenomena such as the large-scale closure of schools, reflects that security crises have penetrated deep into social infrastructure. If the problem of insufficient funding cannot be improved, it may further delay relief and reconstruction efforts. Experts point out that if climate resilience building and sustainable development can proceed in parallel with humanitarian aid, some long-term pressures could be alleviated, but current funding and political will remain insufficient.
  
  Overall, Africa's humanitarian and security challenges present a multi-dimensional intertwined state, requiring international cooperation and resource reallocation to achieve results.

# 非洲人道危機深化 恐怖主義與氣候衝擊交織

> 聯合國報告顯示，非洲承擔全球近四成人道援助需求，恐怖襲擊與氣候災害交織，加劇流離失所與教育中斷危機。

聯合國人道事務協調廳（OCHA）發布的《2025年非洲人道主義概覽》指出，非洲氣候危機同時是人道緊急狀況與發展挑戰，亦為強化韌性與支援可持續增長的契機。該報告強調，健康緊急事件日益受到衝突、流離失所、氣候衝擊與衛生系統弱化等複合因素驅動，使本就脆弱的服務面臨更大壓力。OCHA於同年6月發布的中期更新報告進一步量化危機規模，截至2025年4月，全球約有3億人需要人道援助，其中非洲佔據近45%的全球案例量，凸顯該洲在整體人道需求中的沉重負擔。
安全威脅的惡化同樣引人關注。聯合國新聞網站報導，聯合國副秘書長阿明娜·默罕默德近期公開表示，非洲已成為全球恐怖主義致死人數最多的地區。她指出，「基地組織」與「伊斯蘭國」的分支正於非洲大陸各地擴張，僅西非沿海地區的襲擊事件在過去兩年內上升250%。
武裝衝突亦對教育體系造成嚴重衝擊，導致超過1.4萬所學校關閉，影響數百萬兒童的學業。《中國能源報》轉發相關報導時補充，非洲部分地區近期安全壓力增大，武裝衝突、恐怖主義、政治動盪與發展赤字等複雜矛盾交織，致使難民與流離失所者數量持續攀升，人道形勢嚴峻。聯合國秘書長中部非洲問題特別代表阿卜杜·阿巴里亦警告，該區域部分國家正面臨人道機構籌資嚴重不足的困境。
籌資缺口被視為阻礙救援行動的關鍵因素。據《中國能源報》引述，查德2025年應急計劃至今僅獲得部分資金到位，反映整體人道援助資金未能匹配急迫需求。OCHA的概覽報告雖未直接列舉各國籌資資料，但明確將氣候危機與人道緊急狀況相連結，暗示資金與資源分配需同時應對環境與安全雙重挑戰。不同媒體的敘事重點略有差異：聯合國官方管道著重於恐怖主義擴張與教育中斷的具體資料，而中國官方媒體則更強調安全壓力與籌資不足的結構性問題。
兩類來源均確認非洲人道危機正處於惡化軌道，且衝突與氣候變遷的互動影響加劇了民眾脆弱性。
後續觀察顯示，國際社會對非洲人道與安全議題的關注持續升溫。OCHA的中期更新報告與副秘書長公開講話，均指向需要更協調的跨國應對策略。恐怖組織在沿海地區的活動上升，與學校大規模關閉等現象，反映安全危機已深入社會基礎建設。籌資不足的問題若無法改善，可能進一步延緩救援與重建工作。專家指出，氣候韌性建設與可持續發展若能與人道援助並行，或可緩解部分長期壓力，但當前資金與政治意願仍顯不足。
整體而言，非洲的人道與安全挑戰呈現多維度交織狀態，需依賴國際合作與資源重新分配方能見效。
