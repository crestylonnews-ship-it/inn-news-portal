  # East Asia Leads in Digital Education Transformation, Cross-Border Education Becomes Asia's New Normal
  
  > East Asian nations are at the forefront of digital education transformation globally, with China, Japan, and South Korea pioneering the adoption of advanced technologies. Cross-border education models offer Asian students alternative pathways while driving national innovation and competitiveness.
  
  According to the regional edition of UNESCO's "2025 Global Education Monitoring Report," East Asian countries hold a leading position in the digital transformation processes of global economic, social, and educational systems. The report explicitly states that China, Japan, and South Korea are among the first nations to apply advanced technologies in classrooms, demonstrating significant achievements in regional education modernization.
  
  The UNESCO report further explains that China released its "Education Informatization 2.0 Action Plan" in 2018, proposing a vision for smart education and emphasizing the importance of personalized learning. Japan launched its "GIGA School Program" in 2019, with its implementation significantly accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic. These policies demonstrate that major East Asian economies are actively integrating digital technologies into their education systems to address future societal and economic challenges.
  
  Meanwhile, cross-border education models are undergoing significant evolution in the Asian region. According to reports from the China Social Sciences Daily, cross-border education has long provided Asian students with an alternative pathway, enabling them to obtain globally recognized degrees without having to travel to countries such as the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, and Australia. The report notes that this educational model is continuously evolving, becoming a catalyst for driving national innovation and enhancing competitiveness. Demographic changes, economic transitions, and accelerating technological transformations are reshaping the landscape and positioning of cross-border education.
  
  The East Asia Institute for Educational Research (IOEAE) emphasizes in its introduction that the impacts of globalization and industrial structural transformation have permeated all fields—including politics, economy, society, culture, education, environment, healthcare, and science and technology—facilitating exchanges of people and goods within East Asia and accelerating social and cultural development. The institution predicts that the 21st century is known as the "Asian Century," with Asia's GDP expected to exceed 50% of the world's total by 2050. This economic outlook provides a macro-level backdrop for regional educational cooperation and development.
  
  Regarding Taiwan, a research article by Wang Chun-bin published in the journal Contemporary Education Review of National Taiwan Normal University points out that, in a post-globalization context, Taiwan is gaining new development opportunities due to international factors such as international relations, geopolitical dynamics, and industrial supply chain restructuring. These new opportunities include economic investments that will drive human resource demands and international mobility, as well as related developments in language and education. The article focuses on the analysis of globalist and cosmopolitan educational philosophies, reflecting Taiwan's positioning considerations amid regional educational transformation.
  
  Synthesizing the various sources, it is evident that the digital education transformation and cross-border education development in East Asia present mutually reinforcing trends. The technological application leadership revealed by the UNESCO report complements the evolution of cross-border education models, indicating that regional education is advancing toward both diversification and modernization. However, different sources offer varying perspectives on the effectiveness of specific policies and future directions, and certain details still require further observation and verification.

# 東亞數位教育轉型領先 跨國教育成亞洲新常態

> 東亞國家在數位化教育轉型中居全球領先地位，中國、日本、韓國率先應用先進技術。跨國教育模式為亞洲學生提供替代途徑，同時推動國家創新與競爭力提升。

根據聯合國教科文組織（UNESCO）發布的《2025年全球教育監測報告》區域版指出，東亞國家在全球經濟、社會及教育體系的數位化轉型程式中居於領先地位。報告明確表示，中國、日本和韓國是率先在課堂中應用先進技術的國家，展現出區域教育現代化的顯著成果。
UNESCO報告進一步說明，中國於2018年發布《教育資訊化2.0行動計劃》，提出智慧教育願景並強調個性化學習的重要性。日本則於2019年啟動「GIGA學校計劃」，其實施程式因新冠肺炎疫情而顯著加速。這些政策顯示東亞主要經濟體正積極將數位技術融入教育體系，以應對未來社會與經濟的挑戰。
與此同時，跨國教育模式在亞洲地區正經歷重要演變。據《中國社會科學報》報導，長期以來跨國教育為亞洲學生提供了一條替代途徑，讓他們無需遠赴英國、美國、加拿大和澳大利亞等國家，便可獲得被全球認可的學位。報導指出，這種教育模式正在不斷演變，成為推動國家創新和提升競爭力的催化劑。人口結構變化、經濟轉型和不斷加速的技術變革正在重塑跨國教育的格局與定位。
東亞教育研究所（IOEAE）在簡介中強調，全球化與產業構造轉換的影響已波及政治、經濟、社會、文化、教育、環境、醫療和科學技術等所有領域，並促進著東亞人與物的交流、加速了社會與文化的發展。該機構預測，21世紀被稱為「亞洲的世紀」，到2050年亞洲的GDP將超過世界的50%。這一經濟前景為區域教育合作與發展提供了宏觀背景。
臺灣方面，國立臺灣師範大學當代教育評論期刊刊載王俊斌的研究文章指出，在後全球化脈絡下，臺灣正因國際關係、地緣政治動態以及產業供應鏈重組等國際因素，獲得新的發展機會。這些新機會包括將驅動人力資源需求與國際流動性的經濟投資，以及語言與教育領域的相關發展。文章聚焦於全球主義與世界主義教育理念的辨析，反映臺灣在區域教育變局中的定位思考。
綜合各來源可見，東亞地區的數位教育轉型與跨國教育發展呈現相互呼應的趨勢。UNESCO報告所揭示的技術應用領先地位，與跨國教育模式演變形成互補關係，顯示區域教育正朝向多元化與現代化並進的方向發展。然而，不同來源對於具體政策成效與未來走向的論述角度各異，部分細節仍需後續觀察與進一步驗證。
