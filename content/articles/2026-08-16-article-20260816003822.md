  # EU Releases Space Act and Space Economy Vision, Reshaping Europe's Space Policy Landscape
  
  > The EU recently released the "EU Space Act" and the "EU Space Economy Vision," marking a fundamental shift toward the formalization of European space policy. For the first time, the space economy is viewed as a comprehensive ecosystem encompassing both orbital and extra-orbital space, with the global space economy projected to reach €1.6 trillion by 2035.
  
  European space policy is undergoing a major turning point. According to a report by the Shanghai Institute of Technical Information (ISTIS), the EU has recently released the "EU Space Act" and the "EU Space Economy Vision," measures regarded as groundbreaking and marking a fundamental shift toward the formalization of European space policy. These policy documents position the European space economy for the first time as a comprehensive ecosystem, not only covering the traditional industrial base but also expanding to include orbital and extra-orbital space, encompassing emerging areas such as space mining and space resource utilization.
  
  A report published by indsr.org.tw notes that since humanity entered the space age, the space domain has become a strategic high ground for great power competition and security. During the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union were the primary actors competing in the space arena, and space effectively became one of the battlegrounds of their confrontation, sparking a "space race" that lasted approximately two decades. The development of space technology grew significantly during the Cold War due to U.S.-Soviet competition, evident not only in space exploration achievements but also in its profound influence on the subsequent direction of各國's space policies. This historical background provides an important context for understanding the current shift in EU space policy.
  
  The focus of the EU's policy shift lies in treating the space economy as a complete ecosystem. According to ISTIS, this ecosystem not only includes the traditional aerospace industrial base but also takes orbital and extra-orbital space into consideration, particularly emphasizing the development potential of emerging areas such as space mining and space resource utilization. The report estimates that by 2035, the global space economy will reach €1.6 trillion, indicating that the space economy is transitioning from a peripheral industry to an important component of the global economy.
  
  From a policy perspective, the two documents released by the EU carry clear strategic intent. The "EU Space Act" aims to establish a more comprehensive regulatory framework for space activities, while the "EU Space Economy Vision" provides long-term guidance for future space economy development. These two policy documents complement each other and together form a complete blueprint for EU space policy. Analysis by indsr.org.tw points out that the space race during the Cold War primarily focused on national prestige and military advantage, whereas contemporary European space policy places greater emphasis on economic value and industrial development, reflecting a trend in which the space domain is shifting from a tool of geopolitical competition to a driver of economic growth.
  
  Experts observe that the shift in EU space policy is closely related to the rapid development of the global space economy. With the rise of the commercial aerospace industry, areas such as space resource utilization, space tourism, and satellite communications have gradually become new economic growth drivers. Through the establishment of the "EU Space Act" and the "EU Space Economy Vision," the EU aims to ensure that Europe maintains a leading position in global space economy competition. At the same time, this policy also reflects the EU's concern for sustainable space development, particularly on issues such as space debris cleanup and the rational allocation of orbital resources.
  
  From an industry impact perspective, the shift in EU space policy will have far-reaching effects on the European aerospace industry. Traditional aerospace powers such as France, Germany, and the United Kingdom will welcome new development opportunities, while also helping to spur the growth of emerging European aerospace enterprises. The indsr.org.tw report emphasizes that the space domain, as a strategic high ground for great power security, remains no less important today; only the form of competition has shifted from pure technological rivalry to economic and industrial competition. The EU's policy shift is precisely an adaptation to this new competitive landscape.
  
  Regarding future development, the implementation of EU space policy will face multiple challenges. First, the rapid growth of the space economy requires substantial investment and technological innovation; how the EU integrates resources across member states and coordinates policy directions will be a key issue. Second, space resource utilization involves complex issues of international law and space governance, and the EU must strike a balance between safeguarding its own interests and promoting international cooperation. ISTIS reports that the global space economy is expected to reach €1.6 trillion by 2035; this enormous market size will attract competition from major economies worldwide, and the EU's policy shift is precisely aimed at addressing this challenge.
  
  In summary, the EU's release of the "EU Space Act" and the "EU Space Economy Vision" marks a new stage in which European space policy shifts from a traditional security- and technology-oriented approach to an economy- and industry-oriented one. This policy shift not only reflects the rapid development trend of the global space economy but also demonstrates the EU's repositioning of the strategic value of the space domain. In the future, the effectiveness of EU space policy implementation will influence Europe's position in global space economy competition and is worth continued observation.

# 歐盟發布太空法案與空間經濟願景，重塑歐洲太空政策新格局

> 歐盟近日發布《歐盟太空法案》與《歐盟空間經濟願景》，標誌著歐洲太空政策正規化轉變，太空經濟首次被視為涵蓋軌道與軌道外空間的綜合生態系統，預計 2035 年全球太空經濟將達 1.6 兆歐元。

歐盟太空政策迎來重大轉折。根據上海科技情報研究所（ISTIS）報導，歐盟近期發布《歐盟太空法案》與《歐盟空間經濟願景》，此舉被視為一項開創性措施，標誌著歐洲太空政策正規化的根本轉變。該政策檔案首次將歐洲太空經濟定位為一個綜合生態系統，不僅涵蓋傳統工業基礎，更將範圍擴充套件至軌道與軌道外空間，包含太空採礦與太空資源利用等新興領域。
indsr.org.tw 發布的報告指出，自人類進入太空時代以來，太空領域便成為大國戰略與安全的制高點。冷戰時期，美蘇兩強作為太空場域競爭的主要行為者，太空實際上成為兩強對抗的戰場之一，因而引發約二十年的「太空競賽」。太空科技的發展在冷戰時期因美蘇競爭關係而獲得大幅成長，不僅展現在太空探索成就上，更深刻影響後續各國的太空政策走向。這份歷史背景為理解當前歐盟太空政策轉變提供了重要脈絡。
歐盟此次政策轉向的重點在於將太空經濟視為一個完整的生態系統。根據 ISTIS 的報導，這個生態系統不僅包含傳統航天工業基礎，更將軌道與軌道外空間納入考量，特別強調太空採礦與太空資源利用等新興領域的發展潛力。報告預估，到 2035 年，全球太空經濟規模將達到 1.6 兆歐元，顯示太空經濟正從邊緣產業轉變為全球經濟的重要組成部分。
從政策層面觀察，歐盟此次發布的兩份檔案具有明確的戰略意圖。《歐盟太空法案》旨在建立更完善的太空活動監管框架，而《歐盟空間經濟願景》則為未來太空經濟發展提供長期指導方針。這兩項政策檔案相互呼應，共同構成歐盟太空政策的完整藍圖。indsr.org.tw 的分析指出，冷戰時期的太空競賽主要聚焦於國家聲望與軍事優勢，而當代歐洲太空政策則更強調經濟價值與產業發展，反映太空領域從地緣政治競爭工具轉變為經濟發展動力的趨勢。
專家觀察認為，歐盟太空政策的轉變與全球太空經濟的快速發展密切相關。隨著商業航天產業的興起，太空資源利用、太空旅遊、衛星通訊等領域逐漸成為新的經濟增長點。歐盟透過《歐盟太空法案》與《歐盟空間經濟願景》的建立，旨在確保歐洲在全球太空經濟競爭中保持領先地位。同時，這份政策也反映歐盟對太空永續發展的關注，特別是在太空碎片清理與軌道資源合理分配等議題上。
從產業影響來看，歐盟太空政策的轉變將對歐洲航天產業產生深遠影響。傳統航天大國如法國、德國、英國等將迎來新的發展機遇，同時也有助於帶動歐洲新興航天企業的成長。indsr.org.tw 的報告強調，太空領域作為大國戰略與安全的制高點，其重要性在當代並未減弱，只是競爭形式從單純的技術競賽轉向經濟與產業競爭。歐盟此次政策轉向，正是為了適應這種新的競爭格局。
未來發展方面，歐盟太空政策的實施將面臨多項挑戰。首先，太空經濟的快速發展需要大量投資與技術創新，歐盟如何整合各成員國資源、協調政策方向，將是關鍵課題。其次，太空資源利用涉及國際法與太空治理等複雜議題，歐盟需要在維護自身利益與促進國際合作之間取得平衡。ISTIS 的報導指出，全球太空經濟預計在 2035 年達到 1.6 兆歐元，這個龐大的市場規模將吸引全球主要經濟體投入競爭，歐盟的政策轉變正是為了應對此一挑戰。
綜合來看，歐盟發布《歐盟太空法案》與《歐盟空間經濟願景》，標誌著歐洲太空政策從傳統的安全與技術導向，轉向經濟與產業導向的新階段。這份政策轉變不僅反映全球太空經濟的快速發展趨勢，也體現歐盟對太空領域戰略價值的重新定位。未來，歐盟太空政策的實施成效將影響歐洲在全球太空經濟競爭中的地位，值得持續觀察。
