  # Africa's Space Industry Rises Rapidly as China-Africa Cooperation Deepens Aerospace Development
  
  > Africa's space industry is booming, with 318 space companies and nearly 20,000 professionals, reaching a market size of $19.49 billion in 2021. Countries such as Nigeria and Ghana are actively advancing satellite programs, while facing challenges including policy fragmentation and insufficient educational resources.
  
  Africa's space industry is experiencing rapid growth. According to a report released by Global Africa Sciences, Africa currently has 318 space companies and nearly 20,000 professionals, over half of whom are government employees. The African space economy was estimated at $19.49 billion in 2021 and is expected to grow to $22.64 billion by 2026. However, the report also noted that Africa's space economy still accounts for less than 5% of the global total, leaving substantial room for development.
  
  In terms of practical implementation, cases such as Nigeria's NigeriaSat series of satellites and Ghana's GhanaSat-1 demonstrate that African countries have begun to accumulate space technology experience. However, research cited by ebiotrade indicates that Africa's space research as a whole remains in its early stages, facing multiple challenges including policy fragmentation, insufficient investment in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) education, and the ineffective integration of indigenous knowledge systems. If these structural issues are not properly addressed, they may affect the long-term sustainable development of Africa's space industry.
  
  China-Africa cooperation has become an important force driving Africa's aerospace development. According to a report by People's Daily, China has been assisting African countries in enhancing their aerospace technology capabilities through technical assistance and talent development. To date, China has signed 23 space cooperation agreements with African nations, becoming a key partner in the development of Africa's space endeavors. This model of cooperation not only provides hardware and technological support but also encompasses knowledge transfer and talent training, helping to narrow the technological gap between Africa and developed countries in the space sector.
  
  Synthesizing information from various sources, the rise of Africa's space industry presents the following characteristics: first, the industry scale is expanding rapidly, with both the number of enterprises and employment growing in tandem; second, multiple countries are actively participating in satellite launches and space applications, with nations such as Nigeria and Ghana having established a preliminary technological foundation; third, international cooperation, particularly China-Africa cooperation, plays a pivotal role; and fourth, the development process still faces structural challenges including policy coordination, educational resources, and the integration of knowledge systems. Whether Africa's space industry can continue to expand its influence in the future will depend on whether countries can effectively resolve the above issues and further deepen regional and international cooperation.

# 非洲太空產業快速崛起 中非合作深化航天發展

> 非洲太空產業蓬勃發展，已有318家太空公司與近兩萬名從業人員，2021年產業規模達194.9億美元。奈及利亞、迦納等國積極推動衛星計畫，同時面臨政策碎片化與教育資源不足等挑戰。

非洲太空產業正經歷快速成長期。根據全球非洲科學組織（Global Africa Sciences）發布的報告指出，非洲目前已出現318家太空公司，從業人員近兩萬人，其中超過一半為公務人員。2021年非洲太空經濟規模估計達194.9億美元，並預期至2026年將成長至226.4億美元。不過該報告同時提醒，非洲太空經濟在全球總量中仍佔不到5%的份額，發展空間依然龐大。
在具體實踐方面，奈及利亞的NigeriaSat系列衛星與迦納的GhanaSat-1等案例，顯示非洲國家已開始累積太空技術經驗。然而，《生物貿易網》（ebiotrade）引用的研究指出，非洲太空研究整體仍處於萌芽階段，面臨政策碎片化、科學技術（STEM）教育投入不足，以及本土知識體系未被有效整合等多重挑戰。這些結構性問題若無法妥善解決，可能影響非洲太空產業的長期可持續發展。
中非合作成為推動非洲航天發展的重要力量。《人民日報》報導指出，中國透過技術援助與人才培養等方式，協助非洲國家提升航天技術能力。截至目前，中國已與非洲國家簽署23項航天合作協議，成為非洲太空事業發展的重要夥伴。這種合作模式不僅提供硬體技術支援，也包含知識轉移與人才培訓，有助於縮小非洲與發達國家在太空領域的技術落差。
綜合各來源資訊，非洲太空產業的崛起呈現以下特徵：一是產業規模快速擴張，企業數量與就業人口同步成長；二是多國積極參與衛星發射與太空應用，奈及利亞、迦納等國已具備初步技術基礎；三是國際合作尤其是中非合作扮演關鍵角色；四是發展過程仍面臨政策協調、教育資源與知識體系整合等結構性挑戰。未來非洲太空產業能否持續擴大影響力，將取決於各國能否有效解決上述問題，並進一步深化區域與國際合作。
