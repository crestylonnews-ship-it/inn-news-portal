  # Record Forced Displacement in the Americas as Global Humanitarian Aid Gap Widens
  
  > The UNHCR report indicates that the Americas have hosted approximately 20.3 million displaced people by mid-2024, while roughly 130 armed conflicts worldwide continue to intensify the humanitarian crisis and widen the funding gap for aid.
  
  The UNHCR's 2024 regional operations update for the Americas reveals an unprecedented crisis of mass forced displacement and mixed migration flows. The report notes that root causes—including violence, security threats, inequality, and a lack of socioeconomic opportunities—continue to drive displacement, while climate change and natural disasters further exacerbate the situation. By mid-2024, the Americas had hosted approximately 20.3 million forcibly displaced people, reflecting the increasingly severe humanitarian conditions in the region.
  
  The ICRC's 2024 annual report similarly notes that the number of armed conflicts worldwide has reached approximately 130, more than doubling over the past 15 years. The report emphasizes that people are facing multiple and compounding crises, with the number of those affected by armed conflict and its humanitarian consequences continuing to rise. The ICRC provides humanitarian assistance to millions across more than 100 countries, but the operational environment is becoming increasingly complex, presenting numerous challenges for aid delivery.
  
  The Global Humanitarian Overview 2025 report, published by the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) on July 4, 2025, focuses on funding gaps and compound disasters such as droughts and flash floods across 32 countries worldwide. Titled "The Cruel Math of Aid Cuts," the report highlights the stark gap between insufficient humanitarian funding and rapidly rising needs, calling on the international community to increase resource commitments.
  
  On the human rights front, Amnesty International continues to monitor the situation in the Americas, publishing research, advocacy materials, and educational resources. Amnesty International Hong Kong has also followed the case of Guatemala's former president Ríos Montt, who was convicted in May 2013 of genocide and crimes against humanity, though the verdict was overturned ten days later. Between 1982 and 1983, 1,771 Maya Ixil indigenous people were killed, tortured, subjected to sexual violence, or displaced during the country's internal armed conflict, and victims and their families have long sought justice.
  
  Reviewing multiple reports together, the forced displacement crisis in the Americas and the expansion of armed conflicts globally appear to be interconnected structural issues. The UN and humanitarian organizations consistently identify insufficient funding and lack of political will as the key bottlenecks in the current aid system, with further progress depending on sustained international attention and action.

# 美洲被迫流離失所創紀錄 全球人道援助缺口擴大

> 聯合國難民署報告指出，美洲地區至2024年中已接待2030萬流離失所者，同時全球約130場武裝衝突持續加劇人道危機，援助資金缺口擴大。

聯合國難民署（UNHCR）於2024年發布的美洲地區行動更新報告顯示，該地區正面臨前所未有的大規模被迫遷移與混合流動危機。報告指出，暴力、安全威脅、不平等、缺乏社會經濟機會等根本原因持續驅動人口流離失所，而氣候變遷與天然災害更使局勢雪上加霜。至2024年中，美洲地區已接待約2030萬被迫流離失所者，這一數字反映區域人道處境日益嚴峻。
紅十字國際委員會（ICRC）2024年年度報告同樣指出，全球武裝衝突數量已達約130場，較過去15年增長逾一倍。報告強調，人們正面臨多重且疊加的危機，受武裝衝突與相關人道影響的民眾規模持續攀升。ICRC在超過100個國家為數百萬民眾提供人道援助，但行動環境日益複雜，援助工作面臨諸多挑戰。
全球人道主義概覽2025報告由聯合國人道主義事務協調廳（OCHA）於2025年7月4日發布，聚焦全球32個國家的融資缺口、乾旱與暴洪等複合型災害。報告以「援助削減的殘酷數學」為題，揭示人道資金不足與需求急遽上升之間的巨大落差，呼籲國際社會加強資源投入。
人權組織方面，大赦國際持續追蹤美洲地區人權狀況，並發布相關研究、倡議與教育材料。香港大赦國際亦曾關注瓜地馬拉前總統裡奧斯·蒙特（Ríos Montt）案，該案於2013年5月判定犯有種族滅絕罪與危害人類罪，但十天後判決遭撤銷，1982至1983年間1771名瑪雅伊西爾原住民在國內武裝衝突期間遭殺害、折磨、遭受性暴力或流離失所，受害者與家屬長期爭取正義。
綜合多份報告觀察，美洲地區的被迫遷移危機與全球武裝衝突擴張形成相互關聯的結構性問題。聯合國與人道組織一致指出，資金短缺與政治意願不足是當前援助體系的關鍵瓶頸，後續進展有待國際社會持續關注與行動。
