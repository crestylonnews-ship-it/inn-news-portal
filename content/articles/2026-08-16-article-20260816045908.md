  # Africa's Energy Transition Opportunity: Clean Energy Investment and Climate Policy Advance Together
  
  > Africa accounts for 15% of the global population but consumes only 5% of global energy. The United Nations and research institutions are promoting clean energy transition, focusing on investment environments in 11 countries and climate strategies for 2025-2035.
  
  The African continent is facing dual challenges of energy development and climate change. According to reports from renewable energy professional media, Africa accounts for approximately 15% of the global population but consumes only 5% of global energy, forming a sharp contrast with the continent's abundant energy potential. Currently, about 90% of Africa's energy still comes from biomass, including wood and coal. This energy structure is not only unfavorable to environmental sustainability but also highlights the urgency of transitioning to clean energy. Reports indicate that due to the severe economic and environmental conditions facing the African continent, the development of clean and renewable energy is crucial to the region's future.
  
  At the policy and investment level, the Global Energy Internet Development and Cooperation Organization released a report titled "Research on Clean Energy Development and Investment in Africa," which systematically reviews Africa's overall policy environment. The report conducts in-depth analysis of 11 countries, including Egypt, Morocco, Guinea, and Nigeria, across six categories of power project development-related policies: business environment, clean energy development, electricity markets, industry investment, fiscal policy, and land, labor, and environmental protection. This research provides an important reference for international investors and policymakers, helping to clarify the opportunities and challenges facing African countries in the process of clean energy transition.
  
  The United Nations Economic Commission provides institutional support for Africa's climate action. The African Climate Policy Centre (ACPC), under the UN Economic Commission for Africa (ECA), has released its 2025-2035 strategic plan. As a continental-level knowledge hub and policy coordination platform, the center is committed to assisting African countries in advancing climate resilience, low-carbon development, and sustainability. According to official information, ACPC collaborates with governments at all levels to provide professional support for African climate policy formulation, with the goal of driving the entire continent toward green energy transition.
  
  Information from climate data institutions shows that climate patterns across Africa vary significantly. Taking Abidjan, the economic capital of Côte d'Ivoire, as an example, the city has a tropical climate with an annual average temperature of approximately 25.9°C and annual precipitation of about 1,441 millimeters. It experiences significant rainfall for most of the year, with only a brief dry period. These climate characteristics have important implications for energy infrastructure planning and renewable energy development.
  
  Geographical analysis indicates that Central Africa is primarily located in the Congo Basin, the largest tropical rainforest climate zone in Africa, covering nine countries: Angola, Congo-Brazzaville, Congo-Kinshasa, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Central African Republic, Chad, Cameroon, and São Tomé and Príncipe. The ecological environment and energy development issues in this region deserve continued attention.
  
  Synthesizing information from various sources, Africa's energy transition is at a critical stage. The involvement of international organizations and research institutions provides a policy framework and investment guidance for the continent's clean energy development. However, differences exist in the business environment, policy stability, and infrastructure conditions across different countries, and actual progress still depends on observing each country's specific implementation. Whether Africa can effectively harness its energy potential in the future will depend on the combined effectiveness of policy coordination, international cooperation, and private investment.

# 非洲能源轉型契機：清潔能源投資與氣候政策並進

> 非洲佔全球人口15%卻僅消耗5%能源，聯合國與研究機構推動清潔能源轉型，聚焦11國投資環境與2025-2035氣候策略。

非洲大陸正面臨能源發展與氣候變遷的雙重挑戰。根據再生能源專業媒體報導，非洲佔全球人口約15%，卻僅消耗5%的全球能源，與該大陸豐富的能源潛力形成鮮明對比。目前非洲約90%的能源仍來自生物質，包括木材和煤炭，這種能源結構不僅不利於環境永續，也凸顯轉向清潔能源的迫切性。報導指出，由於非洲大陸正面臨嚴峻的經濟和環境形勢，清潔和再生能源的發展對於該地區的未來至關重要。
在政策與投資層面，全球能源網際網路發展合作組織發布的《非洲清潔能源開發與投資研究》報告，系統性梳理了非洲整體政策環境。該報告針對埃及、摩洛哥、幾內亞、奈及利亞等11個國家，從經商環境、清潔能源開發、電力市場、行業投資、財政政策、土地勞工環保等六類電力專案開發相關政策進行深入分析。這項研究為國際投資者和政策制定者提供了重要的參考依據，有助於釐清非洲各國在清潔能源轉型過程中的機會與挑戰。
聯合國經濟委員會為非洲的氣候行動提供制度性支援。聯合國經濟委員會非洲經濟委員會（ECA）旗下的非洲氣候政策中心（ACPC）發布了2025-2035年策略規劃。該中心作為大陸級知識樞紐和政策協調平臺，致力於協助非洲各國推進氣候韌性、低碳和可持續發展。根據官方資料顯示，ACPC與各級政府合作，為非洲氣候政策制定提供專業支援，目標是推動整個大陸向綠色能源轉型。
氣候資料機構的資訊顯示，非洲各地氣候型態差異顯著。以象牙海岸經濟首都阿比讓為例，該地屬於熱帶氣候，全年平均氣溫約25.9攝氏度，年降水量約1441毫米，大部分時間經歷顯著降水量，僅有短暫降雨稀少期。這種氣候特徵對能源基礎設施規劃和可再生能源開發具有重要影響。
地理分析指出，中部非洲地區主要位於剛果盆地，是非洲最大的熱帶雨林氣候區，涵蓋安哥拉、剛果（布）、剛果（金）、赤道幾內亞、加彭、中非共和國、查德、喀麥隆、聖多美普林西比等九個國家，該區域的生態環境與能源發展議題值得持續關注。
綜合各來源資訊，非洲能源轉型正處於關鍵階段。國際組織與研究機構的介入，為該大陸的清潔能源發展提供政策框架和投資指引。然而，不同國家的經商環境、政策穩定性和基礎設施條件存在差異，實際進展仍需觀察各國具體執行情況。未來非洲能否有效運用其能源潛力，將取決於政策協調、國際合作與民間投資的綜合成效。
