  # The Economist: Africa's Energy Underuse Will Perpetuate Poverty; Green Energy Transition Draws Attention
  
  > An article in The Economist points out that Africa will remain poor if it cannot increase its energy use, with the roles of renewable energy and natural gas power generation becoming focal points, and global green energy investment trends also drawing attention.
  
  According to an article titled "Africa will remain poor unless it uses more energy," published by The Economist on November 5, 2024, the African continent will struggle to escape long-term poverty unless it can effectively increase its energy consumption. The article analyzes Africa's existing energy structure and the current state of renewable energy development, noting that while governments and major Western energy companies are actively investing in the renewable energy sector, natural gas power generation is still regarded as the primary option during the transitional phase.
  
  The article also criticizes a "Gaslighting Africa" phenomenon present in some international discussions, suggesting that external forces may be misleading Africa's energy policy direction. This issue has sparked widespread debate on Africa's energy transition pathway, particularly focusing on how to balance economic development with climate goals.
  
  The global green energy investment boom is also indirectly affecting Africa's energy market. Analysis by The Motley Fool in the United States indicates that the renewable energy sector has become the focus of capital markets in 2026, expected to attract trillions of dollars in investment annually, covering diverse areas such as solar, wind, and green hydrogen. Although the report centers on the U.S. market, it notes that the global energy transition wave will continue to drive clean energy development, indirectly reflecting the investment opportunities and challenges that emerging markets like Africa may face. However, the report does not directly discuss specific African policies or industrial progress, serving only as a reference for global trends.
  
  Regarding Africa's energy development, some analyses argue that natural gas power generation can serve as a transitional solution to meet immediate electricity demand and support industrialization, while another school emphasizes that renewable energy should become the long-term mainstay to avoid locking in high-carbon infrastructure. No cross-border consensus has been reached, and policies across countries still vary according to differences in resource conditions and economic circumstances. Future observations will focus on the implementation progress of the African Union's energy plans and whether international funding truly flows to grassroots electrification projects.

# 經濟學人指非洲能源使用不足將維持貧困 綠能轉型引關注

> 《經濟學人》文章指出非洲若無法增加能源使用將持續貧困，再生能源與天然氣發電角色成焦點，全球綠能投資趨勢亦受關注。

根據《經濟學人》2024年11月5日刊載的文章〈Africa will remain poor unless it uses more energy〉，非洲大陸若無法有效提升能源使用量，將難以擺脫長期貧困的困境。該文分析非洲現有的能源結構與再生能源發展現況，指出各國政府與西方大型能源企業正積極投入再生能源產業，但天然氣發電仍被視為過渡階段的主力選項。
文章同時批評部分國際討論存在「煤氣燈操縱」（Gaslighting Africa）現象，暗示外部勢力可能誤導非洲能源政策走向。此議題引發對非洲能源轉型路徑的廣泛討論，尤其聚焦於如何平衡經濟發展與氣候目標。
全球綠能投資熱潮亦間接影響非洲能源市場。美國《The Motley Fool》分析指出，2026年再生能源產業已成為資本市場焦點，預計每年吸引數兆美元投資，涵蓋太陽能、風力、綠氫等多元領域。該報導雖以美國市場為主軸，但提及全球能源轉型浪潮將持續推動潔淨能源發展，間接反映非洲等新興市場可能面臨的投資機遇與挑戰。不過，報導未直接論述非洲具體政策或產業進展，僅作為全球趨勢背景參考。
針對非洲能源發展的不同觀點，部分分析認為天然氣發電可作為過渡方案，以滿足即時電力需求並支撐工業化程式；另一派則強調再生能源應成為長期主力，以避免鎖定高碳排基礎設施。目前尚無跨國共識，各國政策仍依資源條件與經濟狀況差異而異。後續觀察將聚焦非洲聯盟能源計畫的落實進度，以及國際資金是否真正流向基層電力普及專案。
