  # Middle East Conflict Fuels Energy Crisis, IEA: Green Transition Accelerates but Warns of 'Black April'
  
  > IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol warned that a prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz could trigger a "Black April," while predicting that the Middle East conflict will accelerate the development of renewables, nuclear energy, and electric vehicles. Asian nations, driven by energy security concerns, are more actively advancing their green energy transitions.
  
  The International Energy Agency (IEA) released its "Global Energy Review 2026" report, compiling data on global total energy supply, electricity generation, technology deployment, and carbon dioxide emissions from 2023 to 2025, along with key indicators for major regions and countries. The report serves as an authoritative benchmark for global energy trends, reflecting the profound impact of recent geopolitical turmoil on energy markets. In an interview with France's Le Figaro, IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol stated that the energy squeeze triggered by the current Middle East war could become one of the most severe energy crises globally, yet it will also serve as a catalyst for accelerating the energy transition.
  
  Birol predicted that this crisis will drive the development of renewable energy, nuclear power, and electric vehicles. He simultaneously issued a stern warning that if the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, the global energy market could face a "Black April." The Strait of Hormuz is a critical channel for international oil transportation, and any disruption would significantly impact global oil supply. This warning has sparked international concern over further deterioration of the Middle East situation and underscored the region's strategic importance in the global energy supply chain.
  
  Asian nations are reassessing their energy security strategies in the face of the Middle East conflict. According to S&P Global, experts believe that amid the Middle East conflict, the case for Asia to develop renewable energy is even stronger. As the world's primary energy consumption region, Asia is highly dependent on imported liquefied natural gas (LNG) and oil from the Middle East, and geopolitical risks directly threaten economic stability. Consequently, countries are accelerating investments in renewable energy, energy storage systems, and the hydrogen industry to reduce reliance on fossil fuels. Singapore established its first IEA regional cooperation center in 2024 to promote energy transition and information sharing across the Asia-Pacific region, highlighting the importance of regional cooperation on energy security issues.
  
  Data from Taiwan's Environmental Protection Administration's Energy Information Platform shows that Singapore, as a leading energy and financial hub in the Asia-Pacific, has limited direct potential for renewable energy applications locally but will play a key policy role in areas such as international carbon markets, energy storage, and smart grids. This trend reflects how Oceania and Asia-Pacific nations are participating in the global energy transition through diverse pathways while balancing regional security and economic development needs.
  
  The Middle East Energy exhibition will be held from September 1 to 3, 2026, bringing together suppliers of transformers, switchgear, solar energy, energy storage, and software for energy infrastructure, providing one-stop solutions for project planning, procurement, financing, and construction. The exhibition reflects the Middle East region's strategic direction of actively expanding its green energy supply chain while maintaining its traditional energy advantages.
  
  Synthesizing information from various sources, while the Middle East situation poses short-term energy supply risks, it also serves as a catalyst for accelerating the global energy transition. Countries and international organizations are addressing geopolitical challenges through policy coordination, technology investment, and regional cooperation, while advancing the long-term transformation toward a low-carbon energy structure.

# 中東戰火推升能源危機，IEA：綠能轉型加速但警示黑色四月

> 國際能源總署署長比羅爾警告，若荷莫茲海峽持續關閉恐出現「黑色四月」，同時預測中東衝突將加速再生能源、核能與電動車發展。亞洲國家基於能源安全考量，更積極推動綠能轉型。

國際能源總署（IEA）發布《2026年全球能源評論》報告，彙整2023年至2025年全球總能源供應、發電量、技術部署與二氧化碳排放資料，並涵蓋重點區域與國家的關鍵指標。該報告為全球能源趨勢提供權威基準，反映近年地緣政治動盪對能源市場的深遠影響。IEA署長比羅爾在接受法國《費加洛報》專訪時指出，當前中東戰爭引發的能源緊縮，可能成為全球最嚴重的能源危機之一，但同時也將成為加速能源轉型的契機。
比羅爾預測，這場危機將推動再生能源、核能及電動車的發展步伐。他同時發出嚴正警告，若荷莫茲海峽持續關閉，全球能源市場可能迎來「黑色四月」。荷莫茲海峽是國際石油運輸的關鍵通道，一旦受阻將對全球供油造成重大衝擊。此警告引發國際社會對中東局勢進一步惡化的擔憂，也凸顯該地區在全球能源供應鏈中的戰略地位。
亞洲國家面對中東衝突，正重新審視能源安全策略。S&P Global報導指出，專家認為在中東衝突背景下，亞洲發展可再生能源的理由更加充分。亞洲作為全球主要能源消費區，高度依賴中東進口的液化天然氣（LNG）與石油，地緣風險直接威脅經濟穩定。因此，各國加速佈局再生能源、儲能系統與氫能產業，以降低對化石燃料的依賴。新加坡於2024年設立首座IEA區域合作中心，促進亞太地區能源轉型與資訊共享，彰顯區域合作在能源安全議題上的重要性。
臺灣環保署能源資訊平臺資料顯示，新加坡作為亞太能源與金融中心，雖本地再生能源直接應用潛力有限，但將在國際碳市場、儲能與智慧電網等領域發揮關鍵政策角色。此趨勢反映大洋洲與亞太國家正透過多元途徑參與全球能源轉型，同時兼顧區域安全與經濟發展需求。
中東能源展（Middle East Energy）將於2026年9月1日至3日舉行，匯集變壓器、開關裝置、太陽能、儲能與軟體等能源基礎設施供應商，提供專案規劃、採購、融資與建設等一站式解決方案。此展會反映中東地區在維持傳統能源優勢的同時，積極拓展綠能產業鏈的戰略方向。
綜合各來源資訊，中東局勢雖帶來短期能源供應風險，但也成為加速全球能源轉型的催化劑。各國與國際組織正透過政策協調、技術投資與區域合作，應對地緣政治帶來的挑戰，同時推動低碳能源結構的長期轉型。
