  # Africa Education Strategy Assessment Report Released; New Framework 2026-2035 Launched
  
  > UNESCO and the African Union jointly released the first periodic education assessment report for Africa, reviewing the implementation of the continental education strategy from 2016 to 2025 and launching a new framework for 2026-2035 to address ongoing challenges.
  
  The UNESCO Institute for Statistics (UIS) and the African Union Commission recently released *Africa Education: Continental Report*, the first periodic education situation assessment conducted through their collaboration. The report aims to systematically review the implementation progress of the *Continental Education Strategy for Africa (2016-2025)* (CESA 2016-2025) and assess progress toward Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG4). Co-led by UNESCO and the African Union, the report covers progress and gaps across regions in areas such as education access, quality improvement, and resource allocation, while outlining structural challenges that remain and directions for future efforts.
  
  The Institute of International and Comparative Education at Shanghai Normal University noted that the African Union Commission developed CESA 2016-2025 primarily to align with the continental sustainable development goals outlined in the AU's *Agenda 2063* and to cultivate a new generation of human resources grounded in African core values. However, the institute's analysis also cautioned that shortcomings exist in both implementation and awareness. Some countries maintain relatively low budget allocations for education, science, and innovation, and government commitment to public education requires strengthening—factors that may affect overall outcomes.
  
  The UNESCO International Institute for Educational Planning (IICBA) introduced the upcoming *Continental Education Strategy for Africa (2026-2035)* (CESA 2026-2035), emphasizing that the new framework regards education as a fundamental human right and highlights its critical role in sustainable development, peace, and socio-economic prosperity. Analysis suggests that the strategic transition from 2016-2025 to 2026-2035 reflects the African education system moving toward a more systematic transformation, but ensuring the fulfillment of budget commitments and narrowing regional development gaps remain key areas for future observation.

# 非洲教育戰略評估報告出爐 新框架2026-2035啟動

> 聯合國教科文組織與非洲聯盟聯合發布首份非洲教育定期評估報告，檢視2016-2025年大陸教育戰略執行成效，並推出2026-2035年新框架以應對持續挑戰。

聯合國教科文組織統計研究所（UIS）與非洲聯盟委員會於近期發布《非洲教育：大陸報告》，這是雙方合作進行的首份定期教育現況盤點，旨在系統性檢視《非洲大陸教育戰略（2016-2025年）》（CESA 2016-2025）的落實進度，並對照永續發展目標4（SDG4）的達成狀況。該報告由聯合國教科文組織與非洲聯盟共同主導，內容涵蓋各區域在教育普及、品質提升、資源分配等方面的進展與缺口，同時梳理當前仍存的結構性挑戰與未來努力方向。
上海師範大學國際與比較教育研究所指出，非洲聯盟委員會於2015年制定CESA 2016-2025，主要目的是配合非盟《2063年議程》所提出的大陸可持續發展目標，並培育具備非洲核心價值觀的新世代人力。然而，該校相關專題分析同時提醒，戰略在執行與認知層面存在不足，部分國家在教育、科技與創新領域的預算投入偏低，政府對公共教育的重視程度亦有待加強，這些因素可能影響整體成效。
聯合國教科文組織國際教育規劃研究所（IICBA）則介紹了即將實施的《非洲大陸教育戰略（2026-2035年）》（CESA 2026-2035），強調新框架將教育視為基本人權，並凸顯其對可持續發展、和平與社會經濟繁榮的關鍵作用。分析認為，從2016-2025年到2026-2035年的戰略過渡，反映非洲教育體系正朝向更系統化的轉型邁進，但如何確保預算承諾落實、縮小區域發展落差，仍是後續觀察的重點。
