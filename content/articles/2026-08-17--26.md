  # UN Warns Global Education Target Severely Off Track as 260 Million Children Miss School
  
  > UN Deputy Secretary-General Amina J. Mohammed warned at the 56th session of the Commission on Population and Development that global education progress has stalled, with over 260 million children and young people out of school, and the 2030 goal of quality education for all is severely off track.
  
  The UN Department of Global Communications released a report stating that Deputy Secretary-General Amina J. Mohammed, in her opening address at the 56th session of the Commission on Population and Development, emphasized that despite international commitments to education, progress remains slow, with over 260 million children and young people currently out of school, causing the 2030 goal of quality education for all to be severely off track. The warning is based on a comprehensive analysis of global education data, highlighting the multiple challenges facing the current education system, including unequal resource distribution, out-of-school children in conflict-affected areas, and the digital divide. The UN has called on countries to accelerate policy reforms and increase funding to regain progress toward education goals.
  
  The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) provided authoritative data on the structure, financing, and performance of global education systems in its *Education at a Glance 2024* report, covering member countries and partner nations. The report notes that the relationship between education investment and learning outcomes varies by region; while some Oceania countries such as Australia and New Zealand show steady performance in higher education participation rates, the Middle East continues to face challenges with out-of-school children and uneven educational quality.
  
  Different media outlets have interpreted the data in varying ways: the UN has focused on the warning regarding the number of out-of-school children globally, while the OECD report emphasizes structural factors and policy recommendations. Neither side has explicitly mentioned specific cross-border events in the Middle East and Oceania, but the data indicates that educational inequality in these regions remains a concern.
  
  Education experts and policy analysts believe this trend may exacerbate socioeconomic inequality, particularly in conflict-affected areas of the Middle East and Pacific island nations, where geographic isolation and resource scarcity further limit children's access to education. Future monitoring will depend on greater cross-border data coordination and international organizational cooperation to assess the effectiveness of policy interventions. Both the UN and OECD reports point to the urgency of transforming education systems, but specific implementation pathways and funding sources still require further consultation among national governments and the international community.

# 聯合國警告全球教育目標嚴重脫軌 2.6億兒童失學

> 聯合國常務副秘書長阿明娜·穆罕默德於人口與發展委員會第56屆會議警告，全球教育進展停滯，超過2.6億兒童與年輕人輟學，2030年優質教育目標嚴重偏離軌道。

聯合國新聞部門發布報告指出，常務副秘書長阿明娜·穆罕默德（Amina J. Mohammed）於人口與發展委員會第56屆會議開幕致詞時強調，儘管國際社會對教育問題作出承諾，但進展緩慢，目前仍有超過2.6億兒童和年輕人輟學，導致2030年人人享有優質教育的目標嚴重偏離軌道。該警告基於全球教育資料的綜合分析，凸顯當前教育體系面臨的多重挑戰，包括資源分配不均、衝突地區學童失學及數位落差等問題。聯合國呼籲各國加速政策改革與資金投入，以挽回教育目標的進展。
經濟合作與發展組織（OECD）於《教育概覽2024》中提供全球教育系統結構、財務與表現的權威資料，涵蓋會員國及夥伴國家。該報告指出，教育投資與學習成果之間的關聯因地區而異，部分大洋洲國家如澳洲與紐西蘭在高等教育參與率上表現穩健，但中東地區仍面臨學童失學與教育品質參差的困境。
不同媒體對資料解讀存在差異：聯合國側重於全球輟學人數的警示，而OECD報告則強調結構性因素與政策建議，兩方均未明確提及中東與大洋洲的具體跨國事件，但資料顯示該區域的教育不平等現象仍待關注。
教育專家與政策分析師認為，此趨勢可能加劇社會經濟不平等，特別是在中東衝突地區與太平洋島國，地理隔離與資源匱乏進一步限制學童受教育機會。後續觀察需依賴更多跨國資料協調與國際組織合作，以評估政策幹預成效。聯合國與OECD的報告均指向教育體系變革的迫切性，但具體實施路徑與資金來源仍待各國政府與國際社會進一步協商。
