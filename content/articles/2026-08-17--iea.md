  # UN Warns Asia-Pacific Transformation Readiness Lacking, World Bank and IEA Advocate Green Energy Pathways
  
  > UN report indicates multiple Asia-Pacific countries are unprepared for green transition; World Bank and IEA propose energy security and climate resilience pathways respectively; Asia becomes key region for global energy transition.
  
  The United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific (ESCAP) released the 2025 edition of the *Asia-Pacific Economic and Social Survey* on April 11, 2025. The report notes that while the Asia-Pacific region drives 60% of global economic expansion, many countries remain unprepared to address climate shocks and the impacts of transitioning to green systems. The UN press release emphasized that the survey focuses on macroeconomic dimensions, highlighting the coexistence of development gaps and transformation challenges within the region.
  
  Meanwhile, the International Energy Agency (IEA) published its report *Climate Resilience for Energy Security in Southeast Asia* in July 2024, offering specific recommendations for Southeast Asian countries. The report advocates that nations strengthen the linkage between energy infrastructure and climate resilience to address potential threats from extreme climate events to power systems and energy supply chains. Although the two reports focus on different sub-regions, both point out that the Asia-Pacific region must simultaneously balance economic growth, energy security, and climate adaptation throughout the energy transition process.
  
  The World Bank released its report *Green Horizons: A Sustainable Energy Future for East Asia* on August 28, 2025, as a follow-up to its 2010 publication *Winds of Change: A Sustainable Energy Future for East Asia*. The report proposes actionable pathways to modernize and upgrade the energy systems of East Asia, emphasizing that supporting the development of the power and industrial sectors is a key measure for the region to achieve long-term growth, create employment, and maintain economic competitiveness.
  
  CSRone Sustainable Think Tank, citing IEA perspectives, noted that the direction of global energy development in the coming years will be shaped by fast-growing markets such as Asia, which continue to advance low-carbon transitions. If countries adhere to their current energy policies and net-zero commitments, global electricity emissions will enter a sustained decline between 2025 and 2030, laying the foundation for achieving global climate targets. This view creates a tension with the UN report's assertion of "insufficient readiness," revealing differences in how various institutions assess the pace of Asia's transition.
  
  Data from the WTO and RTA Center of the Taiwan Institute of Economic Research shows that the Asia Zero Emission Center (AZEC) conducts policy coordination through the framework of the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office (ERIA) and builds green supply chains via collaborative projects such as developing zero-emission industrial parks, while also supporting the AZEC advocacy group in fostering dialogue among businesses. The Asia-Pacific Resilience Foundation, meanwhile, pointed out that amid rising geopolitical uncertainties, countries and industries should embrace the transition through regional economic partnerships and cross-sector cooperation centered on green growth. The foundation will also publish thematic reports on green supply chains, climate finance, workforce transition, the rise of artificial intelligence, and local government energy governance.
  
  A synthesis of all sources indicates that while the Asia-Pacific region's energy transition has clear pathways and emerging institutional frameworks, the readiness gaps flagged by the UN still require further coordination among national governments and international organizations.

# 聯合國警示亞太轉型準備不足 世行與IEA倡綠色能源路徑

> 聯合國報告指亞太多國尚未作好綠色轉型準備，世界銀行與國際能源署分別提出能源安全與氣候韌性路徑，亞洲成全球能源轉型關鍵區域。

聯合國亞洲及太平洋經濟社會委員會於2025年4月11日發布2025年版《亞洲及太平洋經濟社會概覽》，報告指出，推動全球60%經濟擴張的亞太地區，仍有多個國家尚未作好應對氣候衝擊與綠色系統轉型影響的準備。聯合國新聞稿強調，此概覽著重探討宏觀經濟面向，凸顯區域內發展落差與轉型挑戰並存現況。
與此同時，國際能源署（IEA）於2024年7月出版《促進東南亞能源安全之氣候韌性》報告，針對東南亞國家提出具體建議，主張各國應強化能源基礎設施與氣候韌性的連結，以因應極端氣候事件對供電系統與能源供應鏈的潛在威脅。兩份報告雖聚焦不同次區域，但共同指出亞太地區在能源轉型過程中，必須同時兼顧經濟成長、能源安全與氣候適應三大目標。
世界銀行於2025年8月28日發布《綠色地平線：東亞的可持續能源未來》報告，為該行2010年《變革之風：東亞的可持續能源未來》後續研究成果。報告提出切實可行的路徑，推動東亞地區能源系統現代化升級，並強調支援電力與工業部門發展，是東亞實現長期增長、創造就業與保持經濟競爭力的關鍵舉措。
CSRone永續智庫引用IEA觀點指出，未來幾年全球能源發展方向將由亞洲等快速成長市場決定，這些地區正持續推進低碳轉型；若各國能堅持當前能源政策與淨零承諾，2025至2030年期間全球電力排放將進入持續下滑階段，為全球達成氣候目標打下基礎。此說法與聯合國報告所提「準備不足」形成張力，顯示不同機構對亞洲轉型程式的評估存在差異。
中華經濟研究院WTO及RTA中心資料顯示，亞洲零排放中心（AZEC）透過設於東協及東亞經濟研究所（ERIA）的架構進行政策協調，並透過開發零排放工業園區等合作專案建立綠色供應鏈，同時支援AZEC倡議小組在商界間展開對話。亞太堅韌研究基金會則指出，在地緣政治不確定性升高背景下，各國與各產業應以綠色成長的區域經濟夥伴關係及跨部門合作迎向轉型，該計畫亦將針對綠色供應鏈、氣候金融、勞動力轉型、人工智慧興起及地方政府能源治理等主題發表專題報告。
綜合各來源觀察，亞太地區能源轉型雖有明確路徑與機制雛形，但聯合國警示的準備落差仍待各國政府與國際組織進一步協調填補。
