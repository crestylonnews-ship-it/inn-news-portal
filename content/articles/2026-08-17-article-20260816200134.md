  # Asia's Energy Transition Accelerates as East and Southeast Asia Face Resilience and Equity Challenges
  
  > A World Bank report highlights that East Asia must reduce carbon emissions through efficiency improvements and electrification, while the IEA emphasizes that the Asia-Pacific region, home to over half of the world's population, significantly influences the global energy transition trajectory. Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs Energy Bureau has analyzed climate resilience measures in Southeast Asia. Asia leads the world in renewable energy growth, but financing and equitable transition remain pressing concerns.
  
  The World Bank recently released its report "Green Horizon: East Asia's Sustainable Energy Future," providing an in-depth analysis of how the East Asian region can integrate energy and development needs. The report indicates that the power and industrial sectors account for 75% to 87% of the region's emissions. To achieve carbon reduction targets, the region must pursue pathways such as improving energy efficiency, expanding electrification, developing renewable energy, and adopting advanced technologies.
  
  This report is regarded as an important reference document for understanding East Asia's energy transition pathway, providing concrete decarbonization directions for policymakers and industry.
  
  The International Energy Agency (IEA) emphasizes on its Asia-Pacific page that the Asia-Pacific region, which supports over half of the world's population, will play a key role in shaping the global energy future. The report specifically notes that China, as the world's largest energy producer and consumer, will determine the trajectory of the global energy transition. Asia as a whole leads global renewable energy growth, accounting for 71% of new installed capacity. Although China and India face different challenges, both have made significant progress in solar, wind, and energy modernization. However, structural challenges such as regional inequality, dependence on coal, and financing difficulties for an equitable transition remain.
  
  Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs Energy Bureau has conducted a special analysis on energy security and climate resilience in Southeast Asia. Referencing the IEA's July 2024 report "Climate Resilience to Enhance Energy Security in Southeast Asia," the bureau focused on Chapter 3, "Measures to Strengthen Climate Resilience of Energy Systems," exploring what specific actions Southeast Asian countries can take to address the impacts of climate change on energy systems. This analysis demonstrates Taiwan's continued attention to regional energy security issues and its active alignment with international energy policy developments.
  
  The Capri Foundation for Asia-Pacific Resilience Research has also offered observations on green transition and Asia-Pacific economic development, pointing out that in the context of rising geopolitical uncertainty, countries and industries should strengthen resilience through regional economic partnerships for green growth and cross-sector collaboration. The foundation plans to publish special reports on topics including green supply chains, climate finance, workforce transition, the rise of artificial intelligence, and local government energy governance, reflecting the continued attention of private research institutions to the multifaceted issues of Asia's energy transition.
  
  Synthesizing observations from various sources, while Asia's energy transition has shown impressive growth in renewable energy installed capacity, East and Southeast Asia still face complex challenges including decarbonization pathways, climate resilience building, improvement of financing mechanisms, and transition equity. The World Bank report focuses on decarbonization pathways for East Asia's power and industrial sectors, the IEA emphasizes the global impact of China's energy trends, and Taiwan's Energy Bureau has analyzed climate resilience measures in Southeast Asia, indicating that regional countries and international organizations are advancing the energy transition agenda from different angles. How Asian countries will balance economic development with carbon reduction goals in the future will be a key factor influencing the effectiveness of the global energy transition.

# 亞洲能源轉型加速 東亞東南亞面臨韌性與公平挑戰

> 世界銀行報告指出東亞需透過效率提升與電氣化減碳，IEA強調亞太佔全球人口過半影響能源過渡軌跡，臺灣能源局研析東南亞氣候韌性措施，亞洲再生能源成長領先全球但融資與公平轉型仍待解決。

世界銀行（World Bank）近日發布《綠色地平線：東亞的可持續能源未來》（Green Horizon: East Asia’s Sustainable Energy Future）報告，深入分析東亞地區如何將能源與發展需求相結合。報告指出，電力與工業部門佔該地區排放量的75%至87%，要實現減碳目標，必須透過提升能源效率、擴大電氣化、發展再生能源以及採用先進技術等途徑。
此報告被視為理解東亞能源轉型路徑的重要參考檔案，為政策制定者與產業界提供具體的減碳方向。
國際能源署（IEA）在其亞太地區專頁中強調，亞太地區承載全球超過一半的人口，將在塑造全球能源未來中扮演關鍵角色。報告特別指出，中國作為全球最大的能源生產國與消費國，其能源趨勢將決定全球能源過渡的軌跡。亞洲整體引領全球再生能源成長，佔新增裝置容量的71%，其中中國與印度雖面臨不同挑戰，但已在太陽能、風能及能源現代化方面取得重大進展。然而，地區不平等、對煤炭的依賴，以及公平轉型所需的融資困難等結構性挑戰依然存在。
臺灣經濟部能源局則針對東南亞能源安全與氣候韌性進行專題研析。該局參考國際能源署於2024年7月發布的《促進東南亞能源安全之氣候韌性》報告，聚焦於第三章節「強化能源系統氣候韌性的措施」，探討東南亞國家可採取哪些具體行動來應對氣候變遷對能源系統的衝擊。此研析顯示臺灣持續關注區域能源安全議題，並積極對接國際能源政策發展。
亞太堅韌研究基金會（Capri Foundation）亦針對綠色轉型與亞太經濟發展提出觀察，指出在地緣政治不確定性升高的背景下，各國與各產業應透過綠色成長的區域經濟夥伴關係及跨部門合作來強化韌性。該基金會規劃針對綠色供應鏈、氣候金融、勞動力轉型、人工智慧的興起，以及地方政府能源治理等主題發表專題報告，顯示民間研究機構對亞洲能源轉型多面向議題的持續關注。
綜合各來源觀察，亞洲能源轉型雖在再生能源裝置容量增長上表現亮眼，但東亞與東南亞仍須面對減碳路徑、氣候韌性建構、融資機制完善及轉型公平性等複雜課題。世界銀行報告聚焦東亞電力與工業部門的減碳途徑，IEA強調中國能源趨勢對全球的影響，臺灣能源局則針對東南亞氣候韌性措施進行研析，顯示區域各國與國際組織正從不同角度推動能源轉型議程。未來亞洲各國如何在經濟發展與減碳目標之間取得平衡，將是影響全球能源過渡成效的關鍵因素。
