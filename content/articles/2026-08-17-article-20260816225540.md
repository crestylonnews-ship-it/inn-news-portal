  # China Expands Space Infrastructure Layout in Southeast Asia as Taiwan Advances Space Technology Development
  
  > China is actively expanding its space infrastructure and extending cooperation into Southeast Asia, while Taiwan continues to advance its long-term space technology development plans, gradually shaping the landscape of Asian space competition and cooperation.
  
  As the Chinese People's Liberation Army (PLA) expands its global influence, it is simultaneously actively building up space infrastructure, planning to establish a data-sharing system connecting land, sea, air, and space. According to Focus News, China's planned expansion covers remote sensing stations, launch sites, satellite control centers, data reception stations, communication networks, and deep-space tracking networks, aiming to meet both civilian and military needs. This move demonstrates China's systematic strengthening of space infrastructure and its attempt to extend its cooperation network into Southeast Asia, further consolidating its regional strategic position.
  
  On Taiwan's side, the Executive Yuan's global information website notes that since the Soviet Union launched the world's first artificial satellite in 1957, satellite technology has been widely applied in communications, remote sensing, navigation, meteorology, and scientific fields, profoundly impacting society and people's livelihoods. Recognizing that space technology can drive cutting-edge technological innovation, enhance national competitiveness, and improve public welfare, Taiwan established the National Space Science and Technology Development Long-Term Planning Group in November 1990 (Year 79 of the Republic of China calendar) to formulate long-term development strategies. This policy direction reflects Taiwan's continued emphasis on space technology development and its attempt to maintain technological visibility in regional space competition.
  
  The International Science Council (ISC) is also paying attention to the potential for Asian space science cooperation. Through its regional liaison points, the organization promotes flagship programs based on the priorities and needs of Asia and the Pacific region, and integrates the "Unleashing Science" and "Transforming the Science Model" reports to advance regional scientific cooperation. This initiative provides a multilateral framework for Asian space science cooperation and could serve as an important platform for countries to coordinate space policies and technological development.
  
  In comprehensive observation, China's space infrastructure expansion in Southeast Asia and Taiwan's continued advancement of space technology development reflect increasingly intense regional space competition in Asia. Meanwhile, the involvement of the International Science Council offers another pathway for regional cooperation. The future Asian space landscape will depend on policy coordination, technological cooperation, and geopolitical interactions among countries, warranting continued attention.

# 中國擴大太空基建佈局東南亞 臺灣同步推動太空科技發展

> 中國積極擴建太空基礎設施並拓展東南亞合作，臺灣亦持續推動太空科技發展長程計畫，亞洲太空競爭與合作格局逐步成形。

中國人民解放軍（PLA）在拓展全球影響力之際，同步積極擴建太空基礎設施，計畫建立陸海空與太空之間的資料共享體系。根據《焦點》報導，中國規劃中的擴張領域涵蓋遙測站、發射場、衛星控制中心、資料接收站、通訊網路及深層太空追蹤網路，旨在滿足民用與軍事雙重需求。此舉顯示中國正系統性地強化太空基建，並試圖將合作網路延伸至東南亞地區，進一步鞏固區域戰略地位。
臺灣方面，行政院全球資訊網指出，自1957年蘇聯發射全球第一枚人造衛星以來，衛星科技已廣泛運用於通訊、遙測、導航、氣象及科學領域，對社會與民生影響深遠。鑑於太空科技可帶動尖端技術研發創新、提升國家競爭力及增進民生福祉，臺灣於79年11月成立國家太空科技發展長程計畫規劃小組，擬定長期發展策略。此政策方向顯示臺灣持續重視太空科技發展，並試圖在區域太空競爭中維持技術能見度。
國際科學理事會（ISC）亦關注亞洲太空科學合作潛力。該組織透過區域聯絡點，依據亞洲與太平洋地區的優先事項與需求，推動旗艦計劃，並結合「釋放科學」及「翻轉科學模型」報告，促進區域科學合作。此舉為亞洲太空科學合作提供多邊框架，可能成為各國協調太空政策與技術發展的重要平臺。
綜合觀察，中國在東南亞的太空基建擴張與臺灣持續推動的太空科技發展，反映亞洲區域太空競爭日益激烈。同時，國際科學理事會的介入為區域合作提供另一條路徑。未來亞洲太空格局將取決於各國政策協調、技術合作與地緣政治互動，值得持續關注。
