  # European Political Fragmentation Intensifies as Traditional Parties Decline and Local Forces Rise
  
  > Mainstream traditional left and right parties across Europe are mired in governance difficulties with declining effectiveness; locally oriented and radical parties are rising in their wake, reshaping the political landscape, with populism, climate policy, and digital innovation emerging as key challenges.
  
  According to a report by Hong Kong's Wen Wei Po, European politics is undergoing a profound structural transformation, with mainstream traditional left and right parties broadly陷入執政困境 and finding governance increasingly difficult. Faced with the public's core demands regarding livelihood pressures, economic downturns, and social divisions, traditional parties appear at a loss, their governance effectiveness continuing to decline and gradually eroding their popular support base. Against this backdrop, locally oriented and radical parties are rising, bringing about a restructuring of the European political landscape. The report notes that Reform UK and the Green Party in the UK have seen significant surges in support, reflecting voters' distrust of traditional political elites and dissatisfaction with existing policies.
  
  European political scholars and think tank observers point out that this trend is not a phenomenon limited to a single country but a cross-border structural transformation. The Centre for European Policy Studies (CEPS), an independent think tank that has long tracked EU policy directions, serves government agencies, foreign ministries, and EU representations across member states through its Government Members Programme, providing an independent research and policy dialogue platform. CEPS research shows that the challenges facing European traditional parties stem not only from election results but also from public skepticism toward the EU's overall governance effectiveness. The European Commission's official website also acknowledges that from post-pandemic recovery plans to solidarity support for Ukraine, the EU is striving to translate strategy into concrete policies and initiatives, but the implementation process faces multiple political and economic pressures.
  
  Wen Wei Po further analyzes that the deep-rooted causes of European political fragmentation include: first, economic stagnation and inflationary pressures have undermined the promises of traditional parties; second, immigration issues and identity debates have intensified social divisions; third, on climate policy and digital transformation, traditional parties have held ambiguous positions and struggled to respond to the demands of younger voters. European Times also emphasized that the rise of populism, climate policy, and digital innovation are key issues affecting European Parliament decision-making, and these factors will shape Europe's future governance direction. The report points out that voters' distrust of traditional politics has translated into support for far-right and far-left parties, and the political spectrum within the European Parliament continues to become increasingly multipolar.
  
  At the EU institutional level, according to data from Taiwan's Department of International Trade, after the Lisbon Treaty took effect on December 1, 2009, the practice of rotating ministerial presidencies of the Council of the EU among member states was abolished, replaced by a permanent president elected by the Council, serving a two-and-a-half-year term renewable once, and directly accountable to the European Parliament; additionally, the position of "High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy" was established to strengthen the consistency of the EU's external action. However, the institutional design has not fully resolved the governance challenges brought about by political fragmentation.
  
  Wikipedia data shows that the EU is a political union of 27 countries combining intergovernmental and supranational elements, with important mechanisms including a single market, customs union, a single currency (adopted by 15 of 28 member states as of 2008), a common agricultural policy, and a common fisheries policy, but the difficulty of policy coordination has risen alongside expanding political divisions among member states.
  
  Synthesizing observations from various sources, the trend of European political fragmentation is unlikely to be reversed in the short term. Traditional parties urgently need to rebuild their connection with voters, while local and radical parties must face the test of governing responsibilities. How EU institutions and member state governments can maintain policy continuity under populist pressure will be the core issue of European governance in the coming years.

# 歐洲政治碎片化加劇 傳統政黨式微本土勢力崛起

> 歐洲主流傳統左右翼政黨深陷執政困境，治理效能持續走低；本土傾向與激進派政黨乘勢崛起，政治版圖迎來重構，民粹主義、氣候政策與數位創新成關鍵挑戰。

根據香港《文匯報》報導，歐洲政治正經歷深刻結構性轉變，主流傳統左右翼政黨普遍陷入執政困境，施政舉步維艱。面對民生壓力、經濟頹勢與社會分歧等民眾核心訴求，傳統政黨顯得束手無策，治理效能持續走低，逐漸喪失民意基礎。在此背景下，本土傾向與激進派政黨乘勢崛起，歐洲政治版圖迎來重構。報導指出，英國改革黨（Reform UK）與綠黨（Green Party）聲勢大漲，支援率顯著上升，反映選民對傳統政治精英的不信任與對現有政策的不滿。
歐洲政治學者與智庫觀察指出，此趨勢並非單一國家現象，而是跨國性的結構性轉變。歐洲政策研究中心（CEPS）作為獨立智庫，長期追蹤歐盟政策走向，其政府會員計畫服務於各國主管機關、外交部與駐歐盟代表團，提供獨立研究與政策對話平臺。CEPS的研究顯示，歐洲傳統政黨面臨的挑戰不僅來自選舉結果，更源於民眾對歐盟整體治理效能的質疑。歐盟委員會官方網站亦承認，從疫情後復甦計畫到對烏克蘭的團結支援，歐盟正努力將戰略轉化為具體政策與倡議，但執行過程面臨多重政治與經濟壓力。
《文匯報》進一步分析，歐洲政治碎片化的深層原因包括：一、經濟成長停滯與通膨壓力削弱傳統政黨承諾；二、移民議題與身份認同爭議加劇社會分裂；三、氣候政策與數位轉型議題上，傳統政黨立場模糊，難以回應年輕選民訴求。European Times 報導亦強調，民粹主義興起、氣候政策與數位創新是影響歐洲議會決策的關鍵問題，這些因素將塑造歐洲未來的治理方向。該報導指出，選民對傳統政治的不信任已轉化為對極右與極左政黨的支援，歐洲議會內部政治光譜持續多極化。
歐盟制度層面，根據臺灣經濟部國際貿易局資料，里斯本條約於2009年12月1日生效後，廢止各國部長輪值歐盟理事會主席的成例，改由理事會選出常任主席，任期兩年半，得連任一次，並直接向歐洲議會負責；另設「歐盟外交暨安全政策高階代表」，強化歐盟對外行動的一致性。然而，制度設計並未完全解決政治碎片化帶來的治理挑戰。
維基百科資料顯示，歐盟為27個國家組成的政府間與超國家政治聯盟，擁有單一市場、關稅同盟、單一貨幣（截至2008年已被28個成員國中的15國採納）、共同農業政策與共同漁業政策等重要機制，但政策協調難度隨成員國政治分歧擴大而上升。
綜合各來源觀察，歐洲政治碎片化趨勢短期內難以逆轉，傳統政黨亟需重塑與選民的連結，本土與激進政黨則需面對執政責任的考驗。歐盟機構與成員國政府如何在民粹壓力下維持政策連續性，將是未來幾年歐洲治理的核心課題。
