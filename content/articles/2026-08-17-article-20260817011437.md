  # China-Africa Space Cooperation Heats Up as African Space Industry Sees New Opportunities
  
  > The China-Africa Cooperation Forum has announced deeper space and nuclear technology cooperation, with multiple African countries advancing domestic space programs, as major powers compete for Africa's space market.
  
  Africa's space science and exploration sector is experiencing rapid development. According to public data, since Kenya launched its first satellite into orbit in 2018, Ethiopia, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, and Egypt have successively proposed domestic space programs, bringing a development boom to Africa's aerospace industry. This trend reflects African nations' emphasis on autonomous space capabilities and has attracted global powers to accelerate their space cooperation arrangements in Africa.
  
  In terms of international cooperation, China-Africa space cooperation has become a focal point. At the China-Africa Cooperation Forum summit held in Beijing on September 5, 2024, Chinese President Xi Jinping announced that China would deepen cooperation with Africa in nuclear technology and space exploration, stating that "no country should be left behind on the path to modernization." Xi further explained that China would establish a China-Africa Forum for the Peaceful Use of Nuclear Technology, co-build 30 joint laboratories, and promote specific cooperation projects in areas such as satellite remote sensing, lunar exploration, and deep space探測.
  
  In addition, public reports indicate that the Egyptian Satellite Laboratory began operations in 2023. This facility is regarded as part of China's overseas space program; over the past two years, China has donated a series of space technology equipment to Egypt, including satellites, space monitoring telescopes, and ground stations.
  
  The trend of major powers competing for Africa's space market is equally pronounced. According to data from the Belt and Road Database, between 2000 and 2023, African countries signed over 166 bilateral space agreements involving more than 100 institutions from 32 countries worldwide, with 89 of those agreements signed within the past three years, indicating rapid expansion in cooperation scale in recent years. Reports note that China, the United States, Russia, Japan, India, and the European Union have all accelerated their space cooperation with Africa, reflecting the fact that the space domain has become an important arena for major power diplomacy and technological competition.
  
  The academic community holds a positive view of Africa's potential in space development. An article published in the Eos journal pointed out that Africa has enormous potential in planetary and space science, yet it remains underdeveloped, and nurturing a new generation of scientists will have far-reaching impact. This perspective aligns with African nations' vision of promoting autonomous space development and also highlights that talent and infrastructure remain the primary challenges.
  
  Synthesizing reports from various media, Africa's space industry development exhibits multi-layered characteristics: on one hand, African countries are actively advancing satellite launches and space programs; on the other hand, major powers are deepening their space ties with Africa through technical assistance and cooperation agreements. Through the China-Africa Cooperation Forum platform, China has proposed a specific cooperation framework, including institutional arrangements such as a nuclear technology forum and joint laboratories, while the operation of the Egyptian Satellite Laboratory is seen as a key component of China's overseas space layout. Different sources offer varying interpretations of the nature of the cooperation—some reports describe China's space activities in Africa as a "secret overseas program," while others emphasize the openness and multilateral nature of the cooperation forum. This narrative divergence warrants continued observation.
  
  Overall, Africa's space science and exploration are in a period of rapid development, with domestic capacity building and international cooperation proceeding in parallel. The interplay between major power competition and Africa's autonomous development will continue to shape the evolution of the regional space landscape.

# 中非太空合作升溫 非洲航天事業迎戰新契機

> 中非合作論壇宣佈深化太空與核技術合作，非洲多國相繼推動本土太空計畫，大國競逐非洲太空市場態勢明顯。

非洲太空科學與探索領域正經歷快速發展階段。根據公開資料顯示，自2018年肯亞將首顆衛星送入軌道以來，衣索比亞、迦納、象牙海岸與埃及等國相繼提出本土太空計畫，非洲航天事業迎來發展高潮。此一趨勢反映非洲國家對自主太空能力的重視，也吸引全球大國加速在非洲的太空合作佈局。
在國際合作方面，中非太空合作成為焦點之一。2024年9月5日於北京舉行的中非合作論壇峰會上，中國國家主席習近平宣佈，中國將與非洲在核技術與太空探索領域深化合作，並表示「現代化道路上任何國家都不能掉隊」。習近平進一步說明，中國將建立中非和平利用核技術論壇，共建30個聯合實驗室，並在衛星遙感、月球與深空探測等領域推動具體合作專案。
此外，公開報導指出，埃及衛星實驗室已於2023年開始運營，此設施被視為中國海外太空計畫的一部分，中國過去兩年已向埃及贈送一系列太空技術裝置，包含衛星、太空監測望遠鏡與地面站等。
大國競逐非洲太空市場的趨勢同樣顯著。根據一帶一路資料庫資料，2000年至2023年間，非洲國家共簽署超過166項雙邊太空協議，涉及來自全球32個國家的100多家機構，其中89項協議是在過去三年內簽署，顯示近年合作規模快速擴張。報導指出，中美俄、日本、印度與歐盟等均已加快與非洲的太空合作步伐，反映出太空領域已成為大國外交與科技競爭的重要場域。
學術界對非洲太空發展的潛力持正面看法。Eos期刊刊載文章指出，非洲在行星與太空科學領域擁有巨大潛力，但至今仍未得到充分開發，培育新一代科學家將帶來深遠影響。此一觀點呼應非洲國家推動太空自主發展的願景，也點出當前人才與基礎建設仍是主要挑戰。
綜合各媒體報導，非洲太空事業的發展呈現多層次特徵：一方面，非洲本土國家積極推動衛星發射與太空計畫；另一方面，大國透過技術援助與合作協議加深與非洲的太空聯絡。中國方面透過中非合作論壇平臺提出具體合作框架，包括核技術論壇與聯合實驗室等機制性安排，而埃及衛星實驗室的運營則被視為中國海外太空佈局的重要一環。不同來源對合作性質的解讀存在差異，部分報導將中國在非洲的太空活動描述為「秘密海外計畫」，而其他來源則強調合作論壇的公開性與多邊性，此種敘事差異值得後續觀察。
整體而言，非洲太空科學與探索正處於快速發展期，本土能力建設與國際合作並行，大國競爭與非洲自主發展之間的互動將持續影響區域太空格局的演進。
