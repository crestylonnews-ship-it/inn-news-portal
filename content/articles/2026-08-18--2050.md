  # Climate Change Threatens West African Cocoa Production, May Become Luxury Good by 2050
  
  > Euronews reports that approximately 60% of the world's cocoa beans are produced in West Africa's Ivory Coast and Ghana. Climate change is driving rising temperatures and shifting rainfall patterns, and by 2050 production could drop to zero, potentially turning chocolate into a luxury item.
  
  According to Euronews, the global cocoa supply chain is facing severe challenges, primarily due to the impact of climate change in West Africa. The report states that approximately 60% of the world's cocoa beans are produced in West Africa, particularly in the Ivory Coast and Ghana, two countries that have long relied on hot, humid conditions with stable rainfall to support cocoa tree growth. However, rising temperatures and unstable rainfall patterns are already significantly affecting the producing regions. If current trends continue, cocoa production could cease entirely by 2050, and chocolate may transition from an everyday food to a luxury good. The report highlights the far-reaching impact of climate change on agricultural supply chains and raises concerns about global food security.
  
  Africa, the world's second-largest continent, covers approximately 30.2 million square kilometers—about one-fifth of the Earth's total land area. Bordered by the Indian Ocean to the east and the Atlantic Ocean to the west, with the equator running through its center, Africa's diverse terrain and climate zones nurture rich agricultural resources. According to relevant data, Africa's geographic advantages have made it a major producing region for cash crops such as cocoa and coffee. However, climate change is now threatening these industries that depend on stable environmental conditions. West Africa's cocoa-growing regions are particularly vulnerable, as local farmers largely lack irrigation facilities and adaptation technologies, making them highly susceptible to droughts and heavy rainfall. Experts warn that if temperatures rise another 1 to 2 degrees Celsius, suitable cocoa-growing areas could shrink dramatically, leading to a sharp decline in production.
  
  The impact of climate change on the cocoa industry has drawn international attention, with multiple organizations and research institutions promoting adaptation strategies such as developing drought-resistant varieties, improving soil management, and promoting shade-grown cultivation. However, West African countries including the Ivory Coast and Ghana account for over 70% of global cocoa production, and their economies are heavily dependent on this industry, leaving significant gaps in funding and technology for the transition. Analysts suggest that chocolate prices may continue to rise, affecting consumer markets—particularly in low-income countries, where cocoa products will become increasingly unaffordable. Future observations will focus on whether international cooperation and policy interventions can effectively mitigate the threat of climate change to agricultural supply chains and ensure the sustainability of the global food system.

# 氣候變遷威脅西非可可產量 2050年或成奢侈品

> 歐洲新聞臺報導指出，全球約60%可可豆產自西非象牙海岸與迦納，氣候變遷導致高溫與降雨模式改變，2050年恐面臨產量歸零，巧克力或成奢侈品。

據《歐洲新聞臺》（Euronews）報導，全球可可供應鏈正面臨嚴峻挑戰，主要源於西非地區氣候變遷的衝擊。報導指出，全球大約60%的可可豆產自西非，尤其是象牙海岸與迦納，這兩國長期依賴高溫濕潤且降雨穩定的環境，支撐可可樹生長。然而，氣溫上升與降雨模式不穩定已對產區造成顯著影響，若趨勢持續，2050年恐無可可豆可供生產，巧克力可能從日常食品轉變為奢侈品。此報導凸顯氣候變遷對農業供應鏈的深遠影響，並引發對全球食品安全的關注。
非洲作為世界第二大洲，面積約3020萬平方公里，佔全球總陸地面積的五分之一，地理位置東臨印度洋、西瀕大西洋，赤道貫穿中部，多樣的地形與氣候帶孕育豐富的農業資源。根據相關資料，非洲的地理優勢使其成為可可、咖啡等經濟作物的主要產區，但氣候變遷正威脅這些依賴穩定環境的產業。西非地區的可可產區尤其脆弱，因當地農民多缺乏灌溉設施與適應技術，極易受乾旱或暴雨影響。專家指出，若氣溫再上升1至2攝氏度，可可適生區可能大幅縮減，導致產量銳減。
氣候變遷對可可產業的衝擊已引起國際關注，多個組織與研究機構正推動適應策略，例如培育耐旱品種、改善土壤管理與推廣遮蔭種植。然而，西非國家如象牙海岸與迦納佔全球可可產量超過七成，當地經濟高度依賴此產業，轉型過程面臨資金與技術缺口。有分析認為，巧克力價格可能持續上漲，影響消費市場，尤其對低收入國家而言，可可製品將更難負擔。後續觀察將聚焦於國際合作與政策介入，能否有效緩解氣候變遷對農業供應鏈的威脅，並確保全球食品系統的永續性。
