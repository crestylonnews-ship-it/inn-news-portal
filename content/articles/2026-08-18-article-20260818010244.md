  # Africa's Public Health System Faces Dual Disease Challenges, Significant Regional Disparities
  
  > Africa's public health system is in its early developmental stage from 2025 to 2030, with North Africa and sub-Saharan Africa facing markedly different disease burdens, and the African Union has established multiple anti-epidemic mechanisms to address these challenges.
  
  Africa's public health system is facing a complex situation where structural challenges and transformation opportunities coexist. According to the "2025-2030 Research on Improving Africa's Public Health Service System and Building a Disease Prevention and Control System" published on the docin platform, the region's public health system entered an early developmental stage from 2025 to 2030, facing multiple constraints such as uneven distribution of medical resources and weak infrastructure, while also being affected by complex factors including economic conditions, demographic structure, and environmental changes. Citing World Health Organization data, the study notes that as of 2023, Africa's allocation of medical resources per thousand population remained significantly below the global average, which has become a key bottleneck constraining the development of the public health system.
  
  The December 2021 issue of the International Monetary Fund's (IMF) "Finance & Development" magazine published an article titled "A New Public Health Order for Africa," which documented the African Union's rapid response mechanism following the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic. The article points out that less than six weeks after the first confirmed case appeared in Africa, the AU released an African anti-epidemic strategy and established multiple unprecedented anti-epidemic mechanisms, including the "Pan-African Accelerated COVID-19 Testing Partnership" launched in April 2020 by the AU Commission in collaboration with the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC). This mechanism aims to accelerate testing capacity building and enhance pandemic monitoring and response capabilities across the continent.
  
  Regional differences in disease profiles constitute another major challenge for Africa's public health. A study published on the ebiotrade website, titled "Comparative Analysis of Population and Epidemiological Transition in African Regions: Regional Differentiated Strategies for Public Health Policy," reveals that the burden of non-communicable diseases in North Africa has risen significantly, accounting for 83% of causes of death by 2019, while communicable diseases remain highly prevalent in sub-Saharan Africa.
  
  The study, based on an analysis of World Bank population data from 1960 to 2020 and Global Burden of Disease data from 1990 to 2019, emphasizes that different regions of Africa face fundamentally different public health challenges and require region-specific strategies to address the dual pressures of demographic transition and shifting disease profiles.
  
  A synthesis of multiple studies shows that the development trajectory of Africa's public health system exhibits clear regional heterogeneity. North African countries are relatively advanced in economic development and medical infrastructure, with non-communicable disease control becoming the primary task, while sub-Saharan countries still need to address the high burden of communicable diseases while gradually establishing chronic disease management systems. This dual challenge requires international public health cooperation to adopt differentiated strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all model.
  
  The anti-epidemic mechanisms established by the African Union have provided an institutional framework for public health governance across the continent, but uneven resource allocation and weak infrastructure remain key factors constraining the effectiveness of the system. Experts point out that the 2025 to 2030 period is an important window for consolidating the foundations of Africa's public health system, requiring sustained investment in medical resources, strengthened talent development, and the formulation of precision prevention and control strategies based on regional disease burden characteristics.

# 非洲公共衛生體系面臨雙重疾病挑戰 區域差異顯著

> 非洲公共衛生體系在2025至2030年間處於早期發展階段，北非與撒哈拉以南地區面臨截然不同的疾病負擔，非盟已建立多項抗疫機制應對挑戰。

非洲公共衛生體系正面臨結構性挑戰與轉型機遇並存的複雜局面。根據docin平臺發布的《2025-2030非洲公共衛生服務體系完善與疾病防控體系建設研究》指出，該地區公共衛生體系在2025年至2030年期間進入早期發展階段，面臨醫療資源分佈不均、基礎設施薄弱等多重限制因素，同時還受到經濟條件、人口結構、環境變化等複雜影響。該研究援引世界衛生組織資料顯示，截至2023年，非洲每千人口醫療資源配置仍顯著低於全球平均水準，這成為制約公共衛生體系發展的關鍵瓶頸。
國際貨幣基金組織（IMF）旗下《金融與發展》雜誌2021年12月號刊載的《非洲公共衛生新秩序》一文，記錄了非洲聯盟在新冠疫情爆發後的快速反應機制。文章指出，在非洲出現首例確診病例後不到六週，非盟便發布了非洲抗疫戰略，並建立多項前所未有的抗疫機制，包括由非盟委員會聯合非洲疾控中心於2020年4月啟動的「泛非加快新冠肺炎檢測夥伴關係」。該機制旨在加速檢測能力建設，提升大陸範圍內的疫情監測與應對水準。
疾病譜系的區域差異構成非洲公共衛生的另一項重大挑戰。ebiotrade網站刊登的《非洲地區人口與流行病學轉型比較分析：公共衛生政策的區域差異化策略》研究揭示，北非地區非傳染性疾病負擔顯著上升，2019年已佔死亡原因的83%，而撒哈拉以南地區傳染性疾病仍持續高發。
該研究基於1960年至2020年世界銀行人口資料及1990年至2019年全球疾病負擔資料分析得出，強調非洲不同區域面臨截然不同的公共衛生挑戰，需要制定區域精準化策略以應對人口結構轉型與疾病譜系變遷的雙重壓力。
綜合多項研究顯示，非洲公共衛生體系的發展軌跡呈現明顯的區域異質性。北非國家在經濟發展與醫療基礎設施方面相對領先，非傳染性疾病防控成為主要任務；而撒哈拉以南國家仍須應對傳染性疾病的高負擔，同時逐步建立慢性病患者管理體系。這種雙重挑戰要求國際公共衛生合作必須採取差異化策略，而非單一模式。
非盟建立的抗疫機制為大陸公共衛生治理提供了制度框架，但資源分配不均與基礎設施薄弱仍是制約體系效能發揮的關鍵因素。專家指出，2025至2030年階段是非洲公共衛生體系夯實基礎的重要視窗期，需要持續投入醫療資源、加強人才培養，並根據區域疾病負擔特徵制定精準防控策略。
