  # South Africa Promotes International Humanitarian Law as African Security Trends Draw Attention
  
  > South Africa urges African nations to support international humanitarian law, while 2025 African security trends reveal multiple challenges, drawing international attention to humanitarian aid and peacebuilding progress.
  
  On August 19, 2025, South Africa hosted a high-level advocacy event to mobilize African countries' commitment to international humanitarian law. The event brought together more than 30 African mission chiefs stationed in Pretoria, along with representatives from the founding member states of the Global Initiative for International Humanitarian Law, aiming to persuade African governments to strengthen their support for better implementation and compliance with international humanitarian law. The International Committee of the Red Cross reported that this initiative aims to raise awareness of civilian protection in armed conflicts across Africa and promote the integration of international humanitarian law into domestic legal systems.
  
  Africa's security landscape in 2025 faces multiple challenges. On December 17, 2025, the Africa Center for Strategic Studies (ACSS) published an in-depth article analyzing ten key security trends across Africa, pointing out that conflicts, extreme climate events, and economic instability continue to intensify humanitarian crises. Taiwan World Vision, citing statistics from the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), reported that nearly 300 million people worldwide required aid and protection in 2024, with Africa emerging as one of the regions with the most urgent humanitarian needs due to conflicts in the Sahel, drought in East Africa, and violence in southern Africa. The ICRC emphasized that while the implementation of international humanitarian law can mitigate the impact of armed conflict on civilians, some African countries still lack sufficient capacity for enforcement.
  
  On January 9, 2025, Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi stated at a press meeting in Abuja, Nigeria, that China will firmly support Africa's efforts to strengthen its peace and security capabilities, and proposed establishing a Global Security Initiative partnership and creating cooperation demonstration zones. Xinhua reported that China will provide 1 billion yuan in grant military assistance to African countries, training 6,000 military personnel and 1,000 police officers. This move is seen as an important step in China's implementation of the Global Security Initiative in Africa, though it remains to be seen whether there is complementarity or divergence between China's approach and South Africa's international humanitarian law initiative.
  
  Civil society cooperation projects such as the UN African Girls Code Initiative also demonstrate that the international community is supporting Africa's long-term development from multiple angles.

# 南非推動國際人道法 非洲安全域性勢引關注

> 南非敦促非洲各國支援國際人道法，同時2025年非洲安全趨勢顯示多重挑戰，國際社會關注人道援助與和平建設進展。

南非於2025年8月19日舉辦高階別倡議活動，動員非洲各國對國際人道法做出承諾。活動聚集30餘名駐比勒陀利亞的非洲使團團長，以及國際人道法全球倡議的創始成員國代表，旨在遊說非洲政府加強支援力度，以更好地實施和遵守國際人道法。紅十字國際委員會報導指出，此舉旨在提升非洲地區對武裝衝突中平民保護的意識，並促進各國將國際人道法納入國內法律體系。
2025年非洲安全域性勢面臨多重挑戰。美國非洲戰略研究中心（ACSS）於2025年12月17日刊發長文，剖析非洲安全十大趨勢，指出衝突、極端氣候與經濟動盪持續加劇人道危機。臺灣世界展望會援引聯合國人道事務協調廳（OCHA）統計，2024年全球近3億人需要援助與保護，其中非洲地區因薩赫勒地帶衝突、東非乾旱與南部非洲暴力事件，成為人道需求最迫切區域之一。紅十字國際委員會強調，國際人道法的落實能減輕武裝衝突對平民的影響，但非洲部分國家仍缺乏足夠執行能力。
中國外交部長王毅於2025年1月9日在奈及利亞阿布賈會見記者時表示，中國將堅定支援非洲加強和平安全能力建設，並提出建立全球安全倡議夥伴關係，打造合作示範區。新華社報導指出，中國將向非方提供10億元人民幣無償軍事援助，培訓6000名軍隊人才與1000名警務執法人員。此舉被視為中國推動全球安全倡議在非洲落地的重要步驟，但國際社會對其與南非倡議的國際人道法路徑之間是否存在互補或差異，尚待進一步觀察。
聯合國非洲女童編碼倡議等民間合作專案，亦顯示國際社會正從多面向支援非洲長遠發展。
