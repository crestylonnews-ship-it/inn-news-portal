  # Africa's Climate Risks Draw Attention as Solar Market and Policy Initiatives Advance in Parallel
  
  > The IPCC report highlights key climate risks facing Africa; the African solar market is gradually developing amid the energy transition, while the UN Economic Commission for Africa is advancing a climate policy strategy for 2025–2035.
  
  In Chapter 9 of its Sixth Assessment Report (Working Group II), the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) conducted an in-depth analysis of climate change impacts and adaptation strategies across the African region. The report identifies key risks facing Africa as potentially severe risks, a definition that builds on the framework of the Fifth Assessment Report (AR5) by linking "severity" to dangerous anthropogenic interference with the climate system. The IPCC emphasizes that preventing such interference constitutes the ultimate objective established under Article 2 of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC).
  
  The report notes that the process of identifying key risks in Africa involves multiple scientific assessments and regional expert input, aimed at providing policymakers with a concrete risk landscape to help African countries prioritize the most urgent climate adaptation issues.
  
  Running parallel to climate risk assessments, Africa's solar industry is in its early stages of development and is gradually emerging as a new investment market of international interest. According to analysis by InfoLink Consulting, compared to mature solar markets such as Europe, North America, and China, Africa has shown growth potential in recent years against the dual backdrop of energy transition and insufficient power infrastructure. Africa's current energy mix remains heavily reliant on natural gas, coal, hydropower, and petroleum, with North African countries such as Algeria and Egypt still primarily generating electricity from natural gas.
  
  InfoLink points out that the global energy transition trend presents opportunities for Africa's solar market, but infrastructure gaps remain the primary development obstacle, requiring international funding and technical support to accelerate the transition.
  
  On the policy front, the African Climate Policy Centre (ACPC), under the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa (UNECA), has released its 2025–2035 strategic plan. As a specialized UN institution in Africa, the centre is positioned as a continental knowledge hub and policy coordination platform, dedicated to supporting African countries in advancing climate resilience, low-carbon development, and sustainability. The ACPC strategy encompasses multiple dimensions, including cooperation with national governments, policy advocacy, and knowledge integration, aiming to strengthen Africa's autonomous capacity in climate governance.
  
  Synthesizing IPCC scientific assessments, industry market analysis, and the UN policy framework, Africa's climate change agenda is gradually shifting from risk awareness to concrete action, with solar industry development and policy mechanism building emerging as the two key focal points. Future observations will focus on the scale of international funding commitments, the pace of infrastructure progress, and the effectiveness of policy implementation across countries.

# 非洲氣候風險受矚目 太陽能市場與政策佈局同步推進

> 聯合國政府間氣候變遷專門委員會（IPCC）報告指出非洲面臨關鍵氣候風險；非洲太陽能市場在能源轉型背景下逐步發展，聯合國非洲經濟委員會亦推動 2025 至 2035 年氣候政策策略。

聯合國政府間氣候變遷專門委員會（IPCC）於《第六次評估報告》第二工作群組第九章中，針對非洲地區進行氣候變遷影響與適應策略的深入分析。報告指出，非洲面臨的關鍵風險（key risks）被定義為潛在嚴重風險，此定義延續第五次評估報告（AR5）的框架，將「嚴重性」與危險的人為氣候系統幹擾相互關聯。IPCC 強調，防止此類幹擾乃是《聯合國氣候變遷綱要公約》（UNFCCC）第二條所確立的最終目標。
報告提及，識別非洲關鍵風險的過程涉及多項科學評估與區域專家意見，旨在為政策制定者提供具體風險圖譜，以協助非洲各國優先處理最迫切的氣候適應議題。
與氣候風險評估同步，非洲太陽能產業正處於發展初期階段，並逐步成為國際關注的新興投資市場。根據 InfoLink Consulting 分析，相較於歐洲、北美與中國等成熟太陽能市場，非洲在能源轉型與電力基礎建設不足的雙重背景下，近年已展現成長潛力。目前非洲能源結構仍高度依賴天然氣、煤炭、水力與石油，其中北非國家如阿爾及利亞與埃及仍以天然氣發電為主。
InfoLink 指出，全球能源轉型趨勢為非洲太陽能市場帶來契機，但基礎建設缺口仍是主要發展障礙，需仰賴國際資金與技術支援方能加速轉型。
在政策層面，聯合國非洲經濟委員會（UNECA）下屬的非洲氣候政策中心（ACPC）已公佈 2025 至 2035 年計畫策略。該中心作為聯合國在非洲的專業機構，定位為大陸級知識樞紐與政策協調平臺，致力於支援非洲各國推進氣候韌性、低碳與可持續發展。ACPC 策略涵蓋與各國政府合作、政策倡導與知識整合等多項面向，旨在強化非洲在氣候治理中的自主能力。
綜合 IPCC 科學評估、產業市場分析與聯合國政策框架，非洲氣候變遷議題正從風險認知逐步轉向具體行動階段，太陽能產業發展與政策機制建構成為當前兩大焦點。後續觀察將聚焦於國際資金投入規模、基礎建設進展速度，以及各國政策落實成效。
