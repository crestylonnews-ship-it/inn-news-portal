  # African Space Agency Unveiled as Lunar Exploration and Regional Cooperation Advance
  
  > The African Space Agency (AfSA), headquartered in Cairo, Egypt, has been officially established. The Smart Africa Alliance is driving satellite network cooperation among 30 nations, while the Africa2Moon project is expected to launch alongside China's Chang'e-8 mission in 2029, marking a new milestone in Africa's space exploration.
  
  The field of African space exploration is undergoing a structural turning point. According to a report in the journal *Science*, African countries are actively promoting the development of their space industries. The Smart Africa Alliance has already brought together 30 African nations, committed to enhancing satellite network connectivity. Meanwhile, the African Space Agency (AfSA)—the first space agency with jurisdiction over the entire African continent—was recently officially unveiled, with its headquarters located at the Cairo Space City in Egypt. This marks a step toward integrated space governance in Africa and is seen as a key move from fragmentation toward coordination, and from terrestrial applications to deep-space exploration.
  
  Multiple media reports confirm that the establishment of the African Space Agency (AfSA) is an important milestone for African space cooperation. According to the analysis in *Science*, space cooperation within Africa is steadily strengthening, and the formation of the Smart Africa Alliance is a concrete manifestation of this trend. The alliance aims to narrow the digital divide through public-private partnerships while opening doors for African nations to participate in the space market.
  
  Additionally, *Global Times* cited an interview with the Africa2Moon project's African負責人, who stated that the mission is expected to launch around 2029 alongside China's Chang'e-8 mission. Its goal is to conduct exploration near the Leibnitz-Beta Plateau at the Moon's south pole, laying the foundation for the construction of an International Lunar Research Station.
  
  However, existing reports have not provided complete details on the specific coordination mechanisms between the African Space Agency and the Smart Africa Alliance. *Science* emphasizes progress in regional cooperation but does not clarify the organizational relationship and division of responsibilities between AfSA and Smart Africa. Furthermore, information from different sources varies regarding the specific timeline, vehicle specifications, and the actual participation ratio of African nations in the Africa2Moon project. The launch schedule for Chang'e-8 and its lunar south pole exploration objectives currently come primarily from Chinese media reports, while specific coordination details from the African side remain to be officially confirmed.
  
  The establishment of the African Space Agency will have a profound impact on the regional space industry and policy. Through a unified space governance framework, African nations can integrate resources, reduce duplicated investment, and enhance their voice in international space affairs. The satellite network construction promoted by the Smart Africa Alliance will not only improve connectivity within Africa but also help advance education, healthcare, and economic development. Moreover, the advancement of the Africa2Moon project symbolizes Africa's first participation in a lunar exploration mission, which will inspire the younger generation to pursue careers in science and engineering and provide long-term momentum for cultivating Africa's space industry chain.
  
  Despite the optimistic outlook for African space cooperation, scholars caution against overinterpreting current progress. *Science* notes that Africa's space industry still faces structural challenges such as funding shortages and a lack of technical talent; while regional cooperation is strong, its implementation will require time to bear fruit. Additionally, the construction of the International Lunar Research Station involves multi-country collaboration, and the actual level of participation and contribution from African nations remains to be observed. Some analysts argue that Africa's space development must carefully balance autonomy and international cooperation, avoiding becoming a subordinate market for other countries' technologies and missions, while ensuring that the benefits of space are equitably distributed among all member states.
  
  Future areas of interest include the actual operational effectiveness and funding sources of the African Space Agency, the satellite deployment progress of the Smart Africa Alliance, and whether the Africa2Moon project can launch as scheduled in 2029 alongside the Chang'e-8 mission. Additionally, how African nations coordinate domestic policies and establish unified space regulations will affect the depth and breadth of regional cooperation. The international community will also observe Africa's positions and modes of participation on issues such as lunar exploration and space resource utilization, as well as whether the African Space Agency can present an actionable overall space strategy blueprint within the next five years.

# 非洲航天局揭牌 探月計畫與區域合作並進

> 非洲航天局總部位於埃及開羅揭牌成立，Smart Africa聯盟推動30國衛星網路合作，Africa2Moon計畫預計隨嫦娥八號於2029年登月，展現非洲太空探索新里程碑。

非洲太空探索領域迎來結構性轉折。據《科學》（Science）期刊報導，非洲各國正積極推動航天產業發展，其中Smart Africa聯盟已匯聚30個非洲國家，致力於提升衛星網路連線能力。同時，首個管轄整個非洲大陸的航天機構——非洲航天局（AfSA）近日正式揭牌成立，總部設於埃及開羅太空城，標誌著非洲太空治理邁向整合階段。此舉被視為非洲從分散走向協調、從地面應用延伸至深空探索的關鍵一步。
多家媒體報導確認，非洲航天局（AfSA）的成立是非洲太空合作的重要里程碑。根據《科學》期刊的分析，非洲內部的航天合作正穩步加強，Smart Africa聯盟的成立正是這一趨勢的具體體現。該聯盟旨在透過公私部門合作，縮小數位落差，同時開啟非洲國家參與太空市場的門戶。
此外，《環球時報》轉述Africa2Moon專案非洲負責人受訪內容指出，該計畫預計於2029年前後隨中國嫦娥八號任務發射，目標在月球南極附近的萊布尼茨-貝塔高原開展探索，並為國際月球科研站建設奠定基礎。
然而，關於非洲航天局與Smart Africa聯盟之間的具體協調機制，現有報導尚未提供完整細節。《科學》期刊強調區域合作的進展，但未說明AfSA與Smart Africa的組織關係與權責劃分；而關於Africa2Moon計畫的具體執行時程、載具規格與非洲國家的實際參與比例，不同來源的資訊亦存在差異。嫦娥八號任務的發射時間表與月球南極探測目標，目前主要來自中方媒體報導，非洲側的具體配合細節仍有待官方進一步確認。
非洲航天局的成立對區域航天產業與政策具有深遠影響。透過統一的航天治理架構，非洲國家有望整合資源、減少重複投資，並提升在國際航天事務中的話語權。Smart Africa聯盟推動的衛星網路建設，不僅能改善非洲內部的連線品質，也有助於促進教育、醫療與經濟發展。此外，Africa2Moon計畫的推進，象徵非洲首次參與月球探索任務，將激勵年輕世代投入科學與工程領域，並為非洲航天產業鏈的培育提供長期動能。
儘管非洲太空合作前景樂觀，但學者提醒應避免過度解讀現有進展。《科學》期刊指出，非洲航天產業仍面臨資金短缺、技術人才不足等結構性挑戰，區域合作雖強勁但落實層面仍需時間檢驗。此外，國際月球科研站的建設涉及多國合作，非洲國家的實際參與程度與貢獻比例，仍有待後續觀察。部分分析認為，非洲太空發展需謹慎平衡自主性與國際合作，避免淪為他國技術與任務的附屬市場，同時應確保太空紅利能公平分配至各會員國。
未來值得關注的面向包括：非洲航天局的實際運作效能與資金來源、Smart Africa聯盟的衛星部署進度，以及Africa2Moon計畫能否如期於2029年隨嫦娥八號任務發射。此外，非洲國家如何協調各國內部政策、建立統一的航天法規，亦將影響區域合作的深度與廣度。國際社會亦將觀察非洲在月球探索與太空資源利用等議題上的立場與參與方式，以及非洲航天局能否在未來五年內提出具執行力的整體太空戰略藍圖。
