  On March 6, the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) announced that the situation in the Middle East has rapidly deteriorated due to US and Israeli strikes on Iran, constituting a "major humanitarian emergency." Ayumi Ito, Director of Emergencies and Cross-Border Refugee Coordinator at UNHCR, held a press conference in Geneva, stating that the recent escalation of hostilities and attacks have triggered a large-scale displacement crisis. She called on all parties to the conflict to exercise restraint, seek de-escalation, and provide safe passage for affected civilians. This marks a new height in the UN's grave concern over the humanitarian situation in the Middle East.
  
  According to the Middle East and North Africa regional overview in UNHCR's "2025 Global Report," the region is facing overlapping political, security, and socio-economic pressures, with humanitarian needs continuing to deepen and the operational environment constantly reshaping. The report notes that armed conflict, fragile governance, inflation, and economic contraction have significantly increased the vulnerability of refugees, asylum seekers, internally displaced persons, and stateless individuals. UNHCR's declaration echoes the long-term regional trends revealed by the report, indicating that the current situation is not merely a short-term escalation of conflict but a concentrated outbreak of long-term structural crises.
  
  However, different sources still differ on the specific scale and scope of the crisis. While UNHCR emphasizes the characterization of a "major humanitarian emergency," the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) in its official statement only generally referred to "protecting life and dignity in conflict areas," without directly commenting on the specific severity of the current Middle East situation. The International Organization for Migration (IOM) crisis response platform focuses on resource allocation strategies, emphasizing directing limited resources to areas of greatest need and impact, but does not explicitly state whether the Middle East has met its priority response criteria.
  
  These differences in wording reflect that different humanitarian organizations may employ different benchmarks and judgment criteria when assessing the severity of crises, and the public should interpret each organization's public statements with caution.
  
  For the Middle East region, the direct impact of this humanitarian crisis has already manifested across multiple dimensions. UNHCR's report indicates that armed conflict and fragile governance have severely impacted the living conditions of local civilians, while inflation and economic contraction have further exacerbated the plight of refugees and displaced persons. Ito particularly emphasized the necessity of providing safe passage for civilians during the press conference, indicating that the current crisis extends beyond the conflict zone and may spill over into neighboring countries and regional stability. Additionally, UNHCR's cross-regional coordination mechanism has been activated, meaning the scope of humanitarian aid may transcend single national borders, involving a broader Middle East and North Africa region.
  
  Different perspectives also exist on how to address the current crisis. UNHCR advocates de-escalating hostilities among conflicting parties through diplomatic channels and stresses the urgency of ensuring safe passage for civilians; the International Committee of the Red Cross adheres to its principles of neutrality, impartiality, and independence in humanitarian action, focusing on directly providing aid and protection in conflict areas. The IOM's crisis response plan reflects another approach, maximizing humanitarian impact through strategic resource allocation. These different orientations are not contradictory but rather reflect differences in each organization's mandate and operational philosophy. Experts caution that the public should not overinterpret a single organization's statement as a complete assessment of the overall situation, and should instead consider multiple sources comprehensively.

Finally, whether the international community can achieve de-escalation through diplomatic channels to prevent further deterioration of the humanitarian crisis. These developments will determine the trajectory and ultimate scope of the current crisis.

# 聯合國宣佈中東陷重大人道危機 籲保障平民安全通道

> 聯合國難民署宣佈因美以襲擊伊朗而急遽惡化的中東局勢已構成「重大人道主義緊急狀態」，呼籲衝突各方保持冷靜、尋求降級，並為流離失所的平民提供安全通道。

聯合國難民署（UNHCR）於3月6日宣佈，中東地區局勢因美國與以色列對伊朗的襲擊而急遽惡化，已構成「重大人道主義緊急狀態」。聯合國難民署緊急情況主任兼跨區域難民協調員伊藤綾木在日內瓦舉行記者會時表示，近期敵意升級與襲擊已引發大規模流離失所危機，呼籲衝突各方保持冷靜、尋求降級，並為受影響平民提供安全通道。此舉標誌著聯合國對中東人道局勢的嚴重關切已達到新高度。
根據聯合國難民署發布的《2025年全球報告》中東與北非區域概覽，該地區正面臨相互交疊的政治、安全與社會經濟壓力，人道需求持續加深，營運環境也不斷重塑。報告指出，武裝衝突、治理脆弱、通貨膨脹與經濟收縮等因素，已使難民、庇護申請人、國內流離失所者及無國籍人士的脆弱性顯著升高。聯合國難民署的這項宣佈，與報告所揭示的區域性長期趨勢相互呼應，顯示當前局勢不僅是短期衝突升級，更是長期結構性危機的集中爆發。
然而，不同來源對危機的具體規模與影響範圍仍存有差異。聯合國難民署強調「重大人道主義緊急狀態」的定性，但國際紅十字會（ICRC）在其官方說明中僅概括提及「在衝突地區保護生命與尊嚴」，並未直接評論當前中東局勢的具體程度。國際移民組織（IOM）的危機響應平臺則側重於資源配置策略，強調將有限資源導向需求最迫切、影響最大的地區，但未明確指出中東是否已達到其優先響應標準。
這種表述上的差異，反映出不同人道組織在評估危機嚴重性時可能採用不同的基準與判斷標準，外界應謹慎解讀各組織的公開宣告。
對中東地區而言，此一人道危機的直接影響已顯現於多個層面。聯合國難民署報告指出，武裝衝突與治理脆弱已嚴重衝擊當地平民的生存條件，而通貨膨脹與經濟收縮更進一步加劇了難民與流離失所者的困境。伊藤綾木在記者會中特別強調為平民提供安全通道的必要性，顯示當前危機已不僅限於戰區內部，更可能波及鄰國與區域穩定。此外，聯合國難民署的跨區域協調機制已被啟動，意味著人道援助的範圍可能超越單一國家邊界，涉及更廣泛的中東與北非地區。
不同觀點對如何應對當前危機也存在分歧。聯合國難民署主張透過外交途徑促使衝突各方降級敵意，並強調保障平民安全通道的緊迫性；國際紅十字會則堅持其中立、公正與獨立的人道行動原則，側重於在衝突地區直接提供援助與保護。國際移民組織的危機響應計劃則體現了另一種思路，即透過策略性資源分配來最大化人道影響。這些不同取向並非相互矛盾，而是反映各組織在授權範圍與行動哲學上的差異。專家提醒，外界不應過度解讀單一組織的宣告為對整體局勢的完整評估，而應綜合考量多方來源。
後續觀察將聚焦於幾個關鍵面向。首先，聯合國難民署所宣佈的「重大人道主義緊急狀態」是否會觸發更大規模的國際人道援助行動，以及相關資金與資源能否及時到位。其次，衝突各方是否會回應聯合國呼籲，採取實際措施保障平民安全通道與基本人權。第三，中東地區鄰國將如何應對可能擴大的流離失所潮，特別是對區域穩定的潛在衝擊。最後，國際社會能否透過外交途徑促成局勢降級，避免人道危機進一步惡化。這些發展將決定當前危機的走向與最終影響範圍。
