  # NASA Releases New Images of 3I/ATLAS Comet, Dismisses Alien Spaceship Rumors
  
  > NASA released new images of 3I/ATLAS, the third interstellar object ever discovered, confirming it as an ancient comet and dismissing rumors of alien technology; the object is approximately 275 million kilometers from Earth at its closest approach and poses no threat.
  
  The U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) released the latest images of 3I/ATLAS, the third confirmed interstellar object, in late November 2025, and formally dismissed speculation that the object is an alien spacecraft. 3I/ATLAS was first discovered in July 2025 by the ATLAS telescope in Chile. Astronomers have confirmed it is a comet, possibly older than the Solar System itself. NASA officials emphasized during a press briefing that all observational evidence points to a natural cometary nature, with no technological signatures detected.
  
  According to cross-referencing by multiple media outlets, the images released by NASA in this instance come from several space mission platforms, including the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, MAVEN (Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution), the Perseverance Mars rover, and the Psyche and Lucy missions.
  
  NASA Associate Administrator for Science Mission Directorate Nicola Fox and senior scientist Amit Kshatriya held a press briefing in Greenbelt, Maryland, confirming that 3I/ATLAS's appearance and behavior are consistent with a typical comet, and noting that the object has been observed by more than a dozen scientific platforms, including the Hubble Space Telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope.
  
  Regarding the closest approach distance of 3I/ATLAS, different sources present slightly varying figures. The Hawaii Tribune-Herald, citing NASA data, reported the object's closest distance to Earth as approximately 170 million miles, while CGTN (China Global Television Network) reported it as approximately 275 million kilometers — the two figures are similar but expressed in different units. Additionally, all sources consistently confirm that 3I/ATLAS is the "third confirmed interstellar object," though some media emphasized its scientific value lies in providing a rare opportunity to study residual material from ancient regions of the Milky Way. No independent third-party research team has yet questioned NASA's conclusions, but related scientific analyses are still underway.
  
  The discovery and study of 3I/ATLAS carry clear scientific significance for the astronomy community. As the third confirmed interstellar object, following 'Oumuamua in 2017 and 2I/Borisov in 2019, it provides astronomers with additional samples to better understand the composition and origins of interstellar objects. NASA officials noted that such objects may originate from other stellar systems, and studying their composition could help shed light on planet formation processes in other regions of the galaxy. Furthermore, the multi-platform observations in this case have validated the application potential of existing space assets in the study of distant interstellar objects.
  
  From a different perspective, some media reports have highlighted NASA's stance in "dismissing alien spaceship rumors," reflecting continued public interest in extraterrestrial hypotheses regarding such interstellar objects. However, NASA officials have clearly stated that all observational data support a natural cometary explanation, with no anomalous technological signals found. The scientific community broadly considers interpretations of 3I/ATLAS as alien technology to lack empirical basis, but the spread of such rumors also underscores the importance of public science literacy education. Meanwhile, some scholars have cautioned against overinterpreting preliminary conclusions from a single source before complete scientific analysis is finished.
  
  Key areas for follow-up observation include whether NASA will release more detailed spectral data and orbital parameters for 3I/ATLAS; whether the international astronomy community will organize further joint observation efforts; and whether chemical composition analyses of the object will reveal clues about its origin from a specific stellar system. Additionally, as 3I/ATLAS continues to move away from the Solar System, the observation window will gradually narrow, and the scientific community must complete critical measurements within a limited timeframe.

# NASA發布3I/ATLAS彗星新影像，否認外星飛船傳言

> NASA發布第三顆被發現的星際天體3I/ATLAS彗星新影像，確認其為古老彗星，否認外星技術傳言；該天體最近距離地球約2.75億公里，對地球無威脅。

美國國家航空暨太空總署（NASA）於2025年11月下旬發布第三顆被確認的星際天體3I/ATLAS之最新影像，並正式否認有關該天體為外星飛船的猜測。3I/ATLAS於2025年7月由位於智利的ATLAS望遠鏡首次發現，天文學家確認其為一顆彗星，且可能比太陽系更為古老。NASA官員在新聞簡報中強調，所有觀測證據均指向自然彗星本質，未偵測到任何技術特徵。
根據多家媒體交叉比對，NASA此次發布的影像來自多個太空任務平臺，包括火星軌道探測器「火星勘測軌道飛行器」（Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter）、「火星大氣與揮發物演化任務」（MAVEN）、「毅力號」火星車，以及「靈神星號」（Psyche）與「露西號」（Lucy）任務。
NASA科學任務部門副署長Nicola Fox與資深科學家Amit Kshatriya於馬裡蘭州格林貝特舉行簡報會，確認3I/ATLAS的外觀與行為與典型彗星一致，並指出該天體已被哈勃太空望遠鏡、詹姆斯·韋伯太空望遠鏡等超過十二個科學平臺觀測。
關於3I/ATLAS的最近距離，不同來源的資料略有差異。夏威夷論壇報引用NASA資料指出，該天體距地球最近約1.7億英里；而中國環球電視網（CGTN）則報導為約2.75億公里，兩者數值相近但表述單位不同。此外，關於3I/ATLAS是否為「第三顆被確認的星際天體」，所有來源均一致確認，但部分媒體強調其科學價值在於提供研究銀河系古老區域殘餘物質的罕見機會。目前尚無獨立第三方研究團隊對NASA的結論提出質疑，但相關科學分析仍在進行中。
3I/ATLAS的發現與研究對天文學界具有明確的科學意義。該天體作為第三顆被確認的星際天體，繼2017年的『Oumuamua與2019年的2I/Borisov之後，為天文學家提供了更多樣本以理解星際天體的組成與起源。NASA官員指出，此類天體可能源自其他恆星系統，研究其成分有助於推測銀河系其他區域的行星形成過程。此外，此次多平臺觀測也驗證了現有空勤裝置在遠距離星際天體研究中的應用潛力。
不同觀點方面，部分媒體報導強調NASA「否認外星飛船傳言」的立場，反映出公眾對此類星際天體的外星假說仍高度關注。然而，NASA官員明確表示，所有觀測資料均支援自然彗星解釋，未發現任何異常技術訊號。科學界普遍認為，將3I/ATLAS解讀為外星技術的說法缺乏實證基礎，但此類傳言的流傳也凸顯了公眾科學素養教育的重要性。同時，也有學者提醒，在完整科學分析完成前，不應過度解讀單一來源的初步結論。
後續觀察重點包括：NASA是否將發布更多3I/ATLAS的詳細光譜資料與軌道引數；國際天文學界是否會組織進一步的聯合觀測行動；以及該天體的化學組成分析結果是否會揭示其來自特定恆星系統的線索。此外，隨著3I/ATLAS持續遠離太陽系，觀測視窗將逐漸縮小，科學界需在有限時間內完成關鍵測量。
