  # Middle East's Persian Gulf Rises as AI Hub; Australia's Critical Minerals Alliance Reshapes Global Tech Supply Chain
  
  > TechNews reports the Middle East's Persian Gulf region has become a new focal point for AI development, with G42 funding a U.S. data center; UNCTAD report explores inclusive AI to address global challenges; globaltechmap highlights Australia's participation in a critical minerals strategic alliance, impacting AI hardware supply chains.
  
  According to TechNews, the U.S.-China AI competition landscape is extending into the Middle East, with Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries leveraging substantial funding and policy willingness to actively develop their AI industry chains, positioning them as potential emerging AI development hubs. The report notes that in March this year, the UAE-based company G42 announced it would fund a large data center within the United States, utilizing hardware from American chip company Cerebras. This move symbolizes a cross-border integration of Middle Eastern capital and American technology, and reflects regional nations' attempts to enhance AI computing capacity through infrastructure investment.
  
  Meanwhile, the UNCTAD's "2025 Technology and Innovation Report" focuses on inclusive artificial intelligence, exploring how AI can assist in addressing global challenges and promoting sustainable development. Although the report does not dedicate a chapter to the Middle East or Oceania, it provides an international framework reference for regional AI policy formulation.
  
  Cross-referencing multiple sources reveals a potential connection between Middle East AI development and Oceania's critical minerals supply chain. TechNews explicitly documents G42's data center investment, confirming that Gulf businesses have substantively participated in U.S. AI infrastructure; globaltechmap reports that 20 countries and organizations, including the United States, European Union, Japan, and Australia, plan to establish a critical minerals strategic alliance and simultaneously launch a "Vault Initiative" to invest $12 billion in strategic reserves. As the largest mineral exporter in Oceania, Australia's lithium, cobalt, and rare earth resources directly affect AI chip and battery manufacturing.
  
  While the UNCTAD report does not cite specific country cases, it emphasizes that inclusive AI must combine resource allocation with technology accessibility, implicitly echoing the equity issues surrounding critical minerals supply chains.
  
  Significant gaps and unconfirmed details exist across the sources. TechNews focuses solely on Middle Eastern capital flowing into U.S. data centers, without explaining the development progress or policy support for local AI industry chains in the Gulf; globaltechmap lists Australia's participation in the critical minerals alliance but does not clearly establish a direct link to the AI industry, nor does it provide data on Australia's mineral exports or corporate investment details. The UNCTAD report is a global analysis and offers no specific recommendations or data for the Middle East or Oceania. Furthermore, none of the three sources mention the roles of New Zealand and Pacific island nations in AI or critical minerals; the Oceania scope can only be inferred from Australia's participation, awaiting further verification.
  
  This issue has substantive implications for both the Middle East and Oceania. Gulf countries are accelerating AI computing capacity deployment through capital outflows, which may drive local digital economy transformation and employment restructuring, but over-reliance on overseas technology cooperation could constrain autonomous innovation capacity. On the Oceania front, Australia's position in the critical minerals supply chain will affect global AI hardware costs and stability; if the strategic alliance materializes, Australian mining enterprises may welcome new investment opportunities, though controversies surrounding environmental protection and Indigenous rights remain to be balanced. The inclusive AI framework advocated by UNCTAD, if implemented in regional policies, could help reduce technological development gaps, but the report has not yet been translated into concrete action plans.
  
  Different perspectives and limitations warrant attention. TechNews, under the headline "The Persian Gulf Will Become Another AI Development Hub," implies a Middle Eastern rise trend, but a single media report does not cover local academic or public concerns about AI development; globaltechmap positions the critical minerals alliance as "repairing Western ally partnerships," reflecting geopolitical considerations, but does not assess the long-term ecological impact of mineral extraction on Oceania. The UNCTAD report emphasizes AI's inclusive potential, but as a policy recommendation document, it lacks enforcement mechanisms and funding commitments.
  
  Overall, the existing materials do not provide direct comparative data on AI industries in the Middle East and Oceania, nor do they explain interactions between the two regions in terms of technology standards, regulatory frameworks, or talent mobility; interpretations should avoid overreach.
  
  Key areas for future observation include: when the U.S. data center funded by G42 will be completed and operational, and whether Gulf countries will simultaneously introduce local AI industry policies; whether the specific member list and reserve scale of Australia's critical minerals alliance will be announced; and whether UNCTAD will release regional supplementary research on the Middle East or Oceania following its report. Additionally, the roles of New Zealand and Pacific island nations in AI governance and mineral supply chains, as well as the impact of U.S.-China tech competition on regional cooperation models, are all topics worth tracking.

# 中東波灣躍升AI重地 澳洲關鍵礦產聯盟牽動全球科技供應鏈

> TechNews報導中東波灣地區成為AI發展新重地，G42資助美國資料中心；UNCTAD報告探討包容性AI應對全球挑戰；globaltechmap指出澳洲參與關鍵礦產戰略聯盟，影響AI硬體供應鏈。

根據TechNews科技新報報導，美中AI競爭格局正延伸至中東地區，波灣國家憑藉雄厚資金與政策意願，積極佈局人工智慧產業鏈，被視為潛在的新興AI發展重地。該報導指出，今年3月阿聯酋企業G42宣佈將資助美國境內一座大型資料中心，並採用美國晶片公司Cerebras的硬體裝置，此舉象徵中東資本與美國技術的跨國結合，也反映區域國家試圖透過基礎設施投資提升AI算力規模。
同期，UNCTAD發布的《2025年技術與創新報告》聚焦包容性人工智慧，探討AI如何協助應對全球性挑戰並促進永續發展，報告雖未專章論述中東或大洋洲，但為區域AI政策制定提供國際框架參考。
多來源交叉比對顯示，中東AI發展與大洋洲關鍵礦產供應鏈存在潛在關聯。TechNews明確記載G42的資料中心投資案，確認波灣企業已實質參與美國AI基礎建設；globaltechmap則指出，美國、歐盟、日本、澳洲等20個國家或組織計畫建立關鍵礦產戰略聯盟，並同步推出「金庫計劃」擬投入120億美元建立戰略儲備，其中澳洲作為大洋洲最大礦產出口國，其鋰、鈷、稀土等資源直接影響AI晶片與電池製造。
UNCTAD報告雖未提及具體國家案例，但強調包容性AI需結合資源分配與技術普及，與關鍵礦產供應鏈的公平性議題隱含呼應。
各來源資訊存在明顯落差與未確認細節。TechNews僅聚焦中東資金流向美國資料中心，未說明波灣本地AI產業鏈發展進度或政策配套；globaltechmap列舉澳洲參與關鍵礦產聯盟，但未明確指出該聯盟與AI產業的直接連結，亦未提供澳洲礦產出口資料或企業投資細節。UNCTAD報告為全球性分析，未針對中東或大洋洲地區提出具體建議或資料。此外，三則素材均未提及紐西蘭與太平洋島國在AI或關鍵礦產領域的角色，大洋洲範疇僅能從澳洲參與情況推論，尚待進一步查證。
此議題對中東與大洋洲地區具實質影響。中東波灣國家透過資本輸出加速AI算力佈局，可能帶動當地數位經濟轉型與就業結構調整，但過度依賴海外技術合作亦可能限制自主創新能力。大洋洲方面，澳洲關鍵礦產供應鏈地位將影響全球AI硬體成本與穩定性，若戰略聯盟落實，澳洲礦業企業或迎來新投資契機，惟環境保護與原住民權益等爭議仍待平衡。UNCTAD所倡導的包容性AI框架，若能在區域政策中落實，有助減少技術發展落差，但報告尚未轉化為具體行動計畫。
不同觀點與限制需注意。TechNews以「波灣將成另一AI發展重地」為題，暗示中東崛起趨勢，但單一媒體報導未涵蓋當地學界或民間對AI發展的疑慮；globaltechmap將關鍵礦產聯盟定位為「修復西方盟友夥伴關係」，反映地緣政治考量，但未評估礦產開採對大洋洲生態的長期衝擊。UNCTAD報告強調AI的包容性潛力，但報告性質為政策建議，缺乏執行機制與資金承諾。
整體而言，現有素材未提供中東與大洋洲AI產業的直接比較資料，亦未說明兩地區在技術標準、監管框架或人才流動上的互動，解讀時應避免過度推論。
後續觀察重點包括：G42資助的美國資料中心何時完工營運、波灣國家是否同步推出本土AI產業政策；澳洲關鍵礦產聯盟的具體成員名單與儲備規模是否公佈；UNCTAD報告後續是否針對中東或大洋洲發布區域補充研究。此外，紐西蘭與太平洋島國在AI治理與礦產供應鏈中的角色，以及中美科技競爭對區域合作模式的影響，均為值得追蹤的議題。
