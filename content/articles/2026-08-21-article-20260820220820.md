  # EU Releases Climate and Energy Strategy as Multiple Nations Face Extreme Climate Risks
  
  > The European Commission released its global climate and energy strategy in October 2025, while the first European Climate Risk Assessment report indicates that 36 climate threats have reached critical levels, with experts warning that insufficient grid capacity could hinder AI industry development.
  
  On October 16, 2025, the European Commission released the "EU Global Climate and Energy Vision," proposing a series of action plans in the fields of climate and energy, aimed at promoting the transition to clean energy and enhancing the international competitiveness of industries. On the same day, the first "European Climate Risk Assessment" (EUCRA) report pointed out that Europe is facing 36 climate risks, many of which have reached critical levels, seriously threatening energy and food security, ecosystems, infrastructure, water resources, financial systems, and public health.
  
  The release of these two important documents marks a key step forward for the EU in climate and energy policy, and also highlights the severe climate challenges Europe currently faces.
  
  According to official EU data, the EUCRA report was published by the European Environment Agency (EEA) in 2024 and is the first comprehensive report to assess Europe's current and future major climate risks. The report identifies 36 climate risks covering multiple dimensions including energy and food security, ecosystems, infrastructure, water resources, financial systems, and public health. The European Commission also simultaneously released the "State of the Energy Union Report 2025," focusing on the progress of EU Energy Union construction and the implementation of 2030 targets.
  
  Xinhua News Agency reported that the EU Commission's "EU Global Climate and Energy Vision" proposes to enhance the EU's clean technology manufacturing capacity and promote the transition to clean energy.
  
  However, there are some details from different sources that require further clarification. Xinhua News Agency reported that the EU released strategic documents on October 16, 2025, while another source indicates that the European Commission released the "EU Strategy for a Clean Resilient Global Transition" and the "State of the Energy Union Report 2025" in October and November 2025, respectively. The two sources differ slightly in their time expressions—the former focuses on October 16, while the latter covers the period from October to November—and the specific release timeline remains to be confirmed.
  
  In addition, regarding the publication date of the EUCRA report, EU official sources indicate it was published in 2024, while the related strategic documents were published in 2025, with a time span of approximately one year, reflecting the continuity and phased nature of the EU's climate risk assessment and policy-making.
  
  Extreme climate events have already caused concrete impacts across multiple European countries. According to relevant reports, extreme heat and drought have swept across Europe, with Danube River water levels reaching historic lows, severely threatening the operation of a Hungarian nuclear power plant. Local authorities took emergency action by sinking two 80-meter-long giant barges into the river in an attempt to raise water levels, but the water level at the nuclear plant rose by only one centimeter, demonstrating that the threat of climate change to energy infrastructure is both concrete and urgent. Meanwhile, experts have warned that insufficient EU grid capacity could become a bottleneck for AI industry development.
  
  Davies, co-director of the Belgian think tank Itinera, pointed out that the EU's Green Deal means continued investment in infrastructure and grids, but the problem of insufficient grid capacity has not been adequately addressed, which could make it difficult for the EU to compete with the United States and China in the AI race.
  
  Different perspectives reveal that the EU faces multiple challenges on its path toward climate and energy transition. On one hand, the EU is actively promoting the transition to clean energy, enhancing clean technology manufacturing capacity, and systematically identifying climate risks through the EUCRA report, demonstrating forward-looking policy. On the other hand, insufficient grid capacity, the direct threat of extreme climate to infrastructure, and the rapid growth of energy demand from the AI industry all constitute real-world challenges. Experts caution against over-interpreting any single policy document as a comprehensive solution, noting that the EU still needs to overcome multiple obstacles—including funding, timelines, and cross-border coordination—in implementing the enhancement of clean technology manufacturing capacity and grid expansion.
  
  Furthermore, among the 36 risks identified in the EUCRA report, some have reached critical levels, but the report does not provide detailed explanations of the specific impact magnitude and timeframes for each risk, details that remain to be further confirmed.
  
  Key areas for future observation include: the specific implementation pathways and budget allocation for the EU's "Global Climate and Energy Vision"; whether the EUCRA report will be followed by corresponding adaptation policies; long-term response plans for critical infrastructure such as the Hungarian nuclear power plant; and whether the EU's grid expansion plans can keep pace with the growing energy demands of the AI industry. Meanwhile, the specific targets and indicators of the strategic documents and status reports released between October and November 2025 are also worth continued tracking, to assess the EU's progress toward its 2030 climate and energy goals.

# 歐盟發布氣候與能源戰略 多國面臨極端氣候風險

> 歐盟委員會於2025年10月發布全球氣候與能源戰略，同時首份歐洲氣候風險評估報告指出36項氣候威脅已達關鍵水準，專家警告電網容量不足恐影響AI產業發展。

歐盟委員會於2025年10月16日發布《歐盟全球氣候與能源願景》，提出一系列氣候與能源領域的行動計劃，旨在推動向清潔能源轉型並增強產業國際競爭力。同日，首份《歐洲氣候風險評估》（European Climate Risk Assessment，EUCRA）報告指出，歐洲正面臨36項氣候風險，其中多項已達關鍵水準，嚴重威脅能源與糧食安全、生態系統、基礎設施、水資源、金融體系及民眾健康。
這兩項重要檔案的發布，標誌著歐盟在氣候與能源政策上邁出關鍵一步，也凸顯歐洲當前所面臨的嚴峻氣候挑戰。
根據歐盟官方資料，EUCRA報告由歐洲環境署（EEA）於2024年發布，是首份全面評估歐洲當前與未來主要氣候風險的綜合性報告。報告識別出36項氣候風險，涵蓋能源與糧食安全、生態系統、基礎設施、水資源、金融體系及公共健康等多個層面。歐盟委員會亦同步發布《能源聯盟現狀報告2025》（State of the Energy Union Report 2025），聚焦歐盟能源聯盟建設進展及2030年目標實施情況。
新華社報導指出，歐盟委員會發布的《歐盟全球氣候與能源願景》提出將提升歐盟清潔技術製造能力，推動向清潔能源轉型。
然而，不同來源之間存在一些需要進一步釐清的細節。新華社報導提及歐盟於2025年10月16日發布戰略檔案，而另一份資料則指出歐盟委員會於2025年10月與11月先後發布《歐盟塑造全球清潔韌性轉型新戰略》及《能源聯盟現狀報告2025》。兩份資料在時間表述上略有出入，前者聚焦10月16日，後者涵蓋10月至11月的時間範圍，尚待進一步確認具體發布時間序列。
此外，關於EUCRA報告的發布時間，歐盟官方資料標註為2024年發布，而相關戰略檔案則於2025年發布，兩者時間跨度約一年，反映歐盟在氣候風險評估與政策制定上的延續性與階段性。
極端氣候事件已對歐洲多國造成具體影響。根據相關報導，極端高溫與乾旱席捲歐洲，多瑙河水位創下歷史新低，嚴重威脅匈牙利核電廠的營運。當地政府採取緊急行動，將兩艘80公尺長的巨型沉船沉入河中試圖提升水位，但核電廠水位僅上升一公分，顯示氣候變遷對能源基礎設施的威脅已相當具體且急迫。同時，專家警告歐盟電網容量不足可能成為AI產業發展的瓶頸。
比利時智庫Itinera共同執行長戴弗斯指出，歐盟的綠色新政意味著持續投資基礎設施和電網，但電網容量不足的問題尚未獲得充分解決，這可能使歐盟在AI競賽中難以與美國和中國抗衡。
不同觀點顯示，歐盟在氣候與能源轉型路上面臨多重挑戰。一方面，歐盟積極推動清潔能源轉型，提升清潔技術製造能力，並透過EUCRA報告系統性識別氣候風險，展現政策上的前瞻性。另一方面，電網容量不足、極端氣候對基礎設施的直接威脅，以及AI產業對能源需求的快速成長，均構成現實挑戰。專家提醒，不應過度解讀單一政策檔案為全面解決方案，歐盟在落實清潔技術製造能力提升與電網擴建方面，仍需克服資金、時程與跨國協調等多重障礙。
此外，EUCRA報告所識別的36項風險中，部分風險已達關鍵水準，但報告未詳細說明各項風險的具體影響程度與時間框架，這些細節尚待進一步確認。
後續觀察重點包括：歐盟《全球氣候與能源願景》的具體實施路徑與預算編列、EUCRA報告後續是否有配套適應政策、匈牙利核電廠等關鍵基礎設施的長期應對方案，以及歐盟電網擴建計畫能否跟上AI產業的能源需求成長。同時，2025年10月至11月發布的戰略檔案與現狀報告，其具體目標與指標也值得持續追蹤，以評估歐盟在2030年氣候與能源目標上的進展。
