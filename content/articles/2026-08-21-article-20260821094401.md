  # SCO Summit Held in Tianjin; Xi and Putin Jointly Condemn Western Bullying
  
  > On September 1, 2025, the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit was held in Tianjin, China, where Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin jointly criticized Western nations. Xi condemned the "bullying behavior" of certain countries, while Putin blamed the West for the Ukraine conflict. More than 20 Eurasian leaders attended the summit.
  
  On September 1, 2025, the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) summit was held in Tianjin, China, marking the largest gathering in the organisation's history, with more than 20 Eurasian leaders in attendance. Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin jointly criticized Western nations during the summit, drawing close international attention. Founded in 2001, the SCO includes member states such as China, Russia, India, and Pakistan, with the aim of promoting regional security and economic cooperation. It is regarded as an alternative to traditional Western alliance systems.
  
  According to reports from Ahram Online, EWN (South African electronic news network), and RTL Today (Luxembourg), Xi condemned the "bullying behavior" of certain countries at the summit without directly naming any specific nation. Putin, meanwhile, explicitly blamed Western nations—not Russia—for the Ukraine conflict, emphasizing that it has lasted three and a half years and that the West triggered it. All three outlets confirmed that Xi's and Putin's remarks echoed each other during the summit, jointly expressing dissatisfaction with Western policies.
  
  The three media outlets were consistent on core facts but differed slightly in their detailed phrasing. Ahram Online and EWN both used expressions such as "swipe at the West" or "round on the West," underscoring the leaders' critical stance toward the West; RTL Today further noted that this was the SCO's largest meeting since its founding, highlighting the organisation's growing geopolitical influence. Additionally, EWN specifically described the SCO as an "alternative to traditional alliances," a point not explicitly mentioned in the summaries of the other two outlets, representing a more distinctive narrative angle.
  
  Regarding the specific timeline of the Ukraine conflict, all three media outlets cited the figure of "three and a half years" but did not provide the exact date the conflict began; this information remains to be further verified.
  
  The geopolitical implications of this summit are noteworthy. As the host city, Tianjin once again underscores China's central role in Eurasian diplomatic affairs. For neighboring countries, the SCO's expansion and strengthening may affect regional security architectures, particularly serving as a counterbalance to NATO and EU eastern enlargement. Through close cooperation with China, Russia seeks to alleviate the international isolation it faces due to the Ukraine war. For Western nations, the signal sent by this summit is that major Eurasian powers are attempting to build a cooperative framework independent of the Western-dominated order.
  
  Observers differ in their interpretations of the summit. Some analysts argue that Xi's and Putin's criticisms are diplomatic rhetoric aimed at consolidating consensus within the SCO rather than posing a direct challenge to any specific country. Others point out that although the SCO emphasizes multipolarity in its rhetoric, differences in interests among member states—such as the India-China border dispute—may limit the organisation's practical capacity for action. Furthermore, there is currently no evidence that specific sanctions measures or joint military operations were agreed upon during the summit, and overinterpreting these criticisms could lead to misjudgments about the regional situation.
  
  Future observations will focus on whether the SCO will put forward concrete proposals in areas such as economic cooperation, security mechanisms, or energy policy, and how Western nations will respond to the diplomatic signals sent by this summit. Additionally, the subsequent developments of the Ukraine conflict, the evolution of China-Russia relations, and the positions of member states such as India within the SCO framework will all influence the organisation's future direction. The international community will also watch whether China will further expand its diplomatic influence in Eurasia through the SCO.

# 上合組織峰會在天津舉行 習普金共同批評西方霸凌行為

> 2025年9月1日，上海合作組織峰會在中國天津舉行，中國國家主席習近平與俄羅斯總統普京共同批評西方國家。習近平譴責某些國家的「霸凌行為」，普京則指責西方為烏克蘭衝突的肇因，超過20位歐亞領導人與會。

2025年9月1日，上海合作組織（Shanghai Cooperation Organisation，簡稱SCO）峰會在中國天津市舉行，這是該組織成立以來規模最大的一次峰會，超過20位歐亞國家領導人出席。中國國家主席習近平與俄羅斯總統普京在峰會期間共同對西方國家提出批評，引發國際社會高度關注。上海合作組織成立於2001年，成員國包括中國、俄羅斯、印度、巴基斯坦等國，宗旨是促進地區安全與經濟合作，並被視為對傳統西方聯盟體系的替代選擇。
根據埃及金字塔新聞網（Ahram Online）、南非電子新聞新聞網（EWN）以及盧森堡RTL今日新聞網（RTL Today）等媒體報導，習近平在峰會上譴責某些國家的「霸凌行為」（bullying behaviour），而未直接點名任何特定國家。普京則在發言中明確指責西方國家，而非俄羅斯，應對烏克蘭衝突負責。他強調該衝突已持續三年半，並認為西方是衝突的觸發者。三家媒體均確認，習與普京的言論在峰會期間形成呼應，共同表達對西方政策的不滿。
三家媒體在核心事實上的報導一致，但在細節表述上存在些微差異。Ahram Online與EWN均使用「swipe at the West」或「round on West」等表述，強調領導人對西方的批評態度；RTL Today則進一步指出，此次峰會是SCO自成立以來最大規模的會議，突顯該組織的地緣政治影響力正在擴大。此外，EWN特別提到SCO被定位為「傳統聯盟的替代方案」，此一點在另外兩家媒體的摘要中未明確提及，屬於較獨特的敘述角度。
關於烏克蘭衝突的具體時間表述，三家媒體均引用「三年半」的說法，但未提供衝突爆發的具體日期，此部分資訊尚待進一步查證。
此次峰會的地緣政治影響值得關注。天津作為主辦城市，再次彰顯中國在歐亞大陸外交事務中的核心角色。對於周邊國家而言，SCO的擴張與強化可能影響區域安全架構，特別是對北約與歐盟的東擴政策形成制衡。俄羅斯透過與中國的緊密合作，試圖緩解因烏克蘭戰爭而面臨的國際孤立局面。對西方國家而言，此次峰會釋放出的訊號是，歐亞大國正試圖建立一套獨立於西方主導秩序之外的合作框架。
不同觀察家對此次峰會的解讀存在分歧。部分分析認為，習與普京的批評屬於外交辭令，旨在凝聚SCO內部共識，而非針對特定國家的直接挑戰。另有觀點指出，SCO雖在口頭上強調多極化，但成員國之間的利益差異（如印度與中國邊境爭議）可能限制該組織的實際行動能力。此外，目前尚無證據顯示峰會期間就具體制裁措施或聯合軍事行動達成協議，過度解讀此次批評可能導致對區域局勢的誤判。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於SCO是否會在經濟合作、安全機制或能源政策等方面提出具體倡議，以及西方國家將如何回應此次峰會釋放的外交訊號。此外，烏克蘭衝突的後續發展、中俄關係的演變，以及印度等成員國在SCO框架內的立場，都將影響該組織未來的走向。國際社會亦將關注中國是否會透過SCO進一步擴大其在歐亞大陸的外交影響力。
