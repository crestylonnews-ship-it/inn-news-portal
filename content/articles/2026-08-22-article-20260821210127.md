  # El Niño Drought Intensifies; Panama Canal to Restrict Ship Transits Starting September
  
  > The Panama Canal Authority announced that due to the El Niño phenomenon causing rainfall in the watershed to be 34% below the historical average, daily ship transits will be reduced from 36 to 34 starting September 3, and further to 32 by September 15, affecting approximately 5% of global maritime trade routes.
  
  As a critical waterway connecting the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans, the Panama Canal handles approximately 5% of global maritime trade volume, and its operational status directly impacts international supply chains and energy transportation networks. According to multiple public reports, the canal's watershed has experienced significantly below-average rainfall in recent periods due to the El Niño climate phenomenon, with precipitation levels approximately 34% lower than historical averages for the same period. This persistent drought has caused water levels in the canal to drop, and freshwater reserves are insufficient to maintain the original frequency of ship transits, forcing the Panama Canal Authority to make the decision to restrict transit volumes.
  
  This measure aims to alleviate water resource pressure by controlling the number of daily transiting ships, ensuring the canal system can continue to operate safely under low-water conditions while avoiding further exacerbation of the risk of water depletion.
  
  According to reports from Tempo, BSS News, and Anadolu Agency (AA), the Panama Canal Authority has officially announced the ship transit control schedule. Most sources confirm that starting September 3, the number of ships allowed to pass daily will be reduced from the original 36 to 34, and subsequently further reduced to 32 per day by September 15. This decision is based on objective data showing that rainfall in the canal watershed remains 34% below the historical average, and the El Niño phenomenon may bring more severe drought threats.
  
  All three media outlets indicated that this adjustment is a preventive measure designed to address the substantial impact of water shortages on canal operations, and emphasized that global trade relies heavily on the proportion of cargo transported through this waterway, meaning any transit restrictions could produce cascading effects.
  
  However, different media outlets show slight discrepancies regarding the specific implementation date. Tempo and BSS News both record that the control measures take effect starting September 3, while Anadolu Agency states the start date is September 4. This date discrepancy may stem from differences in interpretation between the announcement release time and the actual execution schedule, or result from time zone conversions during media translation. Currently, no official documents clearly clarify this date divergence, and no other details such as ship priority, fee adjustments, or exemption clauses have been seen. This difference serves as a reminder to readers and industry professionals to carefully verify when citing a single source and to await further clarification from the Canal Authority.
  
  The reduction in Panama Canal transits will directly impact the global trade network that relies on this route, particularly energy and bulk commodity transportation. The waterway handles approximately 5% of global maritime trade, and many cargo ships transport liquefied natural gas, petroleum, agricultural products, and manufacturing components through it. The reduction from 36 ships per day to 32 means that ship waiting times may be extended, port turnover efficiency will decline, and transportation costs and delivery delay risks will consequently rise. Central American regions, Caribbean coastal countries, and routes from the Far East to the U.S. East Coast will be hit first.
  
  Furthermore, the Canal Authority has previously implemented similar water restriction measures, but this time, with a rainfall deficit of 34%, it is considered one of the more severe drought situations in recent years. The industry expects that if El Niño continues to intensify, control measures may be further tightened.
  
  Different perspectives note that this decision reflects the ongoing pressure that climate change places on critical infrastructure, but some analysts believe the impact could be mitigated in the short term through scheduling optimization. The Canal Authority emphasized that the transit restriction is a scientific decision based on hydrological monitoring data, aimed at balancing operational needs with environmental carrying capacity. However, some shipping companies are concerned that if the drought persists into next year, it may trigger broader supply chain restructuring, prompting cargo owners to seek alternative routes such as rounding Cape Horn or increasing cross-Isthmus land transport. On the other hand, some experts have warned against overinterpreting a single seasonal measure as a long-term trend, because the El Niño phenomenon is cyclical, and rainfall is expected to gradually recover as climate patterns shift.
  
  Currently, there is no evidence that the canal will permanently change its transit capacity; this measure remains emergency in nature.
  
  Subsequent observation will focus on canal water level changes, updates to rainfall prediction models, and whether the Authority issues further control announcements. The industry will closely monitor actual transit conditions after mid-September and assess fluctuations in ship port waiting times and freight indices. Additionally, international meteorological agencies' predictions on El Niño intensity and duration will be key indicators for determining whether the drought will ease. If watershed rainfall gradually recovers to historical average levels, the Canal Authority may relax transit restrictions; conversely, if the drought intensifies, the implementation of stricter water level quotas or priority transit mechanisms cannot be ruled out. This incident also highlights the global supply chain's heavy reliance on a single waterway, and in the long term may prompt the logistics industry to accelerate the diversification of route risks.

# 厄爾尼諾旱情加劇 巴拿馬運河9月起限縮船舶通行量

> 巴拿馬運河管理局宣佈，因厄爾尼諾現象導致流域降雨量較歷史平均低34%，將自9月3日起每日船舶通行量從36艘降至34艘，9月15日進一步降至32艘，此舉將影響全球約5%的海運貿易航線。

巴拿馬運河作為連線大西洋與太平洋的關鍵水道，承載著全球約5%的海運貿易量，其運作狀態直接牽動國際供應鏈與能源運輸格局。根據多項公開報導指出，受厄爾尼諾（El Niño）氣候現象影響，運河流域近期降雨量顯著偏低，已低於歷史同期平均約34%。此種持續性乾旱導致運河水位下降，淡水儲備不足以維持原有船舶通行頻率，迫使巴拿馬運河管理局（Panama Canal Authority）做出限縮通行量的決策。
該措施旨在透過控制每日過境船舶數量，緩解水資源壓力，確保運河系統在低水位條件下仍能安全運作，同時避免進一步加劇水域枯竭風險。
綜合 Tempo、BSS News 與安納多盧通訊社（AA）等媒體報導，巴拿馬運河管理局已正式公告船舶通行管制時程。多數來源確認，自9月3日起，每日允許通行的船舶數量將由原本的36艘減少至34艘；隨後於9月15日進一步降至每日32艘。此決定基於運河流域降雨量持續低於歷史平均34%的客觀資料，且厄爾尼諾現象可能帶來更嚴重的乾旱威脅。
三家媒體均指出，此項調整屬於預防性措施，旨在因應水資源短缺對運河營運造成的實質影響，並強調全球貿易依賴該水道承載的貨運比例，任何通行限制都可能產生連鎖效應。
然而，不同媒體在具體實施日期上存在細微差異。Tempo 與 BSS News 均記載管制措施自9月3日開始生效，而安納多盧通訊社則指出起始日期為9月4日。此種日期落差可能源於公告發布時間與實際執行時程的解釋差異，或為媒體轉譯時的時區換算所致。目前尚無官方檔案明確釐清該日期分歧，亦未見其他細節如船舶優先順序、收費調整或豁免條款等資訊。此差異提醒讀者與業界，在引用單一來源時應謹慎核對，並等待運河管理局進一步澄清。
巴拿馬運河通行量減少將直接衝擊依賴該航線的全球貿易網路，特別是能源與大宗商品運輸。該水道處理全球約5%的海運貿易，許多貨輪需經由此處運送液化天然氣、石油、農產品與製造業零元件。通行量從每日36艘降至32艘，意味著船舶滯留時間可能延長，港口週轉效率下降，進而推升運輸成本與交貨延遲風險。中美洲地區與加勒比海沿岸國家，以及遠東至美國東岸的航線將首當其衝。
此外，運河管理局過去已實施過類似限水措施，但此次因降雨 deficit 幅度達34%，被視為近年較嚴峻的乾旱情境，業界預期若厄爾尼諾持續強化，管制措施可能進一步收緊。
不同觀點指出，此項決策反映氣候變遷對關鍵基礎設施的持續壓力，但亦有分析認為短期內或可透過排程最佳化緩解衝擊。運河管理局強調，限制通行量是基於水文監測資料的科學決策，旨在平衡營運需求與環境承載力。然而，部分航運業者擔心，若乾旱延續至明年，可能引發更廣泛的供應鏈重組，促使貨主尋求替代路線，如繞道好望角或增加跨地峽陸運。另一方面，亦有專家提醒，不應過度解讀單一季節性措施為長期趨勢，因為厄爾尼諾現象具有週期性，隨著氣候模式轉變，降雨量有望逐步恢復。
目前尚無證據顯示運河將永久改變通行容量，此舉仍屬應急性質。
後續觀察重點將聚焦於運河水位變化、降雨預測模型更新，以及管理局是否發布進一步管制公告。業界將密切關注9月中旬後的實際通行狀況，並評估船舶滯港時間與運費指數的波動。此外，國際氣象機構對厄爾尼諾強度與持續時間的預測，將是判斷乾旱是否緩和的關鍵指標。若流域降雨量逐步回升至歷史平均範圍，運河管理局可能放寬通行限制；反之，若乾旱加劇，則不排除實施更嚴格的水位配額或優先通行機制。此事件亦凸顯全球供應鏈對單一水道的高度依賴，長期而言或促使物流業加速分散航線風險。
