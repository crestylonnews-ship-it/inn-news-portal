  # Guatemala Announces Withdrawal from Central American Court, Regional Judicial Body Effectively Dissolved
  
  > On August 21, the Guatemalan government announced via Agreement No. 115-2026 that it can no longer fulfill its obligations under the Statute of the Central American Court of Justice, marking the effective cessation of operations of the regional judicial body following the prior withdrawals of Nicaragua, Honduras, and El Salvador.
  
  The Central American Court of Justice (Corte Centroamericana de Justicia, abbreviated CCJ) is a key regional judicial institution within the framework of the Central American Integration System (SICA), responsible for adjudicating legal disputes between member states and ensuring the application and interpretation of regional integration law. Since its official establishment in 1994, the Court has long played a pivotal role in human rights protection and trade dispute resolution across the Central American region.
  
  However, in recent years the region's political situation has remained increasingly unstable, with Nicaragua, Honduras, and El Salvador successively announcing their withdrawal from the Statute of the Central American Court of Justice, severely undermining the Court's operational foundation. As the last remaining member state participating in the Court, Guatemala's government formally announced its withdrawal on August 21, meaning the regional judicial body now has no member states continuing to participate, effectively bringing it to an end.
  
  According to cross-media verification, the Guatemalan Ministry of Foreign Affairs formally announced via Government Agreement No. 115-2026 that, due to the successive withdrawals of Nicaragua, Honduras, and El Salvador from the Statute, Guatemala can no longer continue to fulfill its obligations under the Court, and therefore declared it "unable to comply" with the Statute. La Hora reported that the Guatemalan government, through this agreement, explicitly announced that the Court would cease operations. Infobae and Emisoras Unidas also confirmed that Guatemala was the last member state to withdraw from the Court following the aforementioned three countries, and its withdrawal resulted in the effective dissolution of the regional judicial body.
  
  The facts confirmed by all four media outlets include: the Guatemalan government used Agreement No. 115-2026 as its legal basis; Guatemala declared it "unable to comply" with the Statute; Nicaragua, Honduras, and El Salvador had already withdrawn beforehand; and the Court has therefore effectively ceased operations.
  
  Although the core narratives across media outlets are consistent, there remain certain differences in their detailed descriptions. Prensa Libre characterized the event as the "extinction" of the tribunal, emphasizing that the Court's legal personality disappeared once the treaty was terminated; La Hora used the term "disappear" to suggest the institution would no longer exist. Infobae, meanwhile, focused on the procedural significance of Guatemala's "formal withdrawal," noting that it rendered the regional organization "ineffective."
  
  Furthermore, regarding the specific date of Guatemala's withdrawal announcement, the media did not provide a clear timestamp; only the publication dates of La Hora and Infobae point to August 21, though it remains to be confirmed whether this date corresponds to the issuance of the Guatemalan government's agreement or merely the media reports. Emisoras Unidas also did not provide specific date information.
  
  This event will have profound implications for the legal and political architecture of Central America. The Central American Court of Justice has long been responsible for adjudicating trade, investment, and human rights disputes between member states; its dissolution means the region will lack a supranational judicial mechanism. Analysts note that cross-border legal disputes among Central American countries in the future will lose an important arbitration platform, potentially forcing nations to resolve disputes through bilateral negotiations or international arbitration, thereby increasing costs and uncertainty. Additionally, the Court had previously handled multiple cases involving refugee rights, labor rights, and environmental protection, and its dissolution may negatively impact regional human rights protection mechanisms.
  
  Guatemalan domestic political observers have also pointed out that this move reflects a serious setback in the Central American regional integration process.
  
  Different media and analysts have approached this event from varying perspectives. Prensa Libre emphasized the impossibility of fulfilling treaty obligations from a legal standpoint, viewing this as a passive choice by the Guatemalan government; Infobae described it as an "active formal withdrawal" by the Guatemalan government, implying political intent. Some regional political analysts believe Guatemala's move is an inevitable result of順應 regional political trends rather than an independent political decision; others suggest the Guatemalan government may be using this to demonstrate its sovereignty stance to domestic political forces.
  
  However, there is currently no official statement detailing the complete internal assessment process behind the Guatemalan government's decision, nor is there evidence that the government attempted to negotiate with other member states to salvage the Court. Therefore, all parties should avoid overinterpreting the motivations and subsequent impacts of this move.
  
  Key areas for future observation include: whether the Central American Integration System (SICA) will activate mechanisms to rebuild the regional judicial framework; whether the Guatemalan government will propose alternative regional legal cooperation plans; whether other Central American countries will rejoin the Court or participate in a new regional judicial mechanism after the withdrawals of Nicaragua, Honduras, and El Salvador; and how the multiple pending cases already filed with the Court will be handled. Additionally, the responses from international human rights organizations and regional monitoring bodies will serve as important indicators for assessing the event's impact on regional governance.

# 瓜地馬拉宣佈退出中美洲法院 區域司法機構實質解散

> 瓜地馬拉政府於8月21日以第115-2026號協議宣佈無法繼續履行《中美洲法院司法規章》義務，此舉標誌著該區域司法機構因尼加拉瓜、宏都拉斯與薩爾瓦多相繼退出後實質停止運作。

中美洲法院司法（Corte Centroamericana de Justicia，簡稱CCJ）是中美洲一體化體系（SICA）框架下的重要區域司法機構，負責裁決成員國之間的法律爭議，並保障區域一體化法的適用與解釋。該法院自1994年正式成立以來，長期扮演中美洲地區人權保障與貿易糾紛裁決的關鍵角色。
然而，近年來該區域政治情勢持續動盪，尼加拉瓜、宏都拉斯與薩爾瓦多相繼宣佈退出《中美洲法院司法規章》（Estatuto de la CCJ），導致該法院的運作基礎遭到嚴重削弱。瓜地馬拉作為最後一個仍參與該法院的成員國，其政府於8月21日正式宣佈退出，意味著該區域司法機構已無任何成員國繼續參與，實質上走向終結。
根據多媒體交叉比對，瓜地馬拉外交部透過第115-2026號政府協議（Acuerdo 115-2026）正式宣佈，因尼加拉瓜、宏都拉斯與薩爾瓦多相繼退出《規章》，瓜地馬拉已無法繼續履行該法院的義務，故宣佈「無法遵守」該規章。《La Hora》報導指出，瓜地馬拉政府透過該協議明確宣佈法院將停止運作。《Infobae》與《Emisoras Unidas》亦確認，瓜地馬拉是繼上述三國之後最後一個退出該法院的成員國，其退出使該區域司法機構實質解散。
四家媒體共同確認的事實包括：瓜地馬拉政府以第115-2026號協議為法律依據；瓜地馬拉宣佈「無法遵守」《規章》；尼加拉瓜、宏都拉斯與薩爾瓦多已先退出；法院因此實質停止運作。
儘管各媒體核心敘述一致，但在細節表述上仍存在若干差異。《Prensa Libre》將此事件定性為「法院消滅」（extinción del tribunal），強調條約被廢止後法院的法律人格隨之消失；而《La Hora》則以「消失」（desaparecerá）一詞描述，暗示該機構將不再存在。《Infobae》則側重於瓜地馬拉「正式退出」的程式性意義，指出其退出使該區域組織「失去效力」。
此外，關於瓜地馬拉宣佈退出的具體日期，各媒體未標示明確時間，僅《La Hora》與《Infobae》的報導日期指向8月21日，但此日期是否為瓜地馬拉政府協議發布日或僅為媒體報導日，尚待進一步確認。《Emisoras Unidas》亦未提供具體日期資訊。
此事件對中美洲地區的法律與政治架構將產生深遠影響。中美洲法院司法長期負責裁決成員國之間的貿易、投資與人權爭議，其解散意味著區域內將缺乏一個超國家的司法裁決機制。分析指出，未來中美洲國家之間的跨境法律糾紛將失去一個重要的仲裁平臺，可能迫使各國透過雙邊談判或國際仲裁解決爭議，增加解決成本與不確定性。此外，該法院亦曾受理多起涉及難民權利、勞工權益與環境保護的案件，其解散可能對區域人權保障機制產生負面影響。
瓜地馬拉國內政治觀察家亦指出，此舉反映中美洲區域一體化程式正面臨嚴重倒退。
不同媒體與觀察家對此事件的分析角度各異。《Prensa Libre》從法律角度強調條約義務的不可履行性，將此視為瓜地馬拉政府的被動選擇；《Infobae》則將此描述為瓜地馬拉政府的「主動正式退出」，暗示其政治意圖。部分割槽域政治分析師認為，瓜地馬拉此舉是順應區域政治趨勢的必然結果，而非單獨的政治決策；亦有觀點認為，瓜地馬拉政府可能藉此向國內政治勢力展示其主權立場。
然而，目前尚無任何官方宣告詳細說明瓜地馬拉政府做出此決策的完整內部評估過程，亦無證據顯示瓜地馬拉政府曾嘗試與其他成員國協商挽救該法院。因此，對於此舉的動機與後續影響，各界應避免過度解讀。
後續觀察重點包括：中美洲一體化體系（SICA）是否會啟動機制以重建區域司法架構；瓜地馬拉政府是否會提出替代性的區域法律合作方案；尼加拉瓜、宏都拉斯與薩爾瓦多退出後，是否會有其他中美洲國家重新加入該法院或參與新的區域司法機制；以及該法院已受理但未結案的多起案件將如何處理。此外，國際人權組織與區域觀察機構對此事的反應，亦將是衡量此事件對區域治理影響的重要指標。
